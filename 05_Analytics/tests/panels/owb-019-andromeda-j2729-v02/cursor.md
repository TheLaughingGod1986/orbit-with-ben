## 1. Title — 6/10

**Current:** Milky Way Andromeda Collision (What Happens to Our Sun?) — 56 characters.

The string does contain the phrase someone would type, and the film can pay the parenthesis off: the Sun is not ploughed into by another star, and the script's later beat is a Sun that keeps its planets. It is short of the 60-character cap.

It still fails as a click title. The first four words are a catalogue label, Milky Way Andromeda Collision. Sun is the ninth word, inside parentheses. On a short browse row the line cuts around "Milky Way Andromeda Collision (What Hap", so the only personal stake is the part that disappears. Two ideas are stacked: a topic label, then a second question.

**Rewrite:** Our Sun in the Milky Way Andromeda Collision? (45 characters). Sun is the second word, the typed phrase stays intact, and a short row still shows the stake.

## 2. Thumbnail at 168×94 — 8/10

**Cover:** STARS RARELY HIT. RARELY is yellow. STARS and HIT are white. Three words, top band, heavy type, dark outline, clear of the galaxy.

At full size and on the real 168×94 tile the whole disk reads: bright core, both ends of the spiral, dark navy around it. The phone tile's bottom-right corner is empty sky. The hook sits at the top, so the duration badge does not cover a word or the core. The small companion sits lower-right of the disk and stays out of that corner. The NASA/JPL-Caltech credit is small, bottom-left.

The picture is one quiet galaxy. The surprise is entirely in the type. That is enough to hold 8, and it is the right fact for this film. No thumbnail rewrite.

## 3. Title + thumbnail together — 7/10

The words do not repeat. Collision is only in the title. STARS RARELY HIT is only on the cover. That split is right: the title should carry the event, the cover should carry the contradiction.

What a phone actually shows is the weak pair. The visible title is the collision label, and the cover says HIT. The viewer gets smash twice, and loses Our Sun, which is the reason to care. The cover cannot fix a title that hides its stake in a parenthesis.

**Rewrite:** keep this exact STARS RARELY HIT file, and pair it with Our Sun in the Milky Way Andromeda Collision?

## 4. First 30 seconds — 5/10

The spoken open is "Look up on a dark night and you can already see it," then a quiet smudge, two and a half million light-years, and a neighbour that is closing in. "Not as a crash" is the only early sharpening.

The new title's promise is the Sun. The cover's promise is that stars rarely hit. Neither is said in the opening. Star-on-star crashes are called rare at the "Stars Almost Never Hit" chapter (description timestamp 5:44). The Sun's fate lands in "Would We Survive?" (7:11): probably not a star ploughing into the Sun, and a Sun that can keep its planets. That is a real payoff. It is late for a title that leads with the Sun.

**Rewrite, one spoken line, no file changed here:** "Andromeda is closing on the Milky Way, and our Sun is not the star that gets hit."

## 5. Description first line and chapters — 6/10

**First line:** "The Milky Way Andromeda collision: in about 4 billion years, the Andromeda galaxy fills our sky — and the galaxies don't smash into fireworks."

The search phrase is in the first line. The chapters are in order and name real sections, from "Andromeda is already coming" through "What Else Is Coming."

The first line spends the cover's whole twist ("don't smash into fireworks" is STARS RARELY HIT, said before play). It also states "about 4 billion years" as the clock. The script treats four to five billion years as the old clean story, and says the merger date is still being sharpened. The chapters can keep their names. The first line should not give the answer or the old date.

**Rewrite:** "The Milky Way Andromeda collision is already inbound. Here is what that meeting does to our Sun and the night sky."
