### Does BABY GALAXIES fit the film and the image?

Partly, but it is the weaker shorthand. Scene 04 concerns multiple galaxies, so plural wording fits the film. However, its roughly 300-million-year number is the universe’s age when the light was emitted, not a measured stellar age or a galaxy formation date. The scene immediately contrasts that young universe with apparently grown-up galaxies. “Baby” loses that contrast and invites the wrong age interpretation.

The loupe shows one selected distant galaxy beside another component; the plural must not make those two components look like two identified early galaxies. NASA’s [JADES pullout](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/jades-gs-z14-0-pullout-nircam/) explicitly points to the redder portion and places the observation less than 300 million years after the Big Bang. Its source identification supports the chosen region, but the candidate needs to preserve that target distinction visually. The origin-to-loupe connection is understandable; I have not independently reproduced its pixel registration. The declared 440/64 = 6.875× resize is resampling, not newly resolved structure. At phone size the softness is visible; more enlargement or synthetic sharpening would not repair the identification problem.
