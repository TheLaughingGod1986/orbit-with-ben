## Part 1: Explicit Answers to the Design Review Brief Questions

1. **Does Earth read as a foreground reference, or misleadingly as a literal size/distance diagram?**
   - **It reads as a misleading size/distance diagram on all three drafts.** Because the identical full-daylight Apollo 17 Earth disc (`as17-148-22727`) is pasted as a cutout sphere onto a flat background beside each primary object, the eye reads the two spheres on the same plane:
     - On `REXYxuLOBoI_B_v01.jpg` (*The Last Star*), it implies **Earth survives to the end of stellar time (~10 trillion years)** and orbits or sits beside the last red dwarf, while depicting the red dwarf at ~2.8× Earth's diameter (wrong for both a red dwarf at ~11–50× Earth's diameter and an Earth-sized white/black dwarf remnant).
     - On `ojk-dfOpAmw_B_v01.jpg` (*Andromeda*), a daylight Earth globe floating in intergalactic space at roughly the size of M31's galactic bulge looks like a literal comparison rather than a view from Earth.
     - On `m09jUdwC5hk_B_v01.jpg` (*Compilation*), Earth sits right under Saturn's ring plane like an icy moon of Saturn.
2. **Is `STARS MISS STARS` clear enough without an absolute scientific claim?**
   - **Yes for a 3-word thumbnail hook, provided the title stays broad, though the phrasing is clunky.** A 2–3 word cover cannot carry hedging adverbs like "USUALLY". Paired with the live title (*What Happens During the Milky Way Andromeda Collision?*), `STARS MISS STARS` creates a strong counter-intuitive contradiction without repeating the title. However, repeating `STARS` twice in three words reads awkwardly; **`STARS DON'T CRASH`** or **`ZERO STAR CRASHES`** is cleaner and matches the proven channel phrasing (*Why Stars Don't Crash When Galaxies Meet*, 249 views, 3.6% like rate).
3. **Does `SATURN TO STARLIGHT` communicate the compilation's range and countdown, or look like another Saturn-only episode? What about the Earth reference at phone size?**
   - **It looks like a Saturn-only episode.** Saturn occupies ~80% of the canvas and `SATURN` is the first word; in the feed it competes directly with Episode 021 (*How Long Do Saturn's Rings Have Left?*, `55AEQwvs36g`) rather than signaling a 48-minute deep-time countdown. `SATURN TO STARLIGHT` is poetic alliteration (`THUMBNAIL_AND_TITLE_RULES.md` §1.5) that hides the countdown hook.
   - **Earth at phone size (`168×94`) fails:** it shrinks to an unrecognizable ~8 px blue dot in the bottom-left corner.
   - **Better sourced reference:** Either (a) use Cassini's **`PIA17172`** (*The Day the Earth Smiled* — backlit Saturn rings where Earth is the actual NASA-sourced story subject, with one house white arrow pointing to Earth per §2.B.4) and change the top hook to a time-scale dare (**`10 TRILLION YEARS`** or **`EVERY COSMIC CLOCK`**), or (b) replace the tiny Earth paste-on with the Galileo Earth–Moon conjunction plate (**`PIA00342`**) or a foreground night-sky limb at ≥18% frame height so the scale reference is legible at 168×94.
4. **Does Andromeda stay a clear object rather than galaxy clutter? Is the bright active-star plate appropriate for red-dwarf longevity?**
   - **Andromeda (`ojk-dfOpAmw_B_v01.jpg`):** The spiral disc itself is recognizable, **but the plate (`PIA15416`) has a visible, unfeathered rectangular crop boundary inside the canvas.** On the left (between Earth and M31) and along the top below `STARS MISS STARS`, the dense yellow/orange foreground starfield cuts off along a straight rectangular seam against the flat `#0b1d48` navy background. That hard rectangular box looks unfinished and adds speckled star clutter around the galaxy.
   - **Red dwarf (`REXYxuLOBoI_B_v01.jpg`):** Two problems: (1) **Circular cutout artifact:** the left limb of the red star has a harsh circular mask edge trapping a bright blue-white crescent left over from the blue horizontal lens flare in Live A (`laststar_master_t243.png`). (2) **Wrong stellar mood:** white-hot surface lightning reads like an erupting flare star, not a slow-burning red dwarf sipping fuel for 10 trillion years before cooling into a cold black dwarf.
5. **Title B alternatives (search wording, factual scope, similarity to live titles):**
   - `REXYxuLOBoI` Title B (*Why Will Red Dwarfs Be the Last Stars?*): Narrows scope to the first half of the video (`0:00–4:17`), cutting out the second half (`4:17 When the Last Star Dies`, `6:47 What Remains`), and overlaps with live Short `wIh3armF7_k` (*The Last Star Will Be a Red Dwarf*).
   - `ojk-dfOpAmw` Title B (*Why Do Stars Usually Miss When Galaxies Collide?*): **Reject.** Violates `THUMBNAIL_AND_TITLE_RULES.md` §1.6 by near-duplicating two live videos (`7YFfY4MdB6c` *Why Don't Stars Smash When Galaxies Collide?* and `xQlV9G9lqLI` *Why Stars Don't Crash When Galaxies Meet*), strips out the primary search terms **Milky Way** and **Andromeda**, narrows a 9-minute collision overview to a single chapter (`5:44`), and would repeat the thumbnail words if paired with `STARS MISS STARS`.
   - `m09jUdwC5hk` Title B (*How Long Do Moons, Rings and Stars Last?*): Stronger than Live A's vague "Anything in Space", because it puts concrete nouns in the first five words, though "the Moon" (singular, familiar) beats "Moons" (plural catalogue noun).
