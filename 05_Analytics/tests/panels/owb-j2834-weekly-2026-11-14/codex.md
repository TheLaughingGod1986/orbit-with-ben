## 1. `m09jUdwC5hk`: *How Long Will Anything in Space Last?* / `ALL STARS FADE`

**Title: live 4, Title B 5.**
Nobody types "how long will anything in space last". "Anything" names no object, so there is nothing to picture and no search term. Title B swaps one vague word for another ("Everything") and bolts the answer on in brackets. "(10 Trillion Years)" is paid off (red dwarfs, Laughlin et al. 1997, in the description), but it is the answer to the last of five chapters, so it gives away the ending while still not telling me what the film is about. The film is a countdown of five named things: Saturn's rings, the Moon, the Milky Way, neutron stars, the last red dwarfs. Name them.
- **Rewrite:** `When Will Saturn's Rings, the Moon and the Last Star Die?` (57 characters). Three searchable nouns, and the question is the film's actual structure.

**Thumbnail at 168×94: 3.**
- At phone size it is a pale cyan smudge with a pink dot on a blue field. It does not read as a star, a nebula or anything dying. The live A, for all its faults, shows a recognisable object; B shows a texture.
- The Helix plate has been pushed so far (flat cyan, flat magenta core, no ring structure left) that the "real NASA photograph" advantage is gone. It looks like a filter effect.
- The arrow points at the pink blob, but nothing says what the blob is, so the arrow adds a question with no answer rather than a focal point.
- The subject is not in the film. The Helix is a Sun-like star's planetary nebula. The film's star chapters are neutron stars (from exploded giant stars) and red dwarfs. No chapter is about a Sun-like star dying. The picture promises something the film does not show.
- `ALL STARS FADE` is a flat statement the viewer already believes. No number, no danger, no gap. It also still says "stars" for a film whose first 29 minutes are rings, the Moon and a galaxy collision.
- Still too close to `REXYxuLOBoI` (*The Last Star*, `LAST LIGHT`): the stated reason for replacing A was that the two looked alike, and B is another "stars end" card.
- Badge zone is clear and the gate numbers pass. Those are not the problem.
- **Rewrite:** a real Cassini photograph of Saturn with the rings filling the frame (the backlit "In Saturn's Shadow" mosaic is the obvious plate), hook `RINGS: GONE` with `GONE` in yellow, and the arrow on the rings. Saturn is the most recognisable object in the film, it is the first chapter (0:53), and "Saturn is losing its rings" is a real, checked claim (O'Donoghue 2019) that most viewers have not heard.

**Title + thumbnail together: 4.**
"How long will anything last?" plus `ALL STARS FADE` is the same vague idea twice, and the thumbnail half-answers the title ("they fade") without making me want the number. The rewrite pair works better: the picture gives the one shocking case, the title promises the rest of the list.

**First 30 seconds: not scored.** No script or cut was supplied for this film, and `02_Video-Projects/005_The-Last-Star-In-The-Universe/01_Script/` (cited in `REVIEW.md`) does not exist in the repo. The first chapter name, "Nothing in the sky is permanent", is the right opening idea.

**Description first line and chapters: 7.**
The first line is good: Saturn's rings "raining into the planet right now" and "ten trillion years" in one sentence. It is better than the title and both thumbnails, and it is where the rewrite above comes from. Chapter names are moody rather than searchable.
- **Rewrite (chapters):** `0:53 When Saturn's rings disappear` · `10:12 The Moon is drifting away` · `18:47 Milky Way meets Andromeda` · `29:19 Neutron stars` · `39:04 The last star in the universe`.

## 2. `Mo93x0fxB1Q`: *Why Haven't We Found Aliens Yet? The Fermi Paradox Explained* / `ZERO SIGNALS`

**Title: live 6, Title B 7.**
The live title is exactly 60 characters and the search term sits at the end, where phones cut it off. Title B (47) fixes both and should be the one tested. It is still the stock phrasing every Fermi video uses, so it wins on search and does little for curiosity.
- **Rewrite (if a second title round is wanted):** `The Fermi Paradox: Why Is the Universe So Quiet?` (48). Same search term first, and "quiet" sets up `ZERO SIGNALS` without repeating it.

**Thumbnail at 168×94: 7.** The best of the three, and clearly better than its A.
- Reads at phone size: white dish bottom-left, yellow target right, two words on top. One idea, and it is the film's idea.
- `ZERO SIGNALS` is concrete where `SILENCE` was vague. Yellow on `ZERO` is the right word to colour.
- The background is the weak part. The red, magenta and blue Galactic Centre composite is busy at 168 px and reads as noise; the reticle's interior is the same texture as everything around it, so the "empty target" joke is lost when small.
- At full size there is a hard horizontal seam at about y=70 where the darker top band stops, cutting through the lettering, and the dish base ends on a slanted cut at the bottom-left corner. Both look like compositing errors.
- The dish is lit flat white with no shadow side, so it looks pasted on rather than standing under that sky.
- Badge zone clear.
- **Rewrite (keep the concept, three fixes):** feather the top band so there is no seam; darken and desaturate the sky inside the reticle by about 40% so the target is visibly empty; drop the dish 10% in brightness with a blue rim light on the sky side.

**Title + thumbnail together: 8.** They add to each other. The title asks why, the thumbnail gives the hard fact (nothing heard) and shows the instrument. No repeated words. With Title B the pair is cleaner still.

**First 30 seconds: 6.**
"Look up on a clear night and the sky feels crowded… So… where is everybody?" is the right question, and it arrives quickly. But it opens on mood, not on the thumbnail's promise: nobody says how long we have listened or that we have heard nothing until the Fermi section. Then "I'm Orbit — and today we're asking…" spends the next beat on an introduction.
- **Rewrite (first line):** "We have been listening to the sky for more than sixty years. In all that time we have heard nothing. Not one signal. So where is everybody?" (Project Ozma was 1960; check the wording against the film's sources before any re-record. This is advice for future films; I am not proposing a re-cut.)

**Description first line and chapters: 7.**
The first line carries "Fermi Paradox explained" and "why does the sky stay so quiet", which is right for search. "Are we alone in the universe?" as the opening words is the most generic phrase in the genre. Chapters are good: Great Filter and Fermi Paradox are named, and the last one ("The clue may already be here") teases without spoiling.
- **Rewrite (first line):** `The Fermi Paradox explained: the Milky Way holds hundreds of billions of stars, so why have we never heard a single signal?`

## 3. `ziKBPJ6FY0U`: *JWST Found Galaxies That Shouldn't Exist Yet* / `300 MILLION YEARS`

**Title: live 8, both Title Bs 5.**
Keep the live title. At 44 characters it leads with JWST, has a real gap ("shouldn't exist"), and the film pays it off as tension with models, not as a hoax. Both proposed Bs are worse: they are 67 and 62 characters (over the limit, and the key number is what gets cut off), and they replace the gap with the answer. "Galaxies 300 Million Years After the Big Bang" is a caption, not a reason to click. I would not run either title test.
- **Rewrite (only if a title B must be tested):** `Why Did JWST Find Galaxies That Shouldn't Exist Yet?` (52). Keeps the gap, adds the "why" search phrasing.

**Thumbnail at 168×94: 6.**
- The structure works: small circle, arrow, big loupe, orange object. Find-and-zoom is the strongest layout of the three.
- **Accuracy problem, and it should block arming.** The loupe magnifies a bright orange gravitationally lensed arc beside a cluster galaxy in the SMACS 0723 deep field. Under the words `300 MILLION YEARS`, the picture says "this is a galaxy from 300 million years after the Big Bang". It is not. The obvious arcs in that image are far closer galaxies, billions of years after the Big Bang, and the script's 300-million-year galaxies are separate, later confirmations (the script's own note points at JADES), which are faint red dots, not bright arcs. `REVIEW.md` itself describes the crop as a "crimson high-redshift galaxy and gravitational lensing arc" without giving a redshift for it. This is the same kind of fault as the 12 November lesson about ungrounded numbers and false scale: a real number attached to the wrong object.
- `REVIEW.md` says the line was "verified verbatim" against Scene 04, but the sentence it quotes is not in the script. The script says: "Some of the galaxies Webb confirmed are luminous when the universe was only about three hundred million years old." The number is paid off; the quote in the review is wrong and should be corrected.
- `300 MILLION YEARS` on its own is ambiguous at a glance: old? away? ago? It only works after reading the title. The live `TOO EARLY?` at least carried the tension.
- Three words in a 1,150 px-wide line is the smallest lettering of the three; "MILLION YEARS" is thin at 168 px.
- The deep field is busy and the plate looks over-sharpened and posterised (hard-edged orange blobs, banding in the blue).
- Loupe bottom edge and badge zone are clear.
- **Rewrite:** keep the find-and-zoom layout, but put a real, named early galaxy in the loupe (a JADES NIRCam cut-out of a confirmed z≈13–14 galaxy, which is a small red smudge, shown honestly as a smudge), and change the hook to `TOO BIG, TOO EARLY` with `TOO EARLY` in yellow. If the SMACS arc stays in the loupe, the hook cannot be a specific age; `SHOULDN'T EXIST` would then repeat the title, so the JADES crop is the better route.

**Title + thumbnail together: 7.** With the live title the pair adds up: the title says the galaxies should not exist, the thumbnail says how early. That is why the title must not change to a version containing "300 Million Years"; it would then repeat the thumbnail word for word. The score assumes the loupe is fixed.

**First 30 seconds: 6.**
"What if the universe was already… busy… when our textbooks said it should still be waking up?" is on the promise, but it is soft and abstract, and it is followed by the long working title card. The number the thumbnail sells does not arrive until Scene 04.
- **Rewrite (first line, for future films):** "Three hundred million years after the Big Bang, there should be almost nothing here. The James Webb telescope found galaxies. Bright ones."

**Description first line and chapters: not scored.** None supplied and none found in the film's `11_Upload-Package/`.
