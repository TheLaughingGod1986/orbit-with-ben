### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **The Pre-Apollo Theories & What Apollo's `381.7 kg` (`842 lb`) of Lunar Rocks Proved (`Darwin 1879`; `Wood et al. 1970`; `Smith et al. 1970`; `Greer et al. 2023`):**
  - Before the Apollo landings (`1969–1972`), three classical hypotheses competed to explain the origin of the Moon (`mass 7.346 × 10²² kg = 0.0123 M_⊕`, `mean radius 1,737.4 km = 0.2727 R_⊕`, unusually large at `1/81` of Earth's mass compared to any other major planet's moon):
    1. **Fission (`George Howard Darwin 1879`, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc.* `170`, `447–538`; `Osmond Fisher 1889`):** Proposed that a rapidly spinning molten early Earth (`period ~2.5–4 hours`) flung off a blob of mantle rock from the Pacific Ocean basin. Disproved because the present Earth-Moon system has only **`~25%` of the angular momentum** required for rotational fission, and the Pacific Ocean crust is `<200 million years` old (`plate tectonics`).
    2. **Intact Capture (`Thomas J. J. See 1909`; `Harold C. Urey 1952`):** Proposed that the Moon formed elsewhere in the Solar System and was gravitationally captured by Earth. Disproved because dissipating the kinetic energy of a passing Mars/Moon-sized body into a closed orbit around Earth without tidal disruption is dynamically near-impossible, and a captured foreigner would not share Earth's exact mantle isotopes.
    3. **Co-Accretion / Binary Sister (`Édouard Roche 1873`; `Viktor S. Safronov 1969`):** Proposed that Earth and the Moon accreted side-by-side from the same circum-solar dust ring. Disproved because a sister world condensing from the same material as Earth (`mean density 5.514 g/cm³`, with an iron-nickel core making up **`~32.5%` of Earth's mass**) would have a large iron core — whereas seismic (`Apollo Passive Seismic Experiment`) and gravity (`GRAIL`) data prove the Moon has a **mean density of only `3.344 g/cm³`** and a **tiny iron core (`radius ~330–350 km`, `<1–2%` of lunar mass; `Weber et al. 2011`, *Science* `331`, `309`)**.
  - Between **July 1969 (`Apollo 11`) and December 1972 (`Apollo 17`)**, twelve NASA astronauts returned **`381.7 kilograms` (`841.6 pounds`, `2,196` individual specimens; supplemented by `~326 g` from Soviet `Luna 16, 20, 24` and `1,731 g` from China's `Chang'e 5` in `2020` / `1,935.3 g` from `Chang'e 6` in `2024`)** of lunar rocks and regolith. In **January 1970** (`Apollo 11 Lunar Science Conference`, *Science* `167`), **John A. Wood** (`1932–2026`, Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory) and independently **James V. Smith** (`1928–2007`, University of Chicago) discovered that the Moon's bright highlands consist of low-density calcium-rich **plagioclase feldspar (`anorthosite`)** that floated like icebergs atop a **global Lunar Magma Ocean** hundreds of kilometres deep, while the Moon as a whole is strongly depleted in volatile elements (`water, sodium, potassium, zinc, lead`) boiled away by extreme heat. In **October 2023**, **Jennika Greer** (`born 1995`), **Bidong Zhang**, **Philipp R. Heck** (`born 1973`), and colleagues (`Greer et al. 2023`, *Geochemical Perspectives Letters* `27`, `49–53`) used atom-probe tomography (`nanoscale Pb-Pb dating`) on zircon crystals inside **`Apollo 17` lunar norite sample `72255`** (collected in **December 1972** by **Harrison H. Schmitt** [`born 1935`] and **Eugene A. Cernan** [`1934–2017`]) to prove the Lunar Magma Ocean had already solidified by **`4.46 ± 0.04 billion years ago`**.
- **The Giant Impact Hypothesis — `Theia` Collides With Proto-Earth (`Hartmann & Davis 1975`; `Cameron & Ward 1976`; `Halliday 2000`; `Canup & Asphaug 2001`):**
  - Formulated in **1974–1975** by planetary scientists **William K. Hartmann** (`born 1939`) and **Donald R. Davis** (`born 1939`, `Hartmann & Davis 1975`, *Icarus* `24`, `504–515`, *"Satellite-sized planetesimals and lunar origin"*) and independently by **Alastair G. W. Cameron** (`1925–2005`) and **William R. Ward** (`1944–2018`, `Cameron & Ward 1976`, *Lunar Science VII*, `120–122`), the **Giant Impact Hypothesis** explained simultaneously the Moon's lack of iron, its volatile depletion, its global magma ocean, and the Earth-Moon system's high angular momentum (`L = 3.5 × 10³⁴ kg·m²/s`):
    - Approximately **`4.51 to 4.46 billion years ago`** (`~50–100 million years` after the Solar System's `4.567-Gyr` CAIs), a differentiated **Mars-sized protoplanet (`mass ~0.10–0.15 M_⊕` [`~6–9 × 10²³ kg`], diameter `~6,000–6,500 km`, named `Theia` in February 2000 by geochemist Alex N. Halliday [`born 1952`, *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* `176`, `17–30`] after the Greek Titaness mother of the Moon goddess Selene)** struck the `~90%-grown` proto-Earth.
    - In the canonical Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (`SPH`) simulations of **Robin M. Canup** (`born 1968`) and **Erik Asphaug** (`born 1961`, `Canup & Asphaug 2001`, *Nature* `412`, `708–712`), `Theia` hit proto-Earth at an **oblique angle of `~45°`** at an impact velocity of **`~9 to 10 km/s`** (`just above mutual escape velocity v_esc ≈ 9.3 km/s`). **`Theia`'s metallic iron core sank through Earth's mantle to merge with Earth's core**, while `1.5–2 lunar masses` of iron-depleted silicate mantle rock was sheared off into orbit beyond Earth's **`~18,400-km` (`~2.9 R_E`) Roche limit**, tilting Earth's rotational axis to **`23.44°`** and accelerating Earth's spin to an initial **`~5-hour` day** (`with the newborn Moon orbiting just ~20,000–25,000 km away — appearing ~15 times wider in the sky than today`).
- **The Lunar Isotope Crisis & the `Synestia` Vaporized-Planet Model (`Wiechert et al. 2001`; `Zhang et al. 2012`; `Lock & Stewart 2017` / `Lock et al. 2018`; `Cano et al. 2020`):**
  - Because `Canup & Asphaug 2001` showed that **`~70 to 80%` of the proto-lunar disk comes from `Theia`'s mantle**, the Moon should carry `Theia`'s distinct primordial isotopic fingerprint (for example, Mars differs from Earth in oxygen **`Δ¹⁷O` by `+320 ppm`**). Instead, laser-fluorination and multi-collector mass spectrometry of Apollo rocks revealed the **Lunar Isotope Crisis**:
    - **Oxygen isotopes (`¹⁶O, ¹⁷O, ¹⁸O`):** **Ulrich Wiechert**, **Alex N. Halliday**, *et al.* (`2001`, *Science* `294`, `345–348`) and **Edward D. Young** *et al.* (`2016`, *Science* `351`, `493–496`) showed that Earth and Moon silicate rocks share identical **`Δ¹⁷O` within `±5 parts per million` (`±0.0005%`)**.
    - **Titanium (`⁵⁰Ti/⁴⁷Ti`) & Tungsten (`¹⁸²W`) isotopes:** **Junjun Zhang**, **Nicolas Dauphas** (`born 1975`), **Andrew M. Davis**, *et al.* (`2012`, *Nature Geoscience* `5`, `251–255`) proved that lunar refractory titanium **`ε⁵⁰Ti` matches Earth within `±4 ppm`**, proving the Moon cannot be `70–80%` unmixed foreign material unless `Theia` either equilibrated completely with Earth's mantle or formed at virtually the same heliocentric distance as Earth (`Dauphas et al. 2014`).
  - **The `Synestia` Solution (`Lock & Stewart 2017`; `Lock et al. 2018`):** Planetary physicists **Simon J. Lock** (`born 1990`) and **Sarah T. Stewart** (`born 1973`, `Lock & Stewart 2017`, *J. Geophys. Res.: Planets* `122`, `950–982`; `Lock et al. 2018`, *JGR Planets* `123`, `910–951`) showed that a faster, more energetic collision pushes the combined Earth-`Theia` system past its corotation limit into a giant, Biconcave spinning donut of supercritical silicate rock vapour (`3,000–4,000 K`, expanding beyond `50,000 km` radius) that they named a **`Synestia`** (after *Hestia*, goddess of architecture). Inside the turbulent `Synestia`, `Theia` and Earth mantle vapour mix thoroughly before the outer cloud cools by radiation, raining liquid rock droplets that coalesce into the Moon *inside* the vapour cloud before the `Synestia` contracts back into the modern Earth.
  - **Deep Lunar Mantle `Δ¹⁷O` Gradient (`Cano, Sharp & Shearer 2020`):** In **March 2020**, **Erick J. Cano**, **Zachary D. Sharp** (`born 1957`), and **Charles K. Shearer** (`Cano et al. 2020`, *Nature Geoscience* `13`, `270–274`) measured high-precision oxygen isotopes across diverse Apollo lithologies and discovered that while shallow crustal rocks match Earth (`homogenized in the outer vapour atmosphere`), **deep-mantle volcanic glasses (`green and orange picritic glasses`) preserve a distinct `Δ¹⁷O` offset (`up to +15–20 ppm`)**, revealing an unmixed **`Theia`-rich core deep inside the Moon**!
- **The `2022` `10⁸`-Particle Supercomputer Discovery (`Kegerreis et al. 2022`) & the `2023` Discovery of `Theia` Inside Earth's Mantle (`Yuan et al. 2023`):**
  - **Formed in Hours (`Kegerreis et al. 2022`, *ApJ Letters* `937`, `L40`):** In **October 2022**, **Jacob A. Kegerreis** (`born 1994`, Durham University / NASA Ames Research Center), **Vincent R. Eke** (`born 1971`), **Richard J. Massey** (`born 1977`), **Luis F. A. Teodoro**, and colleagues ran `400` SPH simulations at **`10⁵` up to `10⁸` (`100 million`) particles** on the **`COSMA` (`DiRAC`) supercomputer** using the open-source **`SWIFT`** hydrodynamics code. At `10⁸`-particle resolution (eliminating numerical artificial-viscosity suppression that plagued `10⁵`-particle models), a single `~45°` collision stretches the disrupted impactor into two massive tidal clumps: the larger inner clump falls back onto proto-Earth while its gravitational torque **slingshots the outer clump directly into a wide, stable orbit outside the Roche limit within just a few hours** — producing an immediate satellite whose **outer layers are `~60%` proto-Earth mantle** wrapped around a **`Theia`-dominated interior** (naturally explaining both Apollo's Earth-like surface isotopes and `Cano et al. 2020`'s deep-interior `Theia` signature, as well as the Moon's tilted `~5°` initial orbital inclination!).
  - **`Theia` Buried `2,900 km` Inside Earth (`Yuan et al. 2023`, *Nature* `623`, `95–99`):** In **November 2023**, Caltech geophysicist **Qian Yuan** (`born 1992`), **Mingming Li**, **Paul D. Asimow** (`born 1970`), **Hongping Deng**, **Vincent Eke**, **Jacob Kegerreis**, and colleagues solved one of deep-Earth seismology's greatest mysteries: the two continent-sized **`Large Low-Shear-Velocity Provinces` (`LLSVPs`)** sitting atop Earth's core-mantle boundary **`~2,900 kilometres` (`1,800 miles`)** beneath **Africa** and the **Pacific Ocean** (`where seismic shear waves slow by ~1–3%, each spanning thousands of kilometres across and up to ~900–1,000 km high`).
