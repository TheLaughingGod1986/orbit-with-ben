On Mercury, the sun's closest planet, why does water ice survive four hundred and thirty-degree heat? The ice hides in polar craters that never see sunlight. At fifty-eight million kilometres out, sunlight shines up to seven times brighter than on Earth, yet without an atmosphere to retain heat, you would feel night plunge to minus one hundred and eighty degrees Celsius. In 1965, Italian mathematician Giuseppe Colombo solved its spin mystery.
