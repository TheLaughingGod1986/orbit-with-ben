## Primary Sources

1. **NASA Science — Parker Solar Probe Mission Overview (`https://science.nasa.gov/mission/parker-solar-probe/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Flying `more than seven times closer to the Sun than any spacecraft` over `seven years` and `24 orbits around the Sun` (baseline mission), top speed at closest approach of `approximately 430,000 mph (700,000 kph)` (`fast enough to get from Philadelphia to Washington, D.C., in one second`), protection by a `4.5-inch-thick (11.43 cm) carbon-composite shield` withstanding `nearly 2,500 degrees Fahrenheit (1,377 Celsius)`, launch on `Aug. 12, 2018 / 7:31 UTC` from `Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, Fla.` aboard a `Delta IV-Heavy with Upper Stage` at a launch mass of `1,510 pounds (685 kilograms)`, naming for `the late Dr. Eugene N. Parker` (`1927–2022`, died `March 15, 2022, at age 94`), who as a young professor at the `University of Chicago in the mid-1950s` developed a mathematical theory predicting the `solar wind`, visited the spacecraft on `Oct. 3, 2017` (`first NASA mission named for a living researcher`), and in `2018` became `the first person to witness the launch of a spacecraft bearing his name`.

2. **NASA Feature — *Traveling to the Sun: Why Won’t Parker Solar Probe Melt?* (Susannah Darling / NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, Jul. 19, 2018, `https://www.nasa.gov/general/traveling-to-the-sun-why-wont-parker-solar-probe-melt/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Susannah Darling, NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center / National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Yardstick scale (`If Earth was at one end of a yard-stick and the Sun on the other, Parker Solar Probe will make it to within four inches of the solar surface`), physical distinction between **temperature** (`how fast particles are moving`) and **heat** (`the total amount of energy that they transfer`) in the low-density solar corona (`several million degrees` vs `about 2,500 degrees Fahrenheit (about 1,400 degrees Celsius)` on the sun-facing shield surface, compared with volcanic lava at `1,300 and 2,200 F (700 and 1,200 C)`), hot oven vs pot of boiling water analogy, Thermal Protection System (`TPS`) dimensions (`8 feet (2.4 meters) in diameter and 4.5 inches (about 115 mm) thick`, carbon composite foam sandwiched between two carbon plates with white ceramic paint on the sun-facing plate, designed by Johns Hopkins APL and built at Carbon-Carbon Advanced Technologies, tested to `3,000 F (1,650 C)`), lead thermal engineer `Betsy Congdon of Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Lab`, comfortable `85 F (30 C)` spacecraft body behind the shield, `Solar Probe Cup` (Faraday cup) built from `Titanium-Zirconium-Molybdenum` (`melting point of about 4,260 F (2,349 C)`), `tungsten` grids (`highest known melting point of 6,192 F (3,422 C)`, acid-etched because lasers could not etch the gridlines), `sapphire crystal tubes` and `niobium` wiring, testing in the `Odeillo Solar Furnace` (`10,000 adjustable mirrors`) plus particle accelerator and jury-rigged `IMAX projectors`, `Justin Kasper` (principal investigator for the `SWEAP` instruments at the `University of Michigan in Ann Arbor`), cooling system (`about a gallon (3.7 liters) of deionized water` operating between `50 F (10 C) and 257 F (125 C)`, powerful enough to cool an average-sized living room), and autonomous attitude control across the `eight minutes` one-way light travel time to Earth using `several sensors, about half the size of a cell phone`, along the edge of the heat shield shadow.

3. **Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) — *Parker Solar Probe: Spacecraft* (`https://parkersolarprobe.jhuapl.edu/Spacecraft/index.php`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL).
   - **Used for:** Eugene Parker theorizing `an explanation for the superheated solar atmosphere, the corona, which is — contrary to what was expected by physics laws — hotter than the surface of the Sun itself`, heat shield weight (`160 pounds (73 kilograms)`), two retractable solar arrays (`each about 3.7 feet (1.1 meters) long by 2.3 feet (0.7 meters) wide`, total area `17.2 square feet (1.6 square meters)`, producing `388 watts of power, depending on configuration — about enough to run a kitchen blender`), `seven Venus flybys over nearly seven years` to shrink the orbit, closest approach of `3.8 million miles (about 6.1 million kilometers)` / `3.83 million miles (and 6.16 million kilometers)`, and solar intensity at closest passes of `about 475 times what spacecraft experience while orbiting Earth`.

4. **NASA News Release & Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) Release — *NASA Enters the Solar Atmosphere for the First Time, Bringing New Discoveries* / *Parker Solar Probe Touches the Sun for the First Time, Bringing New Discoveries* (Dec. 14, 2021, `https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/nasa-enters-the-solar-atmosphere-for-the-first-time-bringing-new-discoveries/` & `https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/211214-parker-solar-probe-touches-the-sun`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Mara Johnson-Groh (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center) & Michael Buckley (JHU APL).
   - **Used for:** First crossing of the `Alfvén critical surface` on `April 28, 2021`, during the `eighth flyby` of the Sun, at `18.8 solar radii (8.127 million miles / around 8.1 million miles)` above the solar surface (`named for Hannes Alfvén`), dipping to `14.97 solar radii (6.4714 million miles / just beneath 15 solar radii, around 6.5 million miles)` through a coronal `pseudostreamer` (`like flying into the eye of a storm`), final closest approach reaching `8.86 solar radii (3.83 million miles)` from the surface compared with Earth `93 million miles away`, discovery in `2019` at `34 solar radii` that magnetic zig-zag structures called `switchbacks` are common in the solar wind, and sixth-flyby data (`less than 25 solar radii out`, led by `Stuart Bale, professor at the University of California, Berkeley`) showing switchbacks occur in patches with a higher percentage of `helium` aligned with magnetic funnels emerging from the photosphere between `supergranules`.

   - **Used for:** Record closest approach set on `Dec. 24, 2024` at `3.8 million miles` from the solar surface and `430,000 miles per hour`, completion of the `24th close approach` on `June 19, 2025` (`3.8 million miles (6.2 million kilometers)`, concluding the spacecraft's baseline 24-orbit mission plan), `June 2018` thermal-blanket installation below the TPS (`NASA/Johns Hopkins APL/Ed Whitman`), completion of the `28th close approach` on `June 8, 2026` and `29th close approach` on `Sept. 4, 2026` (matching the `3.8 million miles` and `430,000 mph` records for the seventh time since Dec. 24, 2024, operating autonomously out of contact for `nine days` and sampling `almost 40% of the solar circumference within one day`), heat shield reaching an estimated `about 1,700 degrees Fahrenheit at closest approach` with consistent barrier-blanket temperatures confirming zero thermal degradation, and formal mission extension `into 2029 as a result of the 2026 Heliophysics Senior Review`.
