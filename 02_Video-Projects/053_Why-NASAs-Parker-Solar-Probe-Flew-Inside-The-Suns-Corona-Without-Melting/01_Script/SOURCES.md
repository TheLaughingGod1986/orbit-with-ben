# Sources — 053 Why NASA's Parker Solar Probe Flew Inside the Sun's Corona without Melting

Every number, date, named person, physical scale, and factual claim in `01_Script/parker_solar_probe_script_master_v00.md` is backed below by primary NASA Science, NASA Feature, Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL), NASA Parker Solar Probe Blog, and Library of Congress Name Authority sources with verbatim quotes verified directly against the live pages on 19 Oct 2026 (`J0666`).

## Primary Sources

1. **NASA Science — Parker Solar Probe Mission Overview (`https://science.nasa.gov/mission/parker-solar-probe/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Flying `more than seven times closer to the Sun than any spacecraft` over `seven years` and `24 orbits around the Sun` (baseline mission), top speed at closest approach of `approximately 430,000 mph (700,000 kph)` (`fast enough to get from Philadelphia to Washington, D.C., in one second`), protection by a `4.5-inch-thick (11.43 cm) carbon-composite shield` withstanding `nearly 2,500 degrees Fahrenheit (1,377 Celsius)`, launch on `Aug. 12, 2018 / 7:31 UTC` from `Cape Canaveral Air Force Station, Fla.` aboard a `Delta IV-Heavy with Upper Stage` at a launch mass of `1,510 pounds (685 kilograms)`, naming for `the late Dr. Eugene N. Parker` (`1927–2022`, died `March 15, 2022, at age 94`), who as a young professor at the `University of Chicago in the mid-1950s` developed a mathematical theory predicting the `solar wind`, visited the spacecraft on `Oct. 3, 2017` (`first NASA mission named for a living researcher`), and in `2018` became `the first person to witness the launch of a spacecraft bearing his name`.

2. **NASA Feature — *Traveling to the Sun: Why Won’t Parker Solar Probe Melt?* (Susannah Darling / NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, Jul. 19, 2018, `https://www.nasa.gov/general/traveling-to-the-sun-why-wont-parker-solar-probe-melt/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Susannah Darling, NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center / National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Yardstick scale (`If Earth was at one end of a yard-stick and the Sun on the other, Parker Solar Probe will make it to within four inches of the solar surface`), physical distinction between **temperature** (`how fast particles are moving`) and **heat** (`the total amount of energy that they transfer`) in the low-density solar corona (`several million degrees` vs `about 2,500 degrees Fahrenheit (about 1,400 degrees Celsius)` on the sun-facing shield surface, hotter than volcanic lava at `1,300 and 2,200 F (700 and 1,200 C)`), hot oven vs pot of boiling water analogy, Thermal Protection System (`TPS`) dimensions (`8 feet (2.4 meters) in diameter and 4.5 inches (about 115 mm) thick`, carbon composite foam sandwiched between two carbon plates with white ceramic paint on the sun-facing plate, designed by Johns Hopkins APL and built at Carbon-Carbon Advanced Technologies, tested to `3,000 F (1,650 C)`), lead thermal engineer `Betsy Congdon of Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Lab`, comfortable `85 F (30 C)` spacecraft body behind the shield, `Solar Probe Cup` (Faraday cup) built from `Titanium-Zirconium-Molybdenum` (`melting point of about 4,260 F (2,349 C)`), `tungsten` grids (`highest known melting point of 6,192 F (3,422 C)`, acid-etched because lasers could not etch the gridlines), `sapphire crystal tubes` and `niobium` wiring, testing in the `Odeillo Solar Furnace` (`10,000 adjustable mirrors`) plus particle accelerator and jury-rigged `IMAX projectors`, `Justin Kasper` (principal investigator for the `SWEAP` instruments at the `University of Michigan in Ann Arbor`), cooling system (`about a gallon (3.7 liters) of deionized water` operating between `50 F (10 C) and 257 F (125 C)`, powerful enough to cool an average-sized living room), and autonomous attitude control across the `eight minutes` one-way light travel time to Earth using `several sensors, about half the size of a cell phone`, along the edge of the heat shield shadow.

3. **Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) — *Parker Solar Probe: Spacecraft* (`https://parkersolarprobe.jhuapl.edu/Spacecraft/index.php`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL).
   - **Used for:** Heat shield weight (`160 pounds (73 kilograms)`), two retractable solar arrays (`each about 3.7 feet (1.1 meters) long by 2.3 feet (0.7 meters) wide`, total area `17.2 square feet (1.6 square meters)`, producing `388 watts of power, depending on configuration — about enough to run a kitchen blender`), `seven Venus flybys over nearly seven years` to shrink the orbit, and solar intensity at closest passes of `about 475 times what spacecraft experience while orbiting Earth`.

4. **Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) / NASA Goddard News Release — *Parker Solar Probe Touches the Sun for the First Time, Bringing New Discoveries* (Dec. 14, 2021, `https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/211214-parker-solar-probe-touches-the-sun`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Michael Buckley (JHU APL) & Mara Johnson-Groh (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center).
   - **Used for:** First crossing of the `Alfvén critical surface` on `April 28, 2021`, during the `eighth flyby` of the Sun, at `18.8 solar radii (8.127 million miles)` above the solar surface (`named for Hannes Alfvén`), dipping to `14.97 solar radii (6.4714 million miles)` through a coronal `pseudostreamer` (`like flying into the eye of a storm`), discovery in `2019` at `34 solar radii` that magnetic zig-zag structures called `switchbacks` are common in the solar wind, and sixth-flyby data (`less than 25 solar radii out`, led by `Stuart Bale, professor at the University of California, Berkeley`) showing switchbacks occur in patches with a higher percentage of `helium` aligned with magnetic funnels emerging from the photosphere between `supergranules`.

5. **NASA Parker Solar Probe Blog — Dec. 27, 2024 (`https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/parker-solar-probe/2024/12/27/nasas-parker-solar-probe-reports-successful-closest-approach-to-sun/`), June 11, 2026 (`https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/parker-solar-probe/2026/06/11/parker-solar-probe-makes-28th-close-pass-of-sun/`), and Sept. 10, 2026 (`https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/parker-solar-probe/2026/09/10/after-latest-swing-past-sun-nasas-parker-solar-probe-checks-in/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team / Johns Hopkins APL.
   - **Used for:** Record closest approach set on `Dec. 24, 2024` at `3.8 million miles` from the solar surface and `430,000 miles per hour`, completion of the `24th close approach` on `June 19, 2025` (concluding the baseline 24-orbit plan), completion of the `29th close approach` on `Sept. 4, 2026` (matching the `3.8 million miles` and `430,000 mph` records for the seventh time since Dec. 24, 2024, operating autonomously out of contact for `nine days` and sampling `almost 40% of the solar circumference within one day`), heat shield reaching an estimated `about 1,700 degrees Fahrenheit at closest approach` with zero thermal degradation, and formal mission extension `into 2029 as a result of the 2026 Heliophysics Senior Review`.

6. **Library of Congress Name Authority (`https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n78096083.json` & `https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n50035250.json`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Library of Congress.
   - **Used for:** `WHO:` card life dates and roles for `Eugene Newman Parker (1927–2022)` (`Parker, E. N. (Eugene Newman), 1927-2022`, American astrophysicist) and `Hannes Alfvén (1908–1995)` (`Alfvén, Hannes, 1908-1995`, Swedish plasma physicist).
