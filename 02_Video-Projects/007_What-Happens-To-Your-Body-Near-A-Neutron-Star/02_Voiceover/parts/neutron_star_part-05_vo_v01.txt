Orbit watches the universe fold. A ring of warped starlight tightens. The neutron star doesn’t look like a distant Sun. It looks like a hole punched in the rules — still with a surface, still with a crust — but already rewriting the map of every photon.

What would your last clear image be?

Maybe not the star at all. Maybe the last honest photons from the galaxy behind you — while ahead, magnetism and radiation erase the idea of a calm view.

And yet — for a fragile moment — your eyes might still work.

Your body will not get that luxury for long.

Why does vision outlast comfort? Because light can still arrive after matter has already started to fail.

That mismatch is the horror — and the wonder.
