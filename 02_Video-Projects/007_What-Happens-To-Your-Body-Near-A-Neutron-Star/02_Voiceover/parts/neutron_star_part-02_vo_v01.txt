Orbit drifts closer and the “star” refuses to get bigger the way a planet would. It stays compact. Fierce. Wrong.

Why does something so small refuse to behave like anything you’ve ever stood on?

Because this isn’t a world with polite gravity and thick air. This is matter that lost to collapse — and survived only by becoming denser than anything Earth has ever manufactured.

A teaspoon of this stuff would outweigh mountains. Not metaphorically. Literally.

But Orbit isn’t holding a teaspoon yet. Orbit is still far enough out that the danger looks like beauty: a leftover engine of stellar death, spinning, maybe flashing like a lighthouse if radiation beams sweep the sky.

Could you watch from here and live?

Yes — if “here” is careful. Distance is mercy.

The question is what happens when mercy runs out.
