People ask: what if you stood on it?

You don’t.

Not because the crust isn’t real — it is, in the sense physicists mean: a rigid outer layer. There may even be “mountains” so shallow they’d shame a sand grain, yet massive enough to ripple spacetime for gravitational-wave hunters.

You don’t stand because your body cannot be a rigid object under that weight. Bones, steel, diamond — all of them are suggestions. Surface gravity can be hundreds of billions of times Earth’s. An apple wouldn’t fall. An apple would become a rumour.

Orbit’s underside glow flares as thrusters refuse the landing. The lesson is brutal and clean: a surface can exist without being a place for visitors.
