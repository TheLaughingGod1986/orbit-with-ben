Could a robot survive on Mars? NASA's Opportunity rover was built to last ninety days and kept going for more than fourteen years. Mars rarely smashes the robots we send. It wears them down, with cold, dust and wear you already know from home.

We follow the Mars rovers and landers that lasted longest, and what finally stopped each one: Spirit stuck in soft sand at Troy as winter cut its power, Phoenix's solar panels most likely bent by winter frost, Opportunity's sky going dark in the 2018 global dust storm, InSight slowly starved by dust after more than a thousand marsquakes, Curiosity's punctured aluminium wheels, and the Ingenuity Mars helicopter, planned for up to five flights and flown seventy-two times. Then the real question: what would a robot need to survive on Mars?
