## 1. Mon 9 Nov: A Mars Rover Built for 90 Days Lasted 14 Years

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. A NASA Mars panorama with rover tracks, already panning. Whoosh. Caption: 90 DAYS. No Orbit.]

This rover was built to last ninety days on Mars.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. NASA Opportunity self-portrait or deck view. Orbit (Omni only) may enter small. If Omni is down, stay on the world.]

It lasted more than fourteen years.

[VISUAL MUST: Dusty deck, then clean deck. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

Dust should have choked its solar panels within months.
Instead, Martian wind kept sweeping them clean.
Then, in 2018, a storm wrapped the whole planet, and the sky went dark.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening panorama.]

The full film: Could a Robot Survive on Mars?

[TEACH: Opportunity was designed for 90 sols and operated January 2004 to June 2018. Cleaning events restored power. The MY34 global storm ended contact (NASA).]

## 2. Wed 11 Nov: Could a Robot Survive a Night on Mars?

Uses the "Could a Robot…" slot. The robot beat needs Omni. If Omni is down, the Short runs on NASA frost and Spirit imagery, and the VO stands either way.

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. A NASA frosty Mars morning, light already rising. Whoosh. Caption: MINUS 90. No Orbit.]

On Mars, the night can fall to minus ninety degrees.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Orbit (Omni) small on the frosted ground, glow flickering.]
[ORBIT ACTS: Orbit shuffles towards the first patch of sunlight.]

Could a robot survive one?

[VISUAL MUST: Dusk on a Mars plain. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

Batteries hate deep cold, like your phone on a frozen day.
So rovers burn power just to keep their insides warm.
In 2009, Spirit got stuck and couldn't catch the winter Sun.

[VISUAL MUST: Spirit's tracks at Troy. The last 3 s return to the opening frost.]

The full film: Could a Robot Survive on Mars?

[TEACH: Viking 1 recorded about −89 °C at night (NASA NSSDCA). Spirit was embedded at Troy in 2009 and last communicated in March 2010.]

## 3. Fri 13 Nov: Ingenuity Was Built for 5 Flights. It Flew 72.

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. NASA Ingenuity in flight, its shadow already crossing the ground. Whoosh. Caption: 72 FLIGHTS. No Orbit.]

This helicopter was built for five flights on Mars.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Ingenuity on the ground beside Perseverance's tracks.]

It flew seventy-two.

[VISUAL MUST: The thin Mars sky. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

The air there is about one percent as thick as ours.
Its blades spun more than two thousand times a minute to lift it.
In 2024, a damaged blade finally grounded it. It's still standing there.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening shadow in flight.]

The full film: Could a Robot Survive on Mars?

[TEACH: Ingenuity was planned for up to 5 flights in 30 days and flew 72; blade damage after Flight 72 (18 Jan 2024) ended operations (NASA). Rotors spun at about 2,400–2,700 rpm in air about 1% of Earth's density.]
