1. **The `110.6-Metre` Saturn V & `Apollo 11`'s `25-Second` Landing (`July 1969`)** — How **John Houbolt** (`WHO: John Houbolt (1919–2014) — Lunar-orbit rendezvous engineer`) convinced NASA that only a **`15.1-tonne` Lunar Module** needed to touch the Moon (`DATA` #1 + `SCALE: Saturn V weighed 2,900 tonnes at launch, ~190x the Lunar Module`), and how on **20 July 1969** (`WHEN: 20 July 1969`) **Neil Armstrong** (`WHO: Neil Armstrong (1930–2012) — Apollo 11 commander`) and **Buzz Aldrin** (`WHO: Buzz Aldrin (born 1930) — Lunar Module Pilot`) rode **Margaret Hamilton**'s (`WHO: Margaret Hamilton (born 1936) — MIT Apollo software lead`) priority-scheduled **`74-KB` guidance computer** through `1202` alarms to land `Eagle` at Tranquility Base with **`~25 seconds` of hover fuel left** (`FACT: 74-KB computer, 25s of fuel`).
2. **Struck Twice by Lightning (`Apollo 12`, `November 1969`) & the `321,860-km` Explosion (`Apollo 13`, `April 1970`)** — When **Apollo 12** was struck twice by lightning at `36.5 s` and `52 s` after liftoff (`WHEN: 14 November 1969`), `26-year-old` controller **John Aaron** (`WHO: John Aaron (born 1943) — NASA EECOM flight controller`) saved the mission with **`"Try SCE to Aux"`**, allowing **Pete Conrad** (`WHO: Pete Conrad (1930–1999) — Apollo 12 commander`) to land **`163 metres` (`535 ft`) from robotic `Surveyor 3`** (`FACT: Landed 163m from Surveyor 3`). Five months later on **13 April 1970** (`WHEN: 13 April 1970`), **Apollo 13** suffered an oxygen-tank explosion **`321,860 km` from Earth** and looped **`400,171 km`** around the far side inside the Lunar Module lifeboat.
3. **Climbing Into the Highlands: `Apollo 14` at Fra Mauro (`February 1971`)** — After **Don Eyles** (`WHO: Don Eyles (born 1944) — MIT LM software programmer`) wrote a mid-descent software patch to bypass a shorted abort switch, **Alan Shepard** (`WHO: Alan Shepard (1923–1998) — Apollo 14 commander`) and Edgar Mitchell landed `Antares` at **Fra Mauro** (`WHEN: 5 February 1971`), hiked toward `Cone Crater`, and brought back **`42.28 kg` of rocks** — including **`Big Bertha` (`14321`)**, containing a `4.0-billion-year-old` zircon clast likely blasted off early Earth (`Bellucci et al. 2019`).
4. **The Electric-Rover J-Missions: `Apollo 15` (`July 1971`) & `Apollo 16` (`April 1972`)** — For the final three landings, NASA added the folding **`210-kg` electric Lunar Roving Vehicle** (`DATA` #2 + `SCALE: 90.8 km driven across Apollo 15–17 vs 250 m walked on Apollo 11`). On **Apollo 15** (`WHEN: 30 July 1971`), **David Scott** (`WHO: David Scott (born 1932) — Apollo 15 commander`) and James Irwin drove **`27.9 km`** along `Hadley Rille`, found the **`4.1–4.5-billion-year-old` `Genesis Rock` (`15415`)**, and dropped a hammer and falcon feather in vacuum; on **Apollo 16** (`WHEN: 21 April 1972`), **John Young** (`WHO: John Young (1930–2018) — Apollo 16 commander`) and Charlie Duke proved the **Descartes Highlands** were giant impact breccias (`95.71 kg` returned).
5. **The Final Footsteps & Volcanic Orange Glass: `Apollo 17` at Taurus-Littrow (`December 1972`)** — On **11 December 1972** (`WHEN: 11 December 1972`), **Eugene Cernan** (`WHO: Eugene Cernan (1934–2017) — Apollo 17 commander`) and geologist **Harrison Schmitt** (`WHO: Harrison Schmitt (born 1935) — Geologist astronaut, Apollo 17`) spent **`75 hours`** on the Moon, driving **`35.9 km`** and returning **`110.52 kg`** of samples (`381.7 kg` across all 6 landings, `DATA` #3 + `SCALE: 381.7 kg total Moon rocks across 6 landings, 5x Apollo 11's 21.55 kg`). At **Shorty Crater**, Schmitt uncovered **`3.64-billion-year-old` volcanic orange glass beads (`Sample 74220`)** whose melt inclusions (`Saal et al. 2008`; `Hauri et al. 2011`) proved the Moon's deep mantle holds as much water as Earth's upper mantle (`FACT: Orange glass held lunar water`).
