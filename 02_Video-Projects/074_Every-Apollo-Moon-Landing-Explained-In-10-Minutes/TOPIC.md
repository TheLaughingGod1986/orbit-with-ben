### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **The Saturn V & Lunar-Orbit Rendezvous Architecture (NASA SP-4029 *Apollo by the Numbers*, `Orloff 2000`/`2004`; NASA SP-4205 *Chariots for Apollo*):**
  - The **Saturn V** stood **`110.6 metres` (`363 ft`)** tall, weighed **`~2,900–2,970 tonnes` (`6.4–6.5 million lb`)** fuelled at liftoff (increasing from `2,909 t` on `Apollo 11` to `2,923 t` on `Apollo 15–17`), and generated **`~33.4–34.5 meganewtons` (`7.5–7.65 million pounds-force`)** of first-stage (`S-IC`) thrust from five Rocketdyne **F-1** engines burning RP-1 kerosene and liquid oxygen at `~13–15 tonnes per second`.
  - Using **John C. Houbolt**'s (`1919–2014`) **Lunar-Orbit Rendezvous (`LOR`)** profile, only the two-stage **`~15.1–16.4-tonne` Grumman Lunar Module (`LM`)** descended to the surface while the Command and Service Module (`CSM`) remained in a `~110-km` lunar orbit.
- **Landing #1 — `Apollo 11` (`20 July 1969`, `Mare Tranquillitatis`, NASA SP-214 & SP-4029):**
  - Crew: **Neil A. Armstrong** (`1930–2012`), **Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin** (`born 1930`), and Command Module Pilot **Michael Collins** (`1930–2021`).
  - During powered descent of Lunar Module **`Eagle` (`LM-5`)**, an unexpected radar phase-mismatch flooded the **`74-kilobyte` (`36,864` 16-bit words ROM + `2,048` words RAM) Apollo Guidance Computer (`AGC`)** with **`1201` and `1202` executive-overflow alarms** (solved in real time by MIT software lead **Margaret Hamilton**'s priority-scheduling architecture and `26-year-old` Guidance Officer **Steve Bales** calling `"GO"`).
  - Seeing the computer steering toward boulder-strewn **West Crater** (`~180 metres` / `600 ft` wide; `~4 miles` downrange of planned ellipse centre), Armstrong took semi-manual (`P66` attitude/rate-of-descent) control at `~150 m` (`500 ft`) altitude and landed at **`20:17:40 UTC` on 20 July 1969** (`0.67° N, 23.47° E`) with **`~25 seconds` of usable descent hover propellant remaining** before the low-level sensor forced an abort-stage (`Orloff 2000`, NASA SP-4029: `25 seconds` remaining above the abort redline after correcting for propellant slosh).
  - Surface stay: **`21 hours, 36 minutes`** (`1` EVA of **`2 hours, 31 minutes, 40 seconds`**, walking up to `60 m` from `Eagle`), deploying the Passive Seismic Experiment and **Laser Ranging Retroreflector (`LRRR`)**, and returning **`21.55 kg` (`47.5 lb`)** of titanium-rich mare basalt (`~3.6–3.8 billion years` old).
- **Landing #2 — `Apollo 12` (`19 November 1969`, `Oceanus Procellarum` & `Surveyor 3`, NASA SP-235 & SP-4029):**
  - Crew: **Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.** (`1930–1999`), **Alan L. Bean** (`1932–2018`), and **Richard F. Gordon Jr.** (`1929–2017`).
  - At **`36.5 seconds` and `52 seconds` after liftoff** on **14 November 1969**, Saturn V triggered **two lightning strikes** through rain clouds that knocked offline all three fuel cells and scrambled telemetry until `26-year-old` EECOM flight controller **John Aaron** (`born 1943`) called **`"Flight, try SCE to Aux"`** (Signal Conditioning Equipment to Auxiliary), allowing Alan Bean to restore telemetry.
  - On **19 November 1969**, Conrad landed Lunar Module **`Intrepid`** in the **Ocean of Storms (`Oceanus Procellarum`)** just **`163 metres` (`535 ft`)** from NASA's robotic **`Surveyor 3`** lander (which had been sitting on the Moon for `31 months` since `20 April 1967`). Across **`2` EVAs (`7 hours, 45 minutes` total)** during a `31.5-hour` stay, they retrieved `Surveyor 3`'s TV camera and returned **`34.35 kg` (`75.7 lb`)** of lunar samples.
- **The Near-Fatal Abort & Landing #3 — `Apollo 13` (`April 1970`) & `Apollo 14` (`5 February 1971`, `Fra Mauro`, NASA SP-272 & SP-4029):**
  - **Apollo 13** (**Jim Lovell**, **Jack Swigert**, **Fred Haise**): On **13 April 1970** (`55 hours, 54 minutes, 53 seconds` MET) at **`321,860 km` (`200,000 miles`)** from Earth, damaged Teflon wire insulation inside Service Module **Oxygen Tank No. 2** ignited during a routine cryo-stir, blowing off the Sector 4 panel. Using Lunar Module **`Aquarius`** as a lifeboat (powering down to `12 amps`, building the mailbox `LiOH` carbon-dioxide scrubber adapter, and looping **`400,171 km`** from Earth around the far side), all three astronauts splashed down safely on **17 April 1970**.
  - **Apollo 14** (**Alan B. Shepard Jr.**, `1923–1998` — America's first person in space, returning at age `47` after inner-ear surgery; **Edgar D. Mitchell**; **Stuart A. Roosa**): Landed Lunar Module **`Antares`** in the hilly **Fra Mauro Highlands** (`Apollo 13`'s intended target, ejecta blanket from the `~3.9-billion-year-old` Imbrium Basin impact) on **5 February 1971** despite an abort-switch solder-ball short patched in real time by MIT programmer **Don Eyles** (`born 1944`) and late landing-radar lock at `~5,400 m`. Across **`2` EVAs (`9 hours, 22 minutes`)** using the two-wheeled **Modular Equipment Transporter (`MET` "rickshaw")**, they climbed within `~20–30 m` of the rim of `300-m` **Cone Crater**, returned **`42.28 kg` (`93.2 lb`)** of rocks (including **`Big Bertha` (`14321`)**, a `9-kg` breccia later found by `Bellucci et al. 2019`, *EPSL* `510`, `173–185` to contain a **`4.0–4.1-billion-year-old` felsite/zircon clast potentially launched from early Earth**), and Shepard hit two golf balls with a makeshift `6-iron` head attached to a contingency sample handle.
- **Landings #4 & #5 — The Electric-Rover J-Missions: `Apollo 15` (`30 July 1971`, `Hadley-Apennine`) & `Apollo 16` (`21 April 1972`, `Descartes Highlands`, NASA SP-289, SP-315 & SP-4029):**
  - **Apollo 15** (**David R. Scott**, **James B. Irwin**, **Alfred M. Worden**): First extended **J-mission** (`66 hours, 55 minutes` on the Moon, **`3` EVAs totaling `18 hours, 35 minutes`**). Deployed the first Boeing/GM **`210-kg` (`463-lb` Earth weight / `35.6-kg` lunar weight) folding electric Lunar Roving Vehicle (`LRV`)**, driving **`27.9 km` (`17.25 miles`)** between `4.5-km`-high **Mons Hadley Delta** and the `1.5-km`-wide, `300-m`-deep volcanic lava channel **Hadley Rille**. Discovered the **`4.1–4.5-billion-year-old` white anorthosite `Genesis Rock` (`Sample 15415`, `269.4 g`)** from the Moon's primordial magma-ocean crust, collected **`77.31 kg` (`170.4 lb`)** of samples, and demonstrated Galileo's equivalence principle live on television by dropping a **`1.32-kg` geological hammer and a `0.03-kg` falcon feather** in vacuum.
  - **Apollo 16** (**John W. Young**, **Charles M. Duke Jr.**, **Thomas K. Mattingly II**): Landed Lunar Module **`Orion`** in the rugged **Descartes Highlands** (`21 April 1972`, `71 hours, 2 minutes` on the Moon, **`3` EVAs totaling `20 hours, 14 minutes`**, driving **`26.7 km`**). Geologists expected volcanic domes, but Young and Duke proved the highlands were shattered **impact breccias** (`95.71 kg` / `211.0 lb` returned, including **`Big Muley` (`61016`)**, at **`11.72 kg`** the largest single rock brought back by Apollo) and deployed the Carruthers far-ultraviolet lunar observatory telescope.
- **Landing #6 — The Final Mission & Orange Glass: `Apollo 17` (`11 December 1972`, `Taurus-Littrow Valley`, NASA SP-330 & SP-4029; `Saal et al. 2008`, *Nature* `454`, `192–195`; `Hauri et al. 2011`, *Science* `333`, `213–215`):**
  - Crew: **Eugene A. Cernan** (`1934–2017`), **Ronald E. Evans** (`1933–1990`), and **Harrison H. "Jack" Schmitt** (`born 1935`) — the **first and only professional geologist (`PhD`, Harvard) to walk on the Moon**.
  - Only night launch of the Saturn V (`7 December 1972`). Lunar Module **`Challenger`** spent a record **`74 hours, 59 minutes, 40 seconds` (`~75 hours`)** in the **Taurus-Littrow valley** (`20.19° N, 30.77° E`), completing **`3` EVAs (`22 hours, 3 minutes, 57 seconds`)**, driving **`35.9 km` (`22.3 miles`)** up to **`7.6 km` from the lander** (setting the lunar speed record of **`~18 km/h` [`11.2 mph`]** downhill), and returning a record **`110.52 kg` (`243.6 lb`)** of rocks plus a `3-metre` deep drill core.
  - At **Station 4 (`Shorty Crater`)** on **12 December 1972**, Schmitt discovered bright **orange soil (`Sample 74220`)** above black vitrophyre glass beads: microscopic (`20–45 µm`) titanium-rich (`~8.9% TiO₂`) **pyroclastic volcanic glass beads erupted in a lunar fire fountain `~3.64 billion years ago`**. Decades later (`Saal et al. 2008`, *Nature*; `Hauri et al. 2011`, *Science*), ion-microprobe measurements of `74220` melt inclusions found **`up to 1,200–1,410 ppm` magmatic water**, proving the Moon's deep mantle holds as much water as Earth's upper mantle and was never bone-dry.
