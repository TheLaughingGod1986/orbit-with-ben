### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **Mission Overview & Fuel Depletion (`Spilker 2019`, *Science* `364`, `eaat3763`; NASA/JPL Cassini End of Mission Fact Sheet):**
  - Launched **15 October 1997**, entered Saturn orbit **1 July 2004 UTC** (`30 June` PDT), operated at Saturn for **13 years** (`294` orbits, `162` targeted moon flybys including `127` of Titan and `23` of Enceladus, `453,048` images, `635 GB` of science data, `$3.9 billion` total mission cost).
  - Carried **`3,132 kg` of bipropellant (`MMH` / `N₂O₄`) and monopropellant (`hydrazine`)** at launch (`5,712 kg` total launch mass) and **three `GPHS-RTGs` containing `32.7 kg` (`72 lb`) of plutonium-238 dioxide (`PuO₂`)**; by 2017, propellant tanks were nearly depleted (`<1%` remaining).
- **Planetary Protection Mandate — Protecting Enceladus & Titan (`Waite et al. 2017`, *Science* `356`, `155–159`; `Postberg et al. 2018`, *Nature* `558`, `564–568`; `Iess et al. 2012`, *Science* `337`, `457–459`):**
  - Cassini discovered that **Enceladus** (`504 km` diameter) hides a global subsurface liquid salt-water ocean venting plumes containing **water vapour, molecular hydrogen (`H₂`, `0.4–1.4%` by volume — a direct chemical energy source for methanogenic life, `Waite et al. 2017`), carbon dioxide (`0.3–0.8%`), methane (`0.1–0.3%`), nanometer silica grains (`Hsu et al. 2015`, requiring `≥90 °C` hydrothermal vents), and complex organic macromolecules (`>200 atomic mass units`, `Postberg et al. 2018`)**.
  - On **Titan** (`5,150 km` diameter), Cassini measured tidal flexing proving a global subsurface liquid water-ammonia ocean (`Iess et al. 2012`) beneath surface hydrocarbon seas (`Kraken Mare`, `Ligeia Mare`).
  - Because Cassini was assembled in the 1990s without ocean-world biological sterilization (`COSPAR` planetary protection rules) and carried `32.7 kg` of hot `PuO₂` capable of melting into an icy crust on impact, NASA HQ and the Cassini project (`Earl Maize`, `Linda Spilker`) chose to dispose of Cassini inside Saturn's atmosphere while its attitude thrusters still had fuel.
- **The 22 Grand Finale Proximal Dives (`22 April – 15 September 2017`):**
  - After its final close Titan flyby (`T-126` on **22 April 2017** at `979 km` altitude), Cassini entered `22` weekly orbits (`6.5-day` period) threading the **`~2,000-kilometre` (`~2,000–2,400 km`) gap** between Saturn's 1-bar cloud tops and the inner edge of the **D ring** at speeds of **`~122,000 to 124,000 km/h` (`~34 km/s` relative to the planet)**.
  - **Key scientific results from the 22 dives:**
    1. **Ring mass & young age (`Iess et al. 2019`, *Science* `364`, `eaat2965`):** Measured total ring mass at **`(1.54 ± 0.49) × 10¹⁹ kg`** (`~0.41 ×` Mimas), pointing to a ring age of **`10 to 100 million years`**.
    2. **Massive D-ring inflow (`Waite et al. 2018`, *Science* `362`, `eaat2382`):** `INMS` directly sampled **`4,800 to 45,000 kg/s` (`~10 tonnes/second`)** of water ice, methane, ammonia, carbon monoxide, molecular nitrogen, and organic compounds pouring from the D ring into Saturn's equator.
    3. **Zero-tilt magnetic field (`Dougherty et al. 2018`, *Science* `362`, `eaat5434`) & `10h 33m 38s` rotation (`Mankovich et al. 2019`, *ApJ* `871`, `1`):** Magnetic dipole tilt `<0.0095°` from the spin axis; ring-wave seismology pinned Saturn's bulk interior rotation period to **`10 hours, 33 minutes, 38 seconds`**.
    4. **Deep atmospheric jet streams (`Iess et al. 2019`):** Zonal winds extend **`~9,000 km` deep** into Saturn.
- **Final Atmospheric Entry (`15 September 2017`, Orbit 293, NASA/JPL End of Mission Timeline):**
