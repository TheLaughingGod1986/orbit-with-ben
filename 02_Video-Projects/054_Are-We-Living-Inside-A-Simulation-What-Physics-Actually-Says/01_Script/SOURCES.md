## 3. Silas R. Beane, Zohreh Davoudi, and Martin J. Savage (2012 / 2014) — Lattice QCD & the Cosmic-Ray Grid Test
- **Source:** Silas R. Beane, Zohreh Davoudi, and Martin J. Savage, *"Constraints on the Universe as a Numerical Simulation"*, *The European Physical Journal A*, Vol. 50, Article 148 (2014); preprint submitted October 2012 (`arXiv:1210.1847`).
- **URLs:**
  - `https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1847`
  - `https://arxiv.org/html/1210.1847v2`
- **Role / Bio verification (`WHO`):** Silas Beane (Nuclear physicist), Zohreh Davoudi (Theoretical physicist), Martin Savage (Nuclear physicist) — University of Washington & University of New Hampshire lattice QCD collaboration (2012).
- **Status:** `MUST QUOTE` — Verified live (`2026-10-19`).
- **Verbatim Quotes:**
  1. **Cubic space-time lattice hypothesis (`WHEN: 2012` / `DATA`):**
     > `"Observable consequences of the hypothesis that the observed universe is a numerical simulation performed on a cubic space-time lattice or grid are explored."`
  2. **Current human lattice QCD simulations at femtometre scales ($1\text{ fm} = 10^{-15}\text{ m}$) (`DATA` / `SCALE`):**
     > `"These simulations are currently performed in femto-sized universes where the space-time continuum is replaced by a lattice, whose spatial and temporal sizes are of the order of several femto-meters or fermis (1 fm = 10^-15 m), and whose lattice spacings (discretization or pixelation) are fractions of fermis"`
  3. **Greisen–Zatsepin–Kuzmin (GZK) cosmic-ray cutoff scale ($\sim 6 \times 10^{20}\text{ eV}$, or $10^{11}\text{ GeV}$) (`DATA`):**
     > `"Processes such as \gamma_CMB + N -> \Delta give rise to the predicted GKZ-cut off scale [50, 51] of ~ 6 x 10^20 eV in the spectrum of high energy cosmic rays."`
  4. **Lattice spacing constraint ($b \lesssim 10^{-12}\text{ fm} = 10^{-27}\text{ m}$) and cubic angular symmetry (`DATA` / `FACT`):**
     > `"Equating this to the GKZ cut off corresponds to a lattice spacing of b ~ 10^-12 fm, or a mass scale of b^-1 ~ 10^11 GeV. Therefore, the lattice spacing used in the lattice simulation of the universe must be b <~ 10^-12 fm in order for the GZK cut off to be present or for the lattice spacing itself to provide the cut off in the cosmic ray spectrum. The most striking feature of the scenario in which the lattice provides the cut off to the cosmic ray spectrum is that the angular distribution of the highest energy components would exhibit cubic symmetry in the rest frame of the lattice, deviating significantly from isotropy."`
