### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **First-Generation Soviet Space Stations — `Salyut 1` (`DOS-1`) & Military `Almaz` (`Salyut 2/3/5`, `1971–1977`; NASA SP-4225 *The Partnership* / *Mir Hardware Heritage* `Portree 1995`, NASA RP-1357; `Siddiqi 2000` *Challenge to Apollo*):**
  - Launched on a Proton-K rocket from Baikonur on **19 April 1971**, **`Salyut 1` (`DOS-1`)** was the world's first space station (`15.8 metres` long, `4.15 m` maximum diameter, **`18,425 kg` [`18.4 tonnes`]** mass, **`~99 m³`** habitable volume). After `Soyuz 10` (`April 1971`) soft-docked but could not open the hatch, **`Soyuz 11`** cosmonauts **Georgi T. Dobrovolski** (`1928–1971`), **Vladislav N. Volkov** (`1935–1971`), and **Viktor I. Patsayev** (`1933–1971`) boarded `Salyut 1` on **7 June 1971** and lived aboard for **`23 days, 18 hours`** (`operating the Orion-1 ultraviolet space observatory`). On **30 June 1971**, during re-entry pyrotechnic separation at **`~168 km` altitude**, a cabin pressure-equalisation valve jolted open prematurely, depressurising the `Soyuz 11` descent module in `~112 seconds` and leading to the mandate that cosmonauts wear `Sokol` pressure suits during launch, docking, and re-entry (`Salyut 1` was deorbited after `175 days` on **11 October 1971**).
  - Under chief designer **Vladimir N. Chelomei** (`1914–1984`), the USSR also launched three military **`Almaz` (`OPS`)** reconnaissance stations disguised under the `Salyut` name: **`Salyut 2` (`OPS-1`, `April 1973`, depressurised after 13 days)**, **`Salyut 3` (`OPS-2`, `June 1974 – January 1975`, crewed by `Soyuz 14` Pavel Popovich & Yuri Artyukhin)**, and **`Salyut 5` (`OPS-3`, `June 1976 – August 1977`, crewed by `Soyuz 21` & `24`)**. Equipped with the `Agat-1` optical spy telescope (`6.375-m` focal length) and film-return capsules, **`Salyut 3` carried a fixed `23-mm` Rikhter R-23M aircraft cannon (`1,000–2,600 rounds/min`)** mounted on the forward belly, which ground controllers test-fired remotely by rotating the entire station on **24 January 1975** just before deorbit (`the only firearm ever fired in space`). Civilian **`Salyut 4` (`DOS-4`, `December 1974 – February 1977`)** tested articulated solar arrays and hosted `Soyuz 17` (`30 days`) and `Soyuz 18` (`63 days`).
- **America's First Space Station — `Skylab` (`14 May 1973 – 11 July 1979`; NASA SP-400 *Skylab, Our First Space Station* `1977`; NASA SP-4208 *Living and Working in Space* `Compton & Benson 1983`):**
  - Launched atop the final **Saturn V (`SA-513` / ` Saturn INT-21`)** on **14 May 1973**, **`Skylab`** converted a Saturn V `S-IVB` liquid-hydrogen third stage into a two-level orbital workshop (**`77,088 kg` [`77.1 tonnes`]** total mass with the Apollo Telescope Mount and CSM, **`351.6 m³` [`12,415 ft³`]** total habitable volume, `36.1 metres` long, `6.6 m` diameter).
  - At **`63 seconds` after liftoff** (`Max Q`), atmospheric drag tore away `Skylab`'s micrometeoroid and thermal shield, ripping off **Solar Array Wing No. 2** completely and pinning **Solar Array Wing No. 1** shut with an aluminum strap. Deprived of electrical power and thermal shielding, `Skylab`'s internal workshop temperature climbed to **`52–54 °C` (`126–130 °F`)** (`external skin reaching ~160 °C / 320 °F`).
  - In a `10-day` crash engineering effort led by NASA Marshall engineer **Jack A. Kinzler** (`1920–2014`, `"Mr. Fix-It"`), NASA built a collapsible **`6.7 × 7.3 m` (`22 × 24 ft`) aluminized-Mylar and nylon sun parasol**. Launched on **`Skylab 2` (`SL-2`, `25 May 1973`)**, commander **Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.** (`1930–1999`), **Paul J. Weitz** (`1932–2017`), and physician-astronaut **Joseph P. Kerwin** (`born 1932`) pushed the parasol through a `20-cm` (`8-inch`) scientific airlock on **26 May 1973**, dropping cabin temperatures to `~24 °C` (`75 °F`), and on **7 June 1973** Conrad and Kerwin performed a dramatic **`3-hour, 25-minute` spacewalk** using a `7.6-metre` (`25-ft`) long-handled cable cutter to snip the metal debris strap and pop Solar Wing No. 1 open. Across `Skylab 2` (`28 days`), `Skylab 3` (`59 days`), and `Skylab 4` (`84 days`, **Gerald Carr**, **Edward Gibson**, **William Pogue**), astronauts lived aboard for **`171 days`** before high solar activity expanded Earth's upper atmosphere and dragged `Skylab` down over the Indian Ocean and **Esperance, Western Australia on 11 July 1979**.
- **Second-Generation Dual-Port Stations & the 1985 Rescue — `Salyut 6` (`1977–1982`) & `Salyut 7` (`1982–1991`; `Portree 1995` NASA RP-1357):**
  - **`Salyut 6` (`DOS-5`, `29 September 1977 – 29 July 1982`, `19.8 tonnes`)** introduced a **second axial docking port** on the aft transfer compartment, allowing uncrewed **`Progress` resupply freighters** (`Progress 1` docked `22 January 1978`) to refuel the station's hypergolic propellants in orbit and visiting `Interkosmos` guest crews to swap aging `Soyuz` ferry craft (`extending stays to 185 days`).
  - **`Salyut 7` (`DOS-6`, `19 April 1982 – 7 February 1991`, `19.8 tonnes`)** saw **Svetlana Y. Savitskaya** (`born 1948`) perform the **first spacewalk by a woman** (`25 July 1984`, `3 hours 35 minutes`, testing an electron-beam hand welder) and a `237-day` endurance record (`Soyuz T-10B`). On **11 February 1985**, while `Salyut 7` was uncrewed, a current spike tripped the primary radio transmitter and drained the station's batteries completely; `Salyut 7` went dark, drifted uncontrolled, and froze inside to **`-10 °C` (`14 °F`)**. On **6 June 1985**, **Vladimir A. Dzhanibekov** (`born 1942`) and flight engineer **Viktor P. Savinykh** (`born 1940`) launched on **`Soyuz T-13`**, used a handheld laser rangefinder on **8 June 1985** to manually match the roll of the dead station and hard-dock at `1.5 m/s`, worked in winter fur coats to bypass the failed charge relay (`10–16 June 1985`), thawed `90 litres` of frozen water, and restored `Salyut 7` to full service.
- **The First Modular Space Station — `Mir` (`20 February 1986 – 23 March 2001`, `129.7 tonnes`; `Harland 2005` *The Story of Space Station Mir*; `Portree 1995`):**
  - Launched beginning with the **`20.4-tonne` `Mir` Core Module (`DOS-7`) on 20 February 1986**, the Soviet Union / Russia assembled **`7` pressurized modules** in orbit over `10 years` using the `Lyappa` pivoting robotic arm: **`Kvant-1`** (`1987`, astrophysics & `Gyrodin` control-moment gyroscopes), **`Kvant-2`** (`1989`, airlock), **`Kristall`** (`1990`, materials & docking node), **`Spektr`** (`1995`, NASA science), the **`Docking Module`** (`1995`, delivered by Space Shuttle `Atlantis` on `STS-74`), and **`Priroda`** (`1996`, Earth remote sensing), reaching **`~129.7 tonnes` (`~350 m³` habitable volume)**.
  - Physician-cosmonaut **Valeri V. Polyakov** (`1942–2022`) lived aboard `Mir` from **8 January 1994 to 22 March 1995** for **`437 days, 17 hours, 58 minutes`** (`7,075` orbits, `~300 million km`) — still the **longest single continuous spaceflight by a human in history**.
  - During the `1995–1998` **Shuttle–`Mir` Program** (`9` Space Shuttle dockings, `7` US astronauts including **Shannon Lucid**, **Jerry Linnehan**, **John Blaha**, and **Michael Foale**), `Mir` survived two near-fatal emergencies in **1997**: on **23 February 1997**, a lithium-perchlorate **`Vika` (`TGK`) solid-fuel oxygen canister** erupted into a **`14-minute` blowtorch fire** inside `Kvant-1`; and on **25 June 1997**, during a manual `TORU` teleoperated docking test, the **`7.2-tonne` `Progress M-34` cargo ship** slammed into the `Spektr` solar array and radiator hull, depressurising `Spektr` and forcing the crew (**Vasily Tsibliyev**, **Aleksandr Lazutkin**, and **Michael Foale**) to slice power cables and seal the `Spektr` hatch in seconds. `Mir` was deorbited smoothly into the South Pacific after **`15 years` (`5,511 days` in orbit, `3,644 days` continuous occupation)** on **23 March 2001**.
- **The `420-Tonne` International Space Station (`ISS`, `1998–Present`) & China's Modular `Tiangong` (`2011–Present`; NASA *ISS Reference Guide* NP-2015-05-022-JSC; CMSA `Tiangong` Technical Briefs `2021–2023`):**
  - Built by **`5` space agencies representing `15` countries** (`NASA`, `Roscosmos`, `ESA`, `JAXA`, `CSA`), the **International Space Station** began with the Proton launch of the Russian **`Zarya` (`FGB`) module on 20 November 1998** and Space Shuttle `Endeavour` (`STS-88`) mating the US **`Unity` (`Node 1`) module on 6 December 1998**. Across `37` Space Shuttle assembly/logistics flights and `5` Russian Proton/Soyuz module launches (`Zvezda`, `Destiny`, `Quest`, `Pirs`, `Harmony`, `Columbus`, `Kibo`, `Poisk`, `Tranquility`, `Cupola`, `Rassvet`, `Leonardo`, `BEAM`, `Nauka`, `Prichal`), the `ISS` grew to **`16` pressurized modules**, a **`109 × 73 metre` (`356 × 240 ft`) footprint** (`the size of an American football field including end zones`), **`~419,725 kg` (`~420 tonnes`) mass**, and **`~916–1,005 m³` pressurized volume (`~388 m³` habitable)**. Since **`Expedition 1`** (**William Shepherd** [`born 1949`], **Yuri Gidzenko** [`born 1962`], **Sergei Krikalev** [`born 1958`]) docked on **2 November 2000**, humanity has maintained **over `26` consecutive years of uninterrupted human presence in space**.
  - Meanwhile, the **China Manned Space Agency (`CMSA`)** launched prototype single-module stations **`Tiangong-1` (`29 September 2011 – 2 April 2018`, `8.5 tonnes`, visited by `Shenzhou 9` & `10` including China's first female astronaut **Liu Yang** [`born 1978`])** and **`Tiangong-2` (`15 September 2016 – 19 July 2019`, `8.6 tonnes`, `Shenzhou 11` `30-day` stay)**, before assembling the permanent **3-module `Tiangong` Space Station** in **`18 months` (`April 2021 – November 2022`)**: the **`22.5-tonne` `Tianhe` Core Module (`29 April 2021`)**, the **`23-tonne` `Wentian` lab (`24 July 2022`)**, and the **`23-tonne` `Mengtian` lab (`31 October 2022`)**, forming a **`~66–100-tonne` (with docked `Shenzhou` crew & `Tianzhou` cargo craft), `~340 m³` T-shaped orbital laboratory** continuously crewed since **`Shenzhou 14` (`June 2022`)**.
