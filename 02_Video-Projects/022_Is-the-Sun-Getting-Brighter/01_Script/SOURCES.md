# Sources — Is the Sun Getting Brighter?

Spoken claims in `sun_brighter_script_master_v01.md`, for the description. Not narration.

| Claim in the film | Source |
|---|---|
| About 1% brighter every 110 million years, as the Sun uses its hydrogen | Schröder, K.-P. & Connon Smith, R. (2008). Distant future of the Sun and Earth revisited. *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society*, 386, 155–163. The paper’s line is “1 per cent in every 110 million years.” |
| Sunspot-cycle change in total brightness is about 0.1%, and it reverses | The solar cycle modulates total solar irradiance by about 0.1% (order of 1 W/m² on about 1361 W/m²). Standard satellite record (SORCE/TIM and successors). This is not the secular climb. |
| The secular climb is not the warming of the last century | At 1% per 110 million years, a human lifetime and a century are a negligible fraction of that pace, far below the 0.1% sunspot wobble. The film does not attribute recent warming to solar brightening. |
| Early Sun about 70% of today’s light (about 30% fainter) | The faint-young-Sun result from standard solar models. Gough, D. O. (1981). Solar interior structure and luminosity variations. *Solar Physics*, 74, 21–34, is the classic statement. The present 1%-per-110-million-year pace is not a flat rate to multiply backwards. |
| Liquid water on early Earth, with greenhouse gases making up the difference | The faint-young-Sun paradox: geological evidence of liquid water under a dimmer Sun, usually resolved with a stronger greenhouse (carbon dioxide, and methane early on). |
| Near a billion years, about 10% brighter, moist greenhouse | Arithmetic of the 2008 pace: 10% is about 1.1 billion years. Schröder & Connon Smith (2008) place Earth’s moist-greenhouse limit near 1 Gyr, around a Sun of order 10% more luminous, with oceans able to lose water to space. |
| This film stops before the red-giant Sun | The same 2008 paper follows the Sun off the main sequence. That ending is a different film and is not told here. |
| Sunlight travel time of eight minutes | Light-travel time from the Sun to Earth at 1 astronomical unit. |
| A prominence is magnetic weather, not the brightening | Prominences are plasma suspended in magnetic field above the limb, on timescales of hours to days. |
