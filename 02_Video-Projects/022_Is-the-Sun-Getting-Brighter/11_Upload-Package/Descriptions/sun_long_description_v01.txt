The Sun is getting brighter, about 1% every 110 million years. And it brightens because it is running out of fuel.

The Sun turns hydrogen into helium in its core. Helium packs more mass into fewer particles, so as the ash builds up the core loses pressure, squeezes in, heats up and fuses faster. More fusion means more light. That is why the young Sun was about 30% dimmer, why early Earth still had oceans (a thicker greenhouse made up the difference), and why, about a billion years from now, a Sun some 10% brighter starts to push Earth's oceans towards a moist greenhouse. Sunspots and prominences are a different clock: the 11-year cycle moves the total light by only about 0.1%, up and down.
