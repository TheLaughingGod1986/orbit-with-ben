**Chapter list after audit** (6 acts, each with a teach-point):

1. **The 420-Tonne Football Field Flying Overhead at 28,000 km/h** — The scale of the International Space Station (`419,725–430,000 kg`, `109 m` / `356 ft` end-to-end, `94 m` truss, `73 m` solar arrays, `67 m` module axis, orbiting at `~400–415 km` / `257 miles` at `5 miles/s` / `28,000 km/h`, `16 orbits/day` over `90%` of Earth's population).
2. **Why the Space Station Can't Stay Up There — Or Be Pushed Higher** — Why thin thermospheric air at `415 km` would drag the station down in `1–2 years` without reboosts, how docking loads and thermal cycling limit its primary structure, and why boosting it to an `800 km` storage orbit is ruled out (`<4 years` mean time between catastrophic debris impacts vs `~51 years` at `415 km`; `120–760 m/s` delta-V vs `57 m/s` for deorbit).
3. **NASA's Baseline 2030–2031 Plan and SpaceX's $843M Deorbit Tug** — Operations committed through `2030` (`2028` for Roscosmos), the `2027` GAO assessment on whether to launch the U.S. Deorbit Vehicle in mid-`2029` or extend operations, and how SpaceX's modified Dragon USDV provides the final `~57 m/s` burn to meet the `1-in-10,000` public safety standard.
4. **The Spacecraft Cemetery: Why Engineers Aim for the South Pacific Around Point Nemo** — How operators align the elongated debris footprint over the **South Pacific Oceanic Uninhabited Area (`SPOUA`)** around **Point Nemo** (`48°52.6′S, 123°23.6′W`), `2,688 km` (`1,670 miles`) from Ducie Island, Motu Nui, and Maher Island, Antarctica.
5. **What Skylab (1979) and Mir (2001) Taught Us About Dropping a Space Station** — How solar expansion dragged `Skylab` down on `11 July 1979` with pieces reaching Western Australia, and how Russia's `143-ton` `Mir` proved controlled three-stage South Pacific deorbit on `23 March 2001` (`20–25 tons` splashing down east of New Zealand).
6. **Three Stages of Breakup: What Vaporizes and What Reaches the Ocean Floor** — NASA's 3-stage re-entry sequence (`solar arrays/radiators` first at `~95 km`, `intact modules/truss` second at `~85 km`, `module skin melting & fragmentation` third), why most hardware vaporizes, and why dense truss sections settle harmlessly on the deep Pacific seabed.
