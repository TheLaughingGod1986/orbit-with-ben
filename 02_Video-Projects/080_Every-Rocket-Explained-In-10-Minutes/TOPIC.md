### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **The Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation & the First Rockets into Space (`Tsiolkovsky 1903`; NASA SP-4206 *Stages to Saturn* `Bilstein 1980`; `Siddiqi 2000` *Challenge to Apollo*):**
  - Derived by Russian schoolteacher **Konstantin E. Tsiolkovsky** (`1857–1935`) in **May 1903** (*Exploration of Outer Space by Means of Rocket Devices*, *Nauchnoye Obozreniye*), the ideal rocket equation $\Delta v = I_{\text{sp}} g_0 \ln(m_0 / m_f)$ governs every launch vehicle: because achieving low Earth orbit (`v ≈ 7.8 km/s` / `28,000 km/h`) requires **`\Delta v \approx 9.3–9.5 km/s`** (including gravity and atmospheric drag losses) while chemical bipropellants achieve exhaust velocities of only **`~3.0–4.5 km/s`** (`I_sp ≈ 300–455 s`), a rocket must shed empty propellant tanks via **staging** (`invented independently by Tsiolkovsky, Robert Goddard, Hermann Oberth [1894–1989], and Mikhail Tikhonravov [1900–1974]`).
  - American physicist **Robert H. Goddard** (`1882–1945`) launched the **world's first liquid-propellant rocket** (burning gasoline and liquid oxygen) on **16 March 1926** in Auburn, Massachusetts (`2.5-second` flight, `12.5 metres` [`41 ft`] altitude).
  - On **20 June 1944**, a German **`A-4` / `V-2`** ballistic rocket (`serial number MW 18014`, `14.0 m` tall, `12.5 tonnes` liftoff mass, `265 kN` ethanol/LOX engine designed under **Wernher von Braun** [`1912–1977`]) launched vertically from Peenemünde to **`176 km` (`109 miles`) altitude**, becoming the **first human-made object to cross the `100-km` Kármán line into space**.
  - In the Soviet Union, Chief Designer **Sergei P. Korolev** (`1907–1966`, `OKB-1`) and engine designer **Valentin P. Glushko** (`1908–1989`, `OKB-456`) developed the **`R-7 Semyorka` (`8K71`)** (`34 m` tall, `280 tonnes`, clustered around `4` conical strap-on boosters and a central core burning RP-1 kerosene and LOX in `RD-107`/`RD-108` four-chamber engines — `20` main thrust chambers plus `12` vernier steering chambers producing `~3.9 MN` liftoff thrust). Modified as the **`Sputnik` (`8K71PS`)** rocket, it launched **`Sputnik 1` (`83.6 kg`) on 4 October 1957**, and with an upper stage as **`Vostok-K` (`8K72K`)** it launched **Yuri A. Gagarin (`1934–1968`) into orbit on 12 April 1961**. America launched **Alan B. Shepard Jr.** (`1923–1998`) suborbitally on a **`Mercury-Redstone` (`347 kN`, `5 May 1961`)**, **John H. Glenn Jr.** (`1921–2016`) into orbit on **Karel J. "Charlie" Bossart**'s (`1904–1975`) pressure-stabilized stainless-steel balloon-tank **`Atlas LV-3B` (`20 February 1962`, `1.6 MN`)**, and `10` crewed **`Gemini`** missions (`1965–1966`) on the hypergolic two-stage **`Titan II GLV` (`1.9 MN`)**.
- **The Moon Giants — NASA's `Saturn V` (`1967–1973`) vs the Soviet `N1` (`1969–1972`; NASA SP-4206 *Stages to Saturn* `Bilstein 1980`; NASA *Saturn V Flight Manual* SA-507; `Siddiqi 2000`):**
  - Standing **`110.6 metres` (`363 feet`) tall** (`10.1 m` / `33 ft` diameter) with a liftoff mass of **`~2,970 tonnes` (`6.54 million lb`)**, NASA's **`Saturn V`** (`developed at Marshall Space Flight Center under Wernher von Braun and program manager Arthur Rudolph [1906–1996]`) remains the tallest and heaviest operational rocket to fly humans beyond low Earth orbit (`13` launches from `Apollo 4` on **9 November 1967** to `Skylab 1` on **14 May 1973**, `13-for-13` reaching orbit).
  - **Three stages of `Saturn V`:**
    1. **`S-IC` First Stage (`Boeing`, `42.1 m` tall):** Powered by **five Rocketdyne `F-1` single-chamber gas-generator engines** burning RP-1 kerosene and liquid oxygen (`LOX`). Each `F-1` generated **`6,770 kN` (`1,522,000 lbf`)** of sea-level thrust (`uprated to 34.5 MN / 7.6–7.75 million lbf combined liftoff thrust on Apollo 15–17`). Its `55,000-horsepower` (`41 MW`) turbopump fed **`2,578 kg/s` (`5,683 lb/s`)** of propellant per engine — so **all five `F-1` engines burned `12,890–13,500 kg` (`~13 metric tonnes` / `~15 US short tons`) of propellant every single second** across `168 seconds` (`consuming ~2,077 tonnes of RP-1 and LOX to reach 67 km altitude and 2.39 km/s`).
    2. **`S-II` Second Stage (`North American`, `24.9 m` tall):** Powered by **five Rocketdyne `J-2` cryogenic liquid-hydrogen/LOX engines** (`1,023 kN` / `230,000 lbf` each; `I_sp = 421 s` in vacuum), burning for `~360 seconds` to `185 km` altitude (`~6.99 km/s`).
    3. **`S-IVB` Third Stage (`Douglas`, `17.9 m` tall):** Powered by **one restartable `J-2` engine**, inserting **`~140 tonnes` (`310,000 lb`) into low Earth orbit** and then reigniting for **Trans-Lunar Injection (`TLI`)** to send **`~48.6 tonnes` (`107,100 lb`) to the Moon** (`10.8 km/s`).
  - By contrast, the Soviet **`N1` (`11A52`) Moon rocket** (`105.3 m` tall, `2,735 tonnes` liftoff mass, `~95 tonnes` LEO payload target), begun under **Sergei Korolev** and continued after his January 1966 death by **Vasily P. Mishin** (`1917–2001`), lacked large high-thrust kerolox chambers after Glushko refused to build them. Instead, engine designer **Nikolai D. Kuznetsov** (`1911–1995`) provided smaller **`NK-15` (`1,544 kN` each)** oxygen-rich staged-combustion engines — forcing `OKB-1` to pack **30 `NK-15` engines** into the `Block A` first stage (`producing 45.4 MN / 10.2 million lbf liftoff thrust`). Acoustic shockwaves, fuel-line water-hammer surges, and the **`KORD` automatic engine-control system** doomed **all four uncrewed `N1` test flights**:
    - **`N1 3L` (`21 February 1969`):** Fire shut down the stage at `T+68.7 s`.
    - **`N1 5L` (`3 July 1969`, `17 days` before `Apollo 11`):** A loose metal fragment shattered a liquid-oxygen turbopump milliseconds before liftoff; `KORD` shut down `29 of 30` engines at `200 metres` altitude, dropping **`~2,300 tonnes` of RP-1 and LOX back onto Site 110 at Baikonur (`~4–5 kilotons TNT equivalent`, the largest non-nuclear explosion in spaceflight history)**.
    - **`N1 6L` (`26 June 1971`):** Uncontrolled roll tore the rocket apart at `T+50.2 s`.
    - **`N1 7L` (`23 November 1972`):** Pogo oscillation ruptured fuel lines at `T+106.9 s` (`40 km` altitude, `7 seconds` before Stage 1 separation), before the Soviet lunar program was cancelled in **May 1974** (`though Kuznetsov's upgraded NK-33 engines survived in storage for decades`).
- **Workhorses & the Reusability Revolution — `Soyuz`, `Ariane 5`, `SpaceX Falcon 9` (`2015`) & `Falcon Heavy` (`2018`):**
  - The **`Soyuz` launch vehicle family** (`direct descendant of Korolev's 1957 R-7`) has completed **`>1,900` orbital launches** (`1966–Present`), while **ESA's `Ariane 5`** (`1996–2023`, `117` launches, `777 tonnes`, `Vulcain 2` hydrolox core + `2` `EAP` solid boosters) launched the **James Webb Space Telescope on 25 December 2021** with such trajectory accuracy that `JWST`'s onboard hydrazine propellant lasts **`>20 years`** (`double its 10-year minimum baseline`).
  - Founded in **2002** by **Elon R. Musk** (`born 1971`) with propulsion chief **Thomas J. Mueller** (`born 1961`), **SpaceX** developed the **9-engine `Merlin 1D` `Falcon 9` (`70 m` tall, `549 tonnes`, `7,607 kN` liftoff thrust, `~22.8 tonnes` expendable / `~17.5 tonnes` reusable to LEO)**. On **21 December 2015** (`Falcon 9 Flight 20` / `Orbcomm-2`), booster **`B1019`** used hypersonic titanium/aluminum grid fins, cold-gas nitrogen thrusters, and three relit `Merlin 1D` burns to touch down vertically at **Cape Canaveral Landing Zone 1** — the **first orbital-class rocket stage in history to land intact after delivering a payload toward orbit**, followed on **8 April 2016** (`CRS-8`, booster `B1021`) by the first autonomous drone-ship landing at sea (*Of Course I Still Love You*). Individual `Falcon 9` boosters have since flown **`20+` times each**, and the **27-engine `Falcon Heavy` (`22.8 MN` liftoff thrust, `63.8 tonnes` to LEO)** debuted on **6 February 2018** with a synchronized double side-booster landing at Cape Canaveral.
- **Super-Heavy Rockets Today — `NASA SLS` (`2022`) & `SpaceX Super Heavy Starship` (`2023–Present`):**
  - On **16 November 2022**, **NASA's `98-metre` (`322-ft`) Space Launch System (`SLS` Block 1)** launched **`Artemis I`** (`uncrewed Orion spacecraft around the Moon`), generating **`39.1 MN` (`8.8 million lbf`) of liftoff thrust** (`15% more than Saturn V`) from `4` Aerojet Rocketdyne `RS-25D` liquid-hydrogen engines and two `5-segment` Northrop Grumman solid rocket boosters (`capable of 95 tonnes to LEO and 27 tonnes to trans-lunar injection`).
  - Standing **`121 metres` (`397 feet`) tall** (`9 metres` diameter, **`~5,000 tonnes`** liftoff mass), **SpaceX's `Starship` and `Super Heavy`** is the tallest and most powerful rocket ever flown: its `71-metre` **`Super Heavy` booster** clusters **33 full-flow staged-combustion liquid-methane/LOX `Raptor` engines** generating **`~74 MN` (`~16.7 million lbf`) of liftoff thrust** (`more than double Saturn V`), topped by the `50-metre`, `6-Raptor` `Starship` spacecraft (`100–150 tonnes` reusable payload to LEO). On **13 October 2024 (`Integrated Flight Test 5` / `IFT-5`)**, `Super Heavy` **`Booster 12`** flew to space, returned to Boca Chica (`Starbase`), Texas, and was **caught mid-air by the two mechanical "chopstick" arms (`Mechazilla`) of the launch tower** `6 minutes, 54 seconds` after liftoff.
