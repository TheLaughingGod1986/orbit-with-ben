### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **The Nebular Hypothesis & `4.567-Billion-Year-Old` Meteorite Time Zero (`Swedenborg 1734`; `Kant 1755`; `Laplace 1796`; `Lee, Papanastassiou & Wasserburg 1976`; `Cameron & Truran 1977`; `Amelin et al. 2002`; `Connelly et al. 2012`):**
  - First sketched by **Emanuel Swedenborg** (`1688–1772`, *Principia Rerum Naturalium*, `1734`), developed by **Immanuel Kant** (`1724–1804`, *Allgemeine Naturgeschichte und Theorie des Himmels*, `1755`), and formulated mathematically by **Pierre-Simon Laplace** (`1749–1827`, *Exposition du système du monde*, `1796`), the **Nebular Hypothesis** explained why all eight major planets orbit the Sun in the same prograde direction close to the flat ecliptic plane (`inclinations ≤7°`, `≤3.4°` excluding Mercury): they condensed from a single rotating, flattening disk of gas and dust (**the solar nebula**).
  - On **8 February 1969**, over `2 metric tonnes` of the **`Allende` carbonaceous chondrite (`CV3`) meteorite** fell over Chihuahua, Mexico, packed with white, millimetre-sized **Calcium-Aluminium-Rich Inclusions (`CAIs`)** — refractory mineral grains (`hibonite, melilite, spinel, perovskite`) that condensed at **`>1,300 to 1,700 K`** as the very first solid matter in the Solar System, alongside spherical silicate **chondrules** (`flash-melted at ~1,500–1,900 K`).
  - High-precision lead-lead (`²⁰⁷Pb–²⁰⁶Pb`) dating of `CAIs` from the `Efremovka` and `Allende` meteorites by **Yuri Amelin** (`born 1957`), **Alexander N. Krot** (`born 1959`), **Ian D. Hutcheon** (`1947–2015`), and **Alexander A. Ulyanov** (`Amelin et al. 2002`, *Science* `297`, `1678–1683`, dating `CAIs` to `4,567.2 ± 0.6 Myr`) and uranium-isotope-corrected (`²³⁸U/²³⁵U`) `Pb–Pb` dating by **James N. Connelly** (`born 1962`), **Martin Bizzarro** (`born 1970`), **Alexander N. Krot**, **Åke Nordlund** (`born 1947`), **Daniel Wielandt**, and **Marina A. Ivanova** (`Connelly et al. 2012`, *Science* `338`, `651–655`) established the Solar System's exact **`Time Zero` (`t = 0`) at `4,567.30 ± 0.16 million years ago` (`4.567 billion years ago`)**, showing `CAIs` condensed within a brief `~160,000-year` window while chondrules formed over the next `~3 million years`.
  - In **1976**, **Typhoon Lee** (`1948–2023`), **Dimitri A. Papanastassiou** (`born 1942`), and **Gerald J. Wasserburg** (`1927–2016`, `Lee, Papanastassiou & Wasserburg 1976`, *Geophysical Research Letters* `3`, `109–112`) discovered a large radiogenic excess of **`Magnesium-26` (`²⁶Mg`)** correlated with `Al/Mg` ratios inside `Allende` `CAIs`, proving that live **`Aluminium-26` (`²⁶Al`, half-life `717,000 years`, canonical initial ratio `(²⁶Al/²⁷Al)_0 ≈ 5.2 × 10⁻⁵`)** was injected into the presolar cloud right as it collapsed. In **1977**, **Alastair G. W. Cameron** (`1925–2005`) and **James W. Truran** (`1940–2022`, `Cameron & Truran 1977`, *Icarus* `30`, `447–461`) proposed the **Supernova Trigger Hypothesis**: a shockwave from a nearby core-collapse supernova (`or massive Wolf-Rayet stellar wind`) compressed the cold (`10–20 K`) presolar molecular cloud past its **Jeans instability limit** (`Sir James Jeans 1902`) and seeded it with `²⁶Al` and `⁶⁰Fe`, whose radioactive decay heated early `>20-km` planetesimals enough to melt their interiors and differentiate iron cores within `<1–2 million years`!
- **`99.86%` Into the Sun, `ALMA`'s `HL Tauri` Protoplanetary Disk & the `~2.7 AU` Frost Line (`Hayashi 1981`; `ALMA Partnership 2015`):**
  - As the rotating presolar cloud (`~10,000 AU` across) collapsed by a factor of `~100`, conservation of angular momentum ($L = m v r$) spun it into a flat, `~100–200 AU` circumstellar **protoplanetary disk** surrounding the central protostar. **`99.86%` of the Solar System's total mass (`1.9885 × 10³⁰ kg`)** fell into the young **Sun**, which passed through a violent, fast-rotating **`T Tauri` phase** (`named after variable star T Tauri, discovered in 1852 by John Russell Hind [1823–1895] and identified as pre-main-sequence stars in 1945 by Alfred Harrison Joy [1882–1973]`, *ApJ* `102`, `168`) before its core reached `~15 million K` and ignited sustained proton-proton hydrogen fusion. Of the remaining **`0.14%` (`~446.6 Earth masses`)**, **`Jupiter` (`317.8 M_⊕`, `71%` of planetary mass)**, **`Saturn` (`95.2 M_⊕`)**, **`Neptune` (`17.1 M_⊕`)**, and **`Uranus` (`14.5 M_⊕`)** hold **`~99.5%`**, leaving all four rocky inner planets (`Earth 1.0 M_⊕`, `Venus 0.815 M_⊕`, `Mars 0.107 M_⊕`, `Mercury 0.055 M_⊕`) with `<2 Earth masses` (`~0.0006%` of the Solar System's total mass)!
  - In **November 2014** (`published July 2015`, **ALMA Partnership**, **Crystal L. Brogan** [`born 1972`], *et al.*, *ApJL* `808`, `L3`), the **`ALMA` (`Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array`)** interferometer at `5,000 metres` on Chile's Chajnantor plateau imaged the `~1-million-year-old` solar-mass T Tauri star **`HL Tauri`** (`450 light-years` away in Taurus) at `0.025-arcsecond` (`3.5 AU`) resolution, revealing sharp concentric dark gaps carved by forming protoplanets inside a dusty protoplanetary disk — direct visual proof of planet formation in action.
  - **The Frost Line (`Snow Line`, `Chushiro Hayashi 1981`, *Prog. Theor. Phys. Suppl.* `70`, `35–53`):** In the Minimum-Mass Solar Nebula (`MMSN`) model of Japanese astrophysicist **Chushiro Hayashi** (`1920–2010`), disk temperature falls with distance from the young Sun ($T(r) \approx 280\,(r/\text{1 AU})^{-1/2}\text{ K}$). Inside the **`~2.7 AU` water-ice Frost Line** (`where T > 170 K`, located in today's outer Asteroid Belt), volatile compounds (`H₂O, NH₃, CH₄, CO, CO₂`) remained vapour and could not condense; only high-temperature refractory **metals (`Fe, Ni`, condensing at `~1,300–1,400 K`)** and **rocky silicates (`olivine, pyroxene`, `~1,000–1,300 K`)** — making up just **`~0.4–0.6%` of the solar nebula's mass** — could solidify into the small terrestrial planets. Outside `~2.7 AU`, water vapour froze into solid ice grains, abruptly **quadrupling the surface density of solid building blocks (`to ~2%` of nebula mass)** so outer planetary cores grew rapidly to **`~10 Earth masses`** (**core accretion model**, **Hiroshi Mizuno** `1980`, *Prog. Theor. Phys.* `64`, `544`; **James B. Pollack** [`1938–1994`], **Olenka Hubickyj**, **Peter Bodenheimer** [`born 1937`], **Jack J. Lissauer** [`born 1957`], *et al.* `1996`, *Icarus* `124`, `62–85`), triggering runaway gas accretion of massive `H₂/He` envelopes (`Jupiter and Saturn`) before the young Sun's extreme-UV photoevaporation and T Tauri winds dispersed the gas disk within **`~3 to 5 million years`** (`leaving slower-growing Uranus and Neptune as "ice giants" that missed runaway gas capture`).
- **The `1-Metre Drift Barrier` & the `Streaming Instability / Pebble Accretion` Solution (`Safronov 1969`; `Weidenschilling 1977`; `Youdin & Goodman 2005`; `Johansen et al. 2007`; `Lambrechts & Johansen 2012`):**
  - In **1969**, Soviet planetary physicist **Viktor Sergeyevich Safronov** (`1917–1999`, *Evolution of the Protoplanetary Cloud and Formation of the Earth and the Planets*, Moscow: Nauka, NASA TTF-677 `1972`) established the quantitative theory of how kilometre-sized **planetesimals** grow via gravitational focusing into `Moon-to-Mars-sized` **planetary embryos (`oligarchic growth`, `Kokubo & Ida 1998`)** over `~0.1–1 million years`, which then collide over `~30–100 million years` to build the rocky planets.
  - However, bridging the gap from `0.1-micron` dust to `1-kilometre` planetesimals hit the **`Metre-Size Barrier`**:
    1. **Bouncing & Fragmentation Barrier (`Blum & Wurm 2008`, *ARA&A* `46`, `21`):** Sub-micron and micron dust grains stick gently via electrostatic and van der Waals forces up to `~1 mm–1 cm` pebbles (`as shown in microgravity dust-collision experiments by` **Jürgen Blum** [`born 1962`] `and` **Lorin Swint Matthews** [`born 1972`]), but once aggregates reach `~10 cm to 1 m`, collisions at `~10–50 m/s` cause them to bounce off or shatter.
    2. **Radial Drift Barrier (`Stuart J. Weidenschilling 1977`, *MNRAS* `180`, `57–70`, *"Aerodynamics of solid bodies in the solar nebula"`):** Because the gas disk is hotter and denser closer to the Sun, an outward radial pressure gradient ($\partial P / \partial r < 0$) partially supports the gas against the Sun's gravity, causing the gas to orbit at **sub-Keplerian speed (`~0.5%` slower, or `~50 m/s` slower at `1 AU`)** than a solid rock (`which feels zero pressure support and must orbit at full Keplerian speed v_K ≈ 30 km/s`). Solid bodies therefore experience a continuous **`~50 m/s` aerodynamic headwind** that robs them of angular momentum. Small micron dust moves with the gas and large `>1-km` planetesimals have too much inertia to slow down, but **`~1-metre` boulders (`Stokes number St ≈ 1`) suffer maximum aerodynamic drag — spiralling from `1 AU` into the Sun in just `~100 years`**!
  - **The Solution — `Streaming Instability` & `Pebble Accretion` (`Youdin & Goodman 2005`; `Johansen et al. 2007`; `Lambrechts & Johansen 2012`):**
    - In **2005**, astrophysicists **Andrew N. Youdin** (`born 1975`) and **Jeremy Goodman** (`born 1958`, `Youdin & Goodman 2005`, *ApJ* `620`, `459–469`) discovered the **`Streaming Instability`**: by Newton's third law, a local clump of drifting centimetre-sized pebbles drags the surrounding gas forward (`reducing the headwind for pebbles behind it, just like a peloton of racing cyclists drafting in a headwind`). Trailing pebbles catch up and pile into dense filaments until the pebble density exceeds the local Roche density (`by ~1,000×`), allowing **3D supercomputer simulations by Anders Johansen (`born 1977`), Jeffrey S. Oishi, Mordecai-Mark Mac Low (`born 1959`), Hubert Klahr, Thomas Henning (`born 1956`), and Michiel Lambrechts (`Johansen et al. 2007`, *Nature* `448`, `1022–1025`)** to show that pebble filaments gravitationally collapse **directly into `100-to-1,000-km` planetesimals inside a few orbital periods (`~10–100 years`)**, leaping straight over the `1-metre` barrier (`a process confirmed in 2019 when NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past the Kuiper Belt contact-binary planetesimal 486958 Arrokoth, revealing two pristine flattened lobes gently merged at <3 m/s`, `Stern et al. 2019` / `McKinnon et al. 2020`, *Science* `367`, `aay6620`)!
    - In **2012**, **Michiel Lambrechts** (`born 1986`) and **Anders Johansen** (`Lambrechts & Johansen 2012`, *Astronomy & Astrophysics* `544`, `A32`, *"Rapid growth of gas-giant cores by pebble accretion"*) proved that once a `~1,000-km` seed forms, gas drag helps its gravity capture drifting centimetre pebbles across its entire Hill sphere (**`Pebble Accretion`**), growing a `10-Earth-mass` giant-planet core in **`<1–2 million years`** (`100 to 1,000 times faster` than classical planetesimal impacts, solving how Jupiter formed before the gas disk vanished!).
- **Jupiter's Inward Invasion (`The Grand Tack`, `Walsh et al. 2011`) & the `Nice Model` Outer Instability (`Tsiganis, Gomes, Morbidelli & Levison 2005`):**
  - **The Small-Mars Problem & the `Grand Tack` (`Walsh, Morbidelli, Raymond, O'Brien & Mandell 2011`, *Nature* `475`, `206–209`):** For decades, N-body simulations of the inner Solar System failed to explain why **Mars has only `0.107 Earth masses` (`10.7%` of Earth's mass)** — standard disks always produced a Mars `5 to 10 times` too massive — and why **Nicolas Dauphas** (`born 1975`) and **Ali Pourmand** (`Dauphas & Pourmand 2011`, *Nature* `473`, `489–492`) found via `¹⁸²Hf–¹⁸²W` chronometry of Martian meteorites that **Mars accreted cleanly in just `~1.8 to 3 million years`** as a stranded planetary embryo (whereas Earth took `~50–100 million years` of giant impacts, culminating in the `Theia` Moon-forming collision). In **June 2011**, **Kevin J. Walsh** (`born 1979`), **Alessandro Morbidelli** (`born 1966`), **Sean N. Raymond** (`born 1977`), **David P. O'Brien**, and **Avi M. Mandell** published the **`Grand Tack` model**:
    1. After forming near the snow line (`~3.5 AU`), **Jupiter** carved a deep annular gap in the protoplanetary gas disk and underwent **inward Type II migration**, spiralling all the way down to **`~1.5 AU` (`Mars's present orbit`)** like a cosmic snowplough!
    2. Jupiter's gravity compressed the inner rocky disk into a narrow dense ring between **`0.7 and 1.0 AU`** (from which **`Earth` and `Venus`** grew large), while scattering most rock out of the `1.5 AU` zone — leaving **`Mars`** stranded at the outer edge with only `0.107 M_⊕` and emptying the Asteroid Belt region.
    3. Just as Jupiter threatened to spiral past `1.5 AU` and destroy the inner planets' building blocks (`the way "Hot Jupiters" like 51 Pegasi b migrated to 0.05 AU in other star systems`), newborn **Saturn** grew large enough to migrate rapidly inward, caught up with Jupiter, and locked into a **`2:3` mean-motion resonance** (`Saturn completing 2 orbits for every 3 of Jupiter's`). The two giants merged their disk gaps, reversing the gas torque and making both planets **"tack" backward like a sailboat** — migrating outward together until the gas disk dissipated with Jupiter near **`~5.2–5.4 AU`** and Saturn near **`~7–8 AU`**!
    4. On its inward and outward sweep across the Asteroid Belt, Jupiter scattered dry inner **`S-type` (silicate) asteroids** and wet outer **`C-type` (carbonaceous, water-rich) asteroids** into the same `2.1–3.3 AU` belt (`reducing the belt's mass by ~99.9%` while delivering water-bearing carbonaceous planetesimals to young Earth!).
  - **The `Nice Model` Giant-Planet Instability (`Tsiganis, Gomes, Morbidelli & Levison 2005`, *Nature* `435`, `459–469`):** Developed at the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur in **Nice, France**, by **Kleomenis Tsiganis** (`born 1974`), **Rodney Gomes** (`born 1958`), **Alessandro Morbidelli** (`born 1966`), and **Harold F. Levison** (`born 1959`) across three companion *Nature* papers on **26 May 2005**, the **`Nice Model`** showed how the Solar System reached its modern architecture:
    1. After the gas disk cleared, the four giant planets orbited on nearly circular, closely packed orbits between **`~5.5 and 17 AU`** (`Jupiter at ~5.45 AU, Saturn at ~8.2–8.5 AU, and the two ice giants at ~11–14 AU and ~14–17 AU`), bordered outside by a **`~35-Earth-mass` disk of icy planetesimals (`from ~17 to 30–35 AU`)**.
    2. Slow gravitational scattering of icy planetesimals caused Jupiter to drift slightly inward (`to 5.20 AU`) while Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune drifted outward — until Jupiter and Saturn crossed their mutual **`1:2` mean-motion orbital resonance** (`or 2:3 / jumping-Jupiter resonance in updated Nice II models`, `Morbidelli et al. 2007`; `Nesvorný & Morbidelli 2012`), pumping up their orbital eccentricities.
    3. The sudden resonance crossing destabilized the entire outer Solar System: **Uranus and Neptune were flung outward into the icy planetesimal disk** (`reaching their modern orbits at 19.2 AU and 30.1 AU, and in ~50% of simulations Neptune leapfrogged past Uranus to become the outermost planet!`), dynamical friction with the scattered planetesimals re-circularized their orbits, and **`~99%` of the outer icy disk was ejected**, sculpting the modern **`Kuiper Belt`**, the **`Scattered Disk`**, and the **`Oort Cloud`**, capturing Jupiter's **Trojan asteroids** (`Morbidelli et al. 2005`), and sending a surge of icy comets and asteroids through the inner Solar System (**the Late Heavy Bombardment**)!
