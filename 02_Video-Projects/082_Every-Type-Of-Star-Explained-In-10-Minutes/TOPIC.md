### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **The Harvard Spectral Classification (`O, B, A, F, G, K, M`) & the Hertzsprung-Russell Diagram (`Cannon & Pickering 1918–1924` *Henry Draper Catalogue*; `Payne 1925`; `Hertzsprung 1911`; `Russell 1914`):**
  - At Harvard College Observatory (`under director Edward C. Pickering [1846–1919]`), astronomer **Annie Jump Cannon** (`1863–1941`) visually classified **`359,083` stellar spectra** (`225,300` in the `1918–1924` *Henry Draper Catalogue*, vols. `91–99`, plus `46,850` in the `1925–1936` *Henry Draper Extension*, vol. `100`, and `86,933` in the posthumous `1949` *Henry Draper Extension Charts*, vol. `112`), organizing stars into a smooth temperature sequence of seven principal spectral classes — **`O, B, A, F, G, K, M`** (subdivided `0–9`, later extended with **`L, T, Y`** for cool brown dwarfs and **`W`** for Wolf-Rayet stars, and paired in **1943** by **William Wilson Morgan** [`1906–1994`] and **Philip C. Keenan** [`1908–2000`] with Roman-numeral **MKK luminosity classes**: `0` or `Ia+` hypergiants, `I` supergiants, `II` bright giants, `III` giants, `IV` subgiants, `V` main-sequence dwarfs, and `D` / `VII` white dwarfs).
  - In **1925**, 25-year-old British-American astrophysicist **Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin** (`1900–1979`, *Stellar Atmospheres*, Harvard PhD thesis) applied **Meghnad Saha**'s (`1893–1956`, `1920`) thermal ionization equation to Cannon's `O–M` spectral sequence, proving that spectral differences across star types reflect **surface temperature (`from ~2,400 K in M-stars to >30,000–50,000 K in O-stars`)** rather than chemical composition, and that stars are composed almost entirely of **hydrogen (`~73%` of baryonic mass) and helium (`~25%`)**, with all heavier elements ("metals") making up `<2%`.
  - Independently developed between **1911 and 1913** by Danish astronomer **Ejnar Hertzsprung** (`1873–1967`) and American astronomer **Henry Norris Russell** (`1877–1957`), the **Hertzsprung-Russell (`H-R`) diagram** plots stellar luminosity (`10⁻⁴ to >10⁶ L_☉`) against surface temperature (`or spectral class O–M`), revealing that **`~90%` of all active stars** lie along the diagonal hydrogen-fusing **Main Sequence (`luminosity class V`)**, where a star's birth mass strictly determines its core temperature, luminosity ($L \propto M^{3.5}$ for intermediate-mass stars), colour, and lifespan ($\tau \propto M / L \approx M^{-2.5}$).
- **Sub-Stellar Failed Stars (`Brown Dwarfs` `L, T, Y`) & Immortal `M-Type Red Dwarfs` (`Rebolo et al. 1995`; `Nakajima et al. 1995`; `Laughlin, Bodenheimer & Adams 1997`, *ApJ* `482`, `420`):**
  - **`L, T, and Y` Brown Dwarfs (`~13 to ~80 Jupiter masses`, `0.012 to 0.075–0.08 M_☉`, surface temperatures `~250 K to 2,200 K`):** First theorized by **Shiv S. Kumar** (`1963`) and named "brown dwarfs" by **Jill C. Tarter** (`born 1944`) in **1975**, objects below the **`~0.075–0.08 M_☉` (`~78–84 M_J`) hydrogen-burning minimum mass** are halted by electron degeneracy pressure before their cores reach the `~3 million K` required for sustained proton-proton (`¹H + ¹H`) fusion. Above **`~13 M_J`** they briefly fuse primordial **deuterium (`²H`)** (`and above ~65 M_J they fuse lithium-7`), then cool across billions of years through spectral classes **`L`** (`1,300–2,200 K`), **`T`** (`550–1,300 K`, methane-dominated, like **`Gliese 229B`**, discovered in **1995** by **Tadashi Nakajima**, **Ben R. Oppenheimer**, **Shrinivas R. Kulkarni**, et al., *Nature* `378`, `463`), and **`Y`** (`<550 K`, down to `~250 K` on `WISE 0855−0714`); the first verified brown dwarf **`Teide 1`** in the Pleiades was announced in **September 1995** by **Rafael Rebolo**, **María Rosa Zapatero Osorio**, and **Eduardo L. Martín** (*Nature* `377`, `129`).
  - **`M-Type Red Dwarfs` (`0.08 to 0.60 M_☉`, `2,400–3,700 K`, `0.0001 to 0.08 L_☉`, radii `~0.10–0.60 R_☉`):** Account for **`~70–75%` of all stars in the Milky Way** (including our nearest stellar neighbour **`Proxima Centauri`** at `4.2465 light-years`, `0.122 M_☉`, `0.154 R_☉`, `~3,042 K`, `0.0017 L_☉`, and **`TRAPPIST-1`** at `0.0898 M_☉`). Below **`~0.35 M_☉`**, red dwarfs are **fully convective** from core to surface (`no radiative zone`), cycling nearly `100%` of their hydrogen through the core (compared to `~10%` in the Sun) and burning it so slowly via the proton-proton (`pp`) chain that an `0.1 M_☉` red dwarf lives for **`~6 to 10 trillion years`** (`Laughlin, Bodenheimer & Adams 1997`, *ApJ* `482`, `420–432`) — **`400 to 700 times` the current `13.8-billion-year` age of the universe**, meaning **not a single red dwarf has ever died of old age** (and at the end of their lives, low-mass red dwarfs `<0.25 M_☉` contract directly into **helium white dwarfs** via a slow **blue-dwarf** radiative stage without ever becoming red giants).
- **Sun-Like & Intermediate Main-Sequence Stars (`K, G, F, A` Classes — `5,778 K` `G2V` Sun to `9,940 K` `Sirius A`; `Pecaut & Mamajek 2013`, *ApJS* `208`, `9`):**
  - **`K-Type` Orange Dwarfs (`0.60–0.90 M_☉` [`0.57–0.88 M_☉` empirical `K9V–K0V`], `3,700–5,200 K`, `0.08–0.60 L_☉`, like `Alpha Centauri B` and `Epsilon Eridani`):** Make up `~12%` of Milky Way stars and remain stably on the main sequence for **`15 to 45 billion years`** with lower extreme-UV/X-ray flare activity than young `M-dwarfs` ("Goldilocks stars").
  - **`G-Type` Yellow Dwarfs (`0.80–1.04 M_☉` classical / `0.88–1.08 M_☉` empirical `G9V–G0V`, `5,200–6,000 K`, `0.60–1.5 L_☉`, `~7.5%` of stars):** Exemplified by our **`G2V` Sun** (`mass 1.9885 × 10³⁰ kg`, equatorial radius **`695,700 km` [`~1,391,400 km` diameter, holding `~1.3 million` Earths by volume and `99.86%` of the Solar System's mass]**, photospheric temperature **`5,778 K`**, luminosity **`3.828 × 10²⁶ W`**). At its **`15.7 million K`** core (`density ~150 g/cm³`), the Sun fuses **`~600 million metric tonnes` of hydrogen into `~596 million tonnes` of helium every second** via the proton-proton chain, converting **`~4.26 million tonnes` of mass per second directly into energy ($E = mc^2$)** across a **`~10-billion-year`** main-sequence lifetime (`currently 4.57 billion years old`).
  - **`F-Type` Yellow-White (`1.04–1.40 M_☉`, `6,000–7,500 K`, `1.5–5 L_☉`, `~3%` of stars, living `2–4 billion years`, e.g. `Procyon A` & `Polaris`)** and **`A-Type` White Stars (`1.40–2.10 M_☉`, `7,500–10,000 K`, `5–25 L_☉`, `~0.6%` of stars, living `~400 million to 2 billion years`, e.g. `Sirius A` [`A1V`, `2.06 M_☉`, `1.71 R_☉`, `9,940 K`, `25.4 L_☉`, `8.6 light-years` away, the brightest star in Earth's night sky] and `Vega`)** mark the transition from proton-proton fusion to the **CNO cycle** and from convective envelopes to radiative envelopes.
- **Massive Blue Giants, Wolf-Rayet Stars & the Largest Known Red Hypergiants (`B & O` Classes, `Bethe 1939`, `Stephenson 2-18` & `UY Scuti`):**
  - **`B-Type` (`2.1–16 M_☉`, `10,000–30,000 K`, `25–30,000 L_☉`, `~0.13%` of stars, e.g. `Rigel` [`B8Ia` blue supergiant, `~21 M_☉`, `~120,000 L_☉`] and `Spica`)** and **`O-Type` Blue Stars (`16 to >150 M_☉`, `30,000–52,000+ K`, `30,000 to >1,000,000 L_☉`, only `~0.00003%` [`1 in ~3 million`] of main-sequence stars, e.g. `Zeta Ophiuchi`, `Theta¹ Orionis C`, and the extreme Wolf-Rayet/O-slash hypergiant `R136a1` in the Tarantula Nebula at `~150–200 M_☉`, `~46,000–53,000 K`, and `~4.7–6.2 million L_☉`; `Crowther et al. 2010`, `Kalari et al. 2022`)** fuse hydrogen via the **carbon-nitrogen-oxygen (`CNO`) cycle** (`Hans A. Bethe 1939`, *Physical Review* `55`, `434`, Nobel Prize 1967), running through their nuclear fuel in just **`3 to 10 million years`**.
  - **`Wolf-Rayet` (`WR`) Stars (`discovered 1867 by Charles Wolf and Georges Rayet at Paris Observatory`):** Evolved massive stars (`initially >25 M_☉`, surface temperatures **`30,000 to 210,000 K`**) that have blown off their outer hydrogen envelopes via radiation-driven stellar winds exceeding **`1,000 to 3,000 km/s`** (losing `10⁻⁵ M_☉/yr`), exposing raw helium, nitrogen (`WN`), carbon (`WC`), or oxygen (`WO`) fusion layers shortly before exploding as Type Ib/Ic core-collapse supernovae (`or long gamma-ray bursts`).
  - **`Red Supergiants` & `Red Hypergiants` (`Betelgeuse`, `VY Canis Majoris`, `UY Scuti`, `Stephenson 2-18`):** When stars of `~8–40 M_☉` exhaust core hydrogen and begin fusing helium (`triple-alpha process` at `100 million K`) and heavier onion shells (`carbon, neon, oxygen, silicon`), their outer hydrogen envelopes expand hundreds to thousands of times in radius while cooling to `~3,200–3,800 K`:
    - **`Betelgeuse` (`Alpha Orionis`, `M1–M2 Ia-ab`):** **`~764–887 R_☉`** (`~1.1–1.2 billion km` wide, large enough to swallow Mars's or Jupiter's orbit).
    - **`VY Canis Majoris` (`M3–M4.5 Ia` red hypergiant):** **`~1,420 ± 120 R_☉`** (`Wittkowski et al. 2012`, *A&A* `540`, `L12`).
    - **`UY Scuti` (`M2–M4 Ia`):** Classical interferometric estimate **`~1,708 ± 192 R_☉`** at `2.9 kpc` (`Arroyo-Torres et al. 2013`, *A&A* `554`, `A76`; revised downward to `~755–909 R_☉` if located at the nearer Gaia DR2/DR3 distance of `~1.55–1.8 kpc`).
    - **`Stephenson 2-18` (`RSGC2-18`, `M6` red supergiant/hypergiant in open cluster Stephenson 2 at `~5.8 kpc`):** Estimated bolometric radius of **`~2,150 solar radii` (`~1.50 billion km` radius / `~3.0 billion km` [`~10.0 AU`] diameter, `T_eff ≈ 3,200 K`, `L ≈ 440,000 L_☉`; `Davies et al. 2007`, *ApJ* `671`, `781`; `Fok et al. 2012`, *ApJ* `760`, `65`)** — so large in volume (**`~10 billion Suns`**) that if placed at the Sun's position its photosphere would extend beyond **Saturn's `9.58 AU` (`1.43 billion km`) orbit**, though because its `~12–20 M_☉` mass is spread across such an immense volume its outer atmosphere is millions of times less dense than air at sea level!
- **The Stellar Graveyard — `White Dwarfs` (`1.44 M_☉` Chandrasekhar Limit), `Neutron Stars`, `716-Hz Pulsars`, & `10¹⁵-Gauss Magnetars` (`Chandrasekhar 1931, 1935`; `Hewish, Bell et al. 1968`; `Hessels et al. 2006`; `Duncan & Thompson 1992`):**
  - **`White Dwarfs` & the Chandrasekhar Limit (`<8 M_☉` progenitors, `~97%` of all stars):** After shedding `~40–80%` of their mass as a planetary nebula, low- and intermediate-mass stars leave behind an exposed carbon-oxygen (or helium / oxygen-neon-magnesium) core with **`~0.5 to 1.4 solar masses` compressed into an Earth-sized sphere (`radius ~5,000–7,000 km` / `diameter ~10,000–14,000 km`, density `~10⁶ g/cm³` — `1 metric tonne per cubic centimetre`)**, such as **`Sirius B`** (`discovered 1862 by` **Alvan Graham Clark** [`1832–1897`]; mass **`1.018 M_☉`**, radius **`~5,840 km` [`0.92 R_⊕`]**, surface temperature `25,200 K`; `Bond et al. 2017`). In **1930** (`published 1931`, *ApJ* `74`, `81–82`, where Eddington's old $\mu_e = 2.5$ yielded $0.91\,M_\odot$, and refined in **1935**, *MNRAS* `95`, `207–225` with the full relativistic equation of state $M_{\text{Ch}} \approx 5.75\,\mu_e^{-2}\,M_\odot \approx 1.44\,M_\odot$ for carbon-oxygen $\mu_e = 2.0$), 19-year-old Indian astrophysicist **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar** (`1910–1995`, Nobel Prize 1983) combined special relativity with quantum Fermi-Dirac statistics to prove that **relativistic electron degeneracy pressure** cannot support a white dwarf more massive than **`~1.44 solar masses`** (the **Chandrasekhar limit**; pushing a carbon-oxygen white dwarf to `~1.44 M_☉` via accretion or merger triggers a runaway thermonuclear **Type Ia supernova** that completely unbinds the star). Because the universe is `13.8 billion years` old and white dwarfs take `>10¹³–10¹⁵ years` to cool to `~5 K`, **zero cold Black Dwarfs exist today**.
  - **`Neutron Stars`, `Pulsars`, & `Magnetars` (`~8 to 20–25 M_☉` progenitors):** When a massive star's degenerate **`Iron-56` (`⁵⁶Fe` / `⁵⁶Ni`) core** exceeds the `1.44 M_☉` Chandrasekhar limit, electron capture ($p + e^- \to n + \nu_e$) and photodisintegration trigger a catastrophic collapse in **`<1 second`** at `~23%` the speed of light (`~70,000 km/s`), rebounding at nuclear saturation density (`~3–5 × 10¹⁴ g/cm³` — **`~300 to 500 million metric tonnes per cubic centimetre`**, or **`>1 billion tonnes per teaspoon`**) in a **Type II / Ib / Ic core-collapse supernova** (`releasing ~10⁴⁶ J in neutrinos and ~10⁴⁴ J in kinetic/optical energy`) to leave behind a **`Neutron Star`** (`theorized in 1934 by` **Walter Baade** [`1893–1960`] and **Fritz Zwicky** [`1898–1974`]): **`~1.1 to 2.1–2.35 solar masses` packed into a sphere just `~20 kilometres` (`~12 miles`) across** (with surface gravity `~2 × 10¹¹ g` and escape velocity `~100,000–150,000 km/s`, `33–50%` of the speed of light, supported by neutron degeneracy pressure and strong-force nuclear repulsion up to the **`~2.2–2.9 M_☉` Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff [`TOV`] limit** [`Tolman 1939`; `Oppenheimer & Volkoff 1939`, *Phys.
