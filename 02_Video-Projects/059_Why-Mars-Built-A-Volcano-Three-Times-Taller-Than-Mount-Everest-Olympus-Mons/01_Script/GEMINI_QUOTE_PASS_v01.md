### Priority 2 — Crust vs. Lithosphere, `70 km` Provenance, Hotspot Certainty, & Basal Clay Spreading (`FLAG`)
1. **Crust vs. Elastic Lithosphere (`FLAG`):**
   - Script Line 3 (`"Mars locked a stationary 70-kilometer-thick crust"`), Line 38 (`"piled onto a 70-kilometer-thick crust"`), and Line 81 (`"70-kilometer stationary crust"` / `"70 km crust"`) call the `70 km` layer the **crust**.
   - In planetary geophysics, the **crust** (compositional shell above the Moho) and the **elastic lithosphere** (mechanically rigid outer shell, $T_e$, which includes the crust plus rigid uppermost mantle) are distinct:
     - **Smith et al. (2001, `mola.summary.pdf`, Section 6.2, p. 23,710 / PDF p. 22):** `"Recent geophysical modeling that incorporates MOLA topography and MGS gravity gives a minimum average crust thickness of ~50 km."`
     - **Zuber et al. (2000, `PMID 10710301`) & Smith et al. (2001, p. 23,711 / PDF p. 23):** `"Very little deflection of the lithosphere is associates with the massive load of Olympus Mons, indicating the footprint of a volcano on an thick elastic lithosphere (Te > 100 km)"` (citing Zuber et al. 2000).
     - **P. J. McGovern et al. (2004, *JGR* 109, E07007) & P. J. McGovern (2010, *AbSciCon 2010*, Abstract #5633, `https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meetings/abscicon2010/pdf/5633.pdf`, p. 1):** `"The mass of the Olympus Mons edifice creates a large downward load on the underlying lithosphere. Estimates of Te from gravity/topography admittance yield the constraint Te > 70 km [16], giving a flexure length scale alpha >= 150 km. For Te = 70 km, the peak deflection w beneath the center of the edifice is about 30 km, declining cleanly across the edifice edge into a surrounding flexural moat (whose volcanic infill masks the bottom ~1–2 km of the outer flank and basal scarp)."`.
     - Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons` (`"supported by a 70 km (43 mi) thick lithosphere"`) summarises this $T_e \ge 70\text{ km}$ elastic lithosphere constraint. Calling it `"70-kilometer-thick crust"` is scientifically inaccurate (`FLAG`).
2. **Stationary Hotspot Certainty & Basal Clay Spreading (`FLAG`):**
   - Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons` qualifies the stationary-plume explanation: `"The extraordinary size of Olympus Mons is likely because Mars lacks mobile tectonic plates. Unlike on Earth, the crust of Mars remains fixed over a stationary hotspot..."`
   - Script Line 36 states as an unqualified event: `"while spreading on weak clays suddenly carved an 8-kilometer perimeter cliff scarp — Olympus Rupes"`. In the primary literature — **P. J. McGovern & J. K. Morgan (2009)**, *"Volcanic spreading and lateral variations in the structure of Olympus Mons, Mars"*, *Geology*, Vol. 37, No. 2, pp. 139–142 (`doi:10.1130/g25180a.1`; ScienceDaily release `https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/02/090203175343.htm`) and **McGovern (2010)** (`abscicon2010/pdf/5633.pdf`) — basal spreading over phyllosilicate clay sediments is a **numerical/mechanical model** (`"Here we propose a pair of bright concepts..."`, `"a basal layer of weak clay sediments underneath the volcano could receive the necessary lubricating water and overpressure from the weight of the overlying rocks"`), while **catastrophic flank landslides / mass failure** (e.g., **J. K. Morgan, P. J. McGovern & G. S.usters / Milazzo et al. 2004**, `doi:10.1029/2004JE002258`, *Olympus Mons aureole deposits: New evidence for a flank failure origin*, and `PIA22181`: `"perhaps formed by enormous landslides off the flanks of the giant volcano"`) form the headwall scarp (`Olympus Rupes`) and aureole lobes (`Lycus Sulci`). Moreover, `SOURCES.md` did not cite McGovern & Morgan (2009) at all.

### Priority 3 — Pressure Units (`600 Pa` vs. `6.36 mbar`), Ratios, & Summit Pressure (`72 Pa`) (`FLAG`)
1. **Unit Conversion Conflict (`600 Pa` vs. `6.36 mbar` — `FLAG`):**
   - Script Line 50 (`"Mars's 600-pascal — 6.36-millibar — surface average"`) and `SOURCES.md` Row 11 treat `600 pascals` and `6.36 millibars` as equal conversions.
   - By exact SI definition, **`1 millibar (hPa) = 100 pascals`**, so **`6.36 mbar = 636 pascals`** (`marsfact.html`: `"Surface pressure: 6.36 mb at mean radius (variable from 4.0 to 8.7 mb depending on season)"`), whereas **`600 pascals = 6.0 millibars`** (and the MOLA datum reference pressure in Smith et al. 2001 `mola.summary.pdf` p. 13 is `6.1 mbar = 610 Pa` at $273\text{ K}$). Equating `600 Pa` with `6.36 mbar` in the same hyphenated phrase is a direct unit mismatch (`FLAG`).
2. **Summit Pressure (`72 Pa` / `12%` of `600 Pa`) & Scale-Height Consistency (`FLAG`):**
   - Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons` states: `"The typical atmospheric pressure at the top of Olympus Mons is 72 pascals, about 12% of the average Martian surface pressure of 600 pascals. Both are exceedingly low by terrestrial standards; by comparison, the atmospheric pressure at the summit of Mount Everest is 32,000 pascals, or about 32% of Earth's sea level pressure."`
   - Inspecting the Wikipedia wikitext (`/tmp/olympus_mons_wikitext.txt`) reveals that `72 pascals` has **no peer-reviewed citation**: an embedded editor comment reads `<!--72 pascals value carefully read off graph of standard pressure profile at 21 km elevation-->`, referencing a dead Stanford MGS Radio Science webpage (`http://www-star.stanford.edu/projects/mgs/dmr.html`, whose quoted text actually says: `"Standard Pressure Profiles measured by MGS Radio Science team at 27 km range from approximately 30 to 50 Pa"`).
   - Furthermore, using the script's own spoken numbers (`600 Pa` or `636 Pa` mean pressure, `11.0 km` atmospheric scale height from `marsfact.html`, and `21.287 km` MOLA elevation), the hydrostatic exponential pressure formula $P(z) = P_0 e^{-z/H}$ gives $600 \times e^{-21.287/11.0} = 86.7\text{ Pa}$ (`14.5%`) or $636 \times e^{-21.287/11.0} = 91.9\text{ Pa}$ (a scale height of $H \approx 10.05\text{ km}$ is required for $600 \times e^{-21.287/H} = 72\text{ Pa}$).
   - In practice, Martian atmospheric pressure at the summit of Olympus Mons varies seasonally (as $\text{CO}_2$ condenses and sublimates at the polar caps) between **`~30 Pa` and `~70–100 Pa`** (`~0.3 to 1.0 mbar`, roughly `5%` to `12%` of the ~600 Pa mean surface pressure). `J1953` should soften `"is 72 pascals"` to an approximate range (`"around 30 to 72 pascals — roughly 5 to 12 percent of Mars's ~600-pascal average"`) and separate `~600 Pa (6 mbar)` from `6.36 mbar (636 Pa)`.

### Priority 4 — `5%` Flank Slope, `"Circus Tent"` Profile, & `3 km` Horizon Assumptions (`FLAG`)
1. **Misattribution to Pettengill & Garvin (`PMID 10348732` — `FLAG`):**
   - Script Line 46 (`"as MIT planetary physicist Gordon H. Pettengill — who lived from 1926 to 2021 — and NASA Goddard scientist James B. Garvin helped map with the Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter, the average flank slope of Olympus Mons is only 5 percent — a gentle shield profile like a circus tent, where an observer sees the horizon just 3 kilometers away"`) attributes all three phrases to MOLA (`PMID 10348732`).
   - Direct search of `PMID 10348732` and the full 38-page `mola.summary.pdf` (Smith et al. 2001) confirms that **none of `"5%"`, `"circus tent"`, or `"3 kilometers"` appears in those MOLA papers**.
   - In Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons`, those three claims come from three different sources:
     - **`5%` (`~2.9°`) average flank slope:** Cited to **Michael H. Carr (2007)**, *The Surface of Mars*, Cambridge University Press, p. 51 (`ISBN 978-0-521-87201-0`). Note also that **J. B. Plescia (2004)**, *JGR* 109, E03003 (`doi:10.1029/2002JE002031`, based on MOLA DEMs), reports `"Average flank slopes are in the range of <1° to ~10°"` (`~4°–5°` / `7%–9%` on Olympus Mons's main flanks, shallower on the lower northwest flank and steeper on the upper southeast flank).
     - **`"Circus tent"` profile:** Coined by **Patrick J. McGovern & Julia K. Morgan (2009)**, *Geology* 37(2), 139–142 (`https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/02/090203175343.htm`: `"Friction decreasing outward from the center of the edifice can explain the overall 'circus tent' shape"`, and McGovern 2010 `abscicon2010/pdf/5633.pdf` p. 1: `"giving the edifice the cross-sectional profile of a 'circus tent'"`), describing the concave-upward topographic profile produced by basal decollement spreading — **not** Pettengill or Garvin.
     - **`"3 kilometers away"` horizon:** Cited in Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons` to an amateur astronomy webpage (`Jeff Beish, 2009, dustymars.net`). Geometrically, on a smooth sphere of Mars's mean radius ($R = 3,389.5\text{ km}$), the horizon distance for a standing human observer of eye height $h = 1.7\text{ m}$ is $d = \sqrt{2 R h} = \sqrt{2 \times 3,389,500 \times 1.7} \approx 3.39\text{ km}$ (`~3 km`). Thus `~3 km` is simply the local curvature horizon for a standing person on a gently curving plateau on Mars (because the `~5%` flank curves convexly/gently over hundreds of kilometers, blocking sight of the distant base beyond the local ~3 km horizon), **not** an instrument measurement by Pettengill or Garvin.
