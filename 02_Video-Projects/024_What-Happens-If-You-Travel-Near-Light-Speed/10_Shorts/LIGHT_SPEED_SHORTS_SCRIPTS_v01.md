## 1. Mon 2 Nov: GPS Only Works Because of Relativity

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. A GPS satellite already crossing the frame over Earth's limb. Whoosh. Caption: TIME SLIPS. No Orbit.]

The clocks above your head are running fast.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. A slow push in on the satellite's body. Orbit (Omni only) may drift past small, holding a tiny clock. If Omni is down, stay on the world.]

GPS clocks gain thirty-eight millionths of a second a day.

[VISUAL MUST: A city map at street level. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

Moving fast slows them down. Sitting higher, in weaker gravity, speeds them up.
Leave it uncorrected, and your map would drift ten kilometres a day.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening satellite.]

The full film: What Happens If You Travel Near the Speed of Light?

[TEACH: About −7.2 μs/day from speed and +45.9 μs/day from gravity, a net of about +38 μs/day. Uncorrected, that is about 10–12 km/day (Ashby 2003).]

## 2. Wed 4 Nov: Could a Robot Reach the Speed of Light?

Uses the "Could a Robot…" slot. The robot beat needs Omni. If Omni is down, the Short runs on the code graphics, and the VO stands either way.

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. The code-rendered starfield already crowding forward (the 024 opening plate). Whoosh. Caption: THE LIMIT. No Orbit.]

Nothing with mass can reach light speed.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Orbit (Omni) strains forward against the stars, thrusters bright.]
[ORBIT ACTS: Orbit pushes harder and harder, and the stars barely change.]

Could a robot do it?

[VISUAL MUST: The code-rendered energy curve, flat and then near-vertical. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

Push one tonne to ninety percent: a fifth of the world's yearly energy.
Ninety-nine percent costs nearly the whole year.
Every extra nine costs more. At the limit, it never ends.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening starfield.]

The full film: What Happens If You Travel Near the Speed of Light?

[TEACH: (γ−1)mc² for 1,000 kg is about 1.16 × 10²⁰ J at 0.9c and about 5.47 × 10²⁰ J at 0.99c, against about 6.2 × 10²⁰ J of world primary energy per year. It diverges as v → c.]

## 3. Fri 6 Nov: Muons Shouldn't Reach the Ground. They Do.

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. A code-rendered cosmic-ray shower already falling through the sky towards the ground. Whoosh. Caption: THEY LIVE. No Orbit.]

Particles from space are passing through you. They shouldn't get here.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. The shower thins towards a city at night. Orbit (Omni) may glance up as a streak passes. If Omni is down, stay on the shower.]

Muons are born high up and live two millionths of a second: barely six hundred metres of flight.

[VISUAL MUST: A tiny clock riding one streak, ticking slowly. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

At nearly light speed, their clocks run slow. So they reach the ground.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening shower.]

The full film: What Happens If You Travel Near the Speed of Light?

[TEACH: Muon lifetime is about 2.2 μs, or about 660 m without dilation. Frisch & Smith (1963) measured the sea-level flux, which matched relativity. Sea-level flux is about 1 per cm² per minute (PDG).]
