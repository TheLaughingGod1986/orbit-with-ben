So why haven't we done it? Because going fast costs more than it seems.

Take a single tonne, about the mass of a small car. To push it to ninety percent of light speed takes around a fifth of all the energy humanity uses in a year. To push the same tonne from ninety to ninety-nine percent takes nearly five times as much, close to a whole year of the world's energy, for one small car.

Each extra nine costs more than the last. Closer to the limit, the energy needed grows without end. That is why nothing with mass ever reaches light speed. It is not a wall you hit. It is a hill that keeps getting steeper.

And at those speeds, space itself turns hard. Every speck of interstellar dust becomes a tiny explosion against the front of the ship.

For scale, the fastest thing we have ever built is the probe that skims past the Sun. It reaches about six hundredths of one percent of light speed. Plans exist to push tiny chips, lighter than a paperclip, to a fifth of light speed with lasers. Nothing human-sized is close.
