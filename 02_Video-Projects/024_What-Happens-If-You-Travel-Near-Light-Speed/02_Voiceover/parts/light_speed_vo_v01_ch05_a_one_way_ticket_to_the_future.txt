Here is the strange gift inside all this.

A ship that could keep accelerating at one Earth gravity, then turn and slow down for the second half, could cross the whole Milky Way in a little over twenty years of the crew's time. A human lifetime is enough to reach almost anywhere.

But on Earth, a hundred thousand years would pass. Everyone you knew, and everyone who remembered them, would be gone long before you arrived. You could not come back to the home you left. Only to a home that had kept going without you.

That is the familiar danger hidden in the title. It is not the speed. It is your own clock, drifting away from everyone else's.

You already live inside this. Your phone depends on it every day. A muon carries it through your body as you sit here. The light from the screen in front of you is travelling at the one speed every clock agrees on.

What if the speed limit of the universe is not a wall at all, but a trade: the faster you go, the more of everyone else's future you spend?

The bigger question is where you would choose to land, if you knew home would not be waiting.
