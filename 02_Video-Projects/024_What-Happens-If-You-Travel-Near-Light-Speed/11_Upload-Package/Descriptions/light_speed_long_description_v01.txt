What happens if you travel near the speed of light? Leave Earth at ninety-nine percent of light speed for one year by your own watch, and seven years pass at home. That is time dilation, and special relativity says nobody cheated: a moving clock really runs slow.

Your phone already depends on it. GPS satellite clocks drift by about 38 millionths of a second a day, and left alone the map would wander by around ten kilometres a day. In 1971 atomic clocks flown around the world on airliners came home out of step, and muons from cosmic rays reach the ground only because their clocks run slow. We follow the twin paradox on a trip to a star ten light-years away, see why each extra nine of speed costs more energy than the last, and why a ship at one Earth gravity could cross the Milky Way in a little over twenty years of the crew's time while a hundred thousand years pass at home.
