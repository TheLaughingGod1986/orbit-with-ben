## Narration (Part 02 only)

Under the ice, the cold should win.

On Earth, oceans freeze from the top. Leave them long enough and winter stacks thicker and thicker — until liquid loses.

But Europa’s shell is not a quiet pond. It is a lid under stress.

Jupiter’s gravity pulls and stretches this moon as it orbits — a slow, relentless kneading. Ice flexes. Fractures open. Heat blooms in the dark.

Orbit drifts deeper. Antenna glowing. Around him, the frozen ceiling is not dead. It creaks. It moves. It leaks warmth into the black water below.

That is why the ocean can stay liquid — not because Europa is close to the Sun, but because a giant neighbour will not stop pulling.

The ice does not win. Tide wins. Heat wins. Time under pressure wins.

And if liquid water can survive here… the next question is sharper.

What else might survive with it?
