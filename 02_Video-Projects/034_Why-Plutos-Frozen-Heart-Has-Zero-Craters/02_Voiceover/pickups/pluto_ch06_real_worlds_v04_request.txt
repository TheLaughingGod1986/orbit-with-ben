Pluto showed a world one-fifth the width of Earth can hold water-ice mountains, a moon that never sets, and a nitrogen heart with zero craters.

Next in our Real Worlds series, we travel inward to Neptune's largest moon, Triton — a captured Kuiper Belt cousin of Pluto that orbits backwards and erupts active geysers on a frozen nitrogen crust at minus two hundred and thirty-five degrees Celsius.

Looking back from two hundred thousand kilometres away on 14 July 2015, New Horizons captured its farewell colour portrait of Pluto's receding crescent and blue atmospheric haze.
