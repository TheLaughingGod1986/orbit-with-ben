Why does Pluto's giant heart-shaped glacier have zero craters, and what did NASA's New Horizons spacecraft find at the edge of our solar system?

Follow the story of Pluto — from Clyde Tombaugh's 1930 discovery in Flagstaff and eleven-year-old Venetia Burney's breakfast-table name suggestion, to the 1978 discovery of its giant moon Charon and New Horizons' nine-year voyage across the solar system.

Explore a world one-fifth the width of Earth where a thousand-kilometre nitrogen glacier called Sputnik Planitia churns with city-sized convection cells, mountains of water ice stand two to three kilometres tall at minus 240 degrees Celsius, and liquid nitrogen rises from basal melting beneath the ice.
