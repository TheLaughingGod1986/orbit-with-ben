**Chapter list after audit** (5 acts, each with a teach-point):

1. **What That Flash in the Night Sky Really Is** — Why a shooting star is not a star and the streak is glowing ionized air (`50–100 km` up in the mesosphere/lower thermosphere) heated by a sand-to-pea-sized meteoroid entering at `11–72 km/s` (`25,000–160,000 mph`), part of `48.5 tons` (`44 tonnes`) of cosmic material hitting Earth every day.
2. **How Comets Leave Ribbons of Debris Across Earth's Orbit** — How solar heating sublimates comet ice, spraying rocky dust along the comet's orbital track, and why perspective makes parallel meteoroid tracks appear to shoot from one constellation (**the radiant**).
3. **Why Halley's Comet Gives Earth Two Meteor Showers Every Year** — Although `1P/Halley` (`15 × 8 km` nucleus, `3%` albedo) visits the inner Solar System only once every `76 years` (`1986`, returning `2061`), Earth crosses its debris stream twice a year: May's **Eta Aquariids** (`peak May 5–6`, `65.4 km/s`) and October's **Orionids** (`peak Oct 21–22`, `66 km/s`).
4. **Swift-Tuttle's Perseids — and the December Shower That Comes From an Asteroid** — August's **Perseids** (`peak Aug 12–13`, `59 km/s`, `109P/Swift-Tuttle`, `133-year` orbit, `26-km` nucleus, linked by Giovanni Schiaparelli in `1865`) vs December's **Geminids** (`peak Dec 13–14`, `35 km/s`, up to `120/hr`), which come from near-Earth asteroid **`3200 Phaethon`** (`1.4-year` orbit).
5. **Fireballs and Meteorites: What Survives the Fall to the Ground?** — Why meteors brighter than Venus are called **fireballs** (bolides), why fragile icy comet particles vaporize high in the air, and why the **meteorites** picked up on the ground come almost entirely from tougher **asteroids** (and rarely the Moon or Mars).
