### 2.3 Story Beats (All Numbers Checked Against Direct NASA Sources in §3)
1. **What a "shooting star" actually is (`Ch 1`):** Not a star, and the bright streak isn't even the rock itself — it is the glowing column of superheated air and vaporizing rock as a meteoroid (from a dust grain or sand-to-pea pebble up to boulder size) hits Earth's atmosphere at **`11 to 72 km/s` (`25,000 to 160,000 mph`)** and burns up in the **mesosphere (`50–80 km` / `31–50 miles` up)**, with bright shower meteors like the Perseids ablating between **`76 and 104 km` (`47–65 miles`, peaking near `90 km` / `56 miles`)**. About **`48.5 tons` (`44 tonnes` / `44,000 kg`)** of meteoritic material falls on Earth every single day.
2. **Where the debris streams come from (`Ch 2`):** Sporadic meteors vs annual meteor showers. As comets warm near the Sun, outgassing ice sprays rocky dust along their orbital paths; Earth crosses those fixed orbital ribbons on the same calendar dates each year, making the meteors appear to radiate from one constellation point (the **radiant**).
3. **Halley's Comet (`1P/Halley`) — One Comet, Two Showers (`Ch 3`):** Halley (`15 × 8 km` dark nucleus reflecting just `3%` of light, `76-year` retrograde orbit, last seen in **`1986`**, returning in **`2061`**) crosses Earth's orbital plane twice, leaving a debris stream that gives us **two** annual meteor showers: the **Eta Aquariids** in early May (`peak May 5–6`, `~65.4 km/s` / `40.7 miles/s`, up to `50/hr` in the Southern Hemisphere, `~10/hr` Northern) and the **Orionids** in mid-October (`peak Oct 21–22`, `~66 km/s` / `41 miles/s`, `10–20/hr`).
4. **The Perseids (`109P/Swift-Tuttle`) vs the Geminids' Asteroid Surprise (`3200 Phaethon`) (`Ch 4`):** August's **Perseids** (`peak Aug 12–13`, `59 km/s` / `37 miles/s`, `50–100/hr`, connected to its parent comet by **Giovanni Schiaparelli in `1865`**) come from the `26-km` (`16-mile`) nucleus of **Comet 109P/Swift-Tuttle** (`133-year` orbit, last perihelion `1992`, next `2125`). By contrast, mid-December's **Geminids** (`active Dec 4–17`, `peak Dec 13–14`, `35 km/s` / `22 miles/s`, up to `120/hr` under dark skies, first recorded in the mid-1800s) come **not** from an icy comet, but from near-Earth **asteroid `3200 Phaethon`** (`1.4-year` orbit — a "rock comet" or dead comet shedding rocky debris close to the Sun).
5. **Fireballs and Meteorites — What Actually Reaches the Ground (`Ch 5`):** Meteoroids brighter than Venus (apparent magnitude brighter than `-3` / `-4`) are **fireballs** (or bolides). Fragile icy comet dust almost never survives to the ground; the **meteorites** (`pebble to fist-sized` and larger) that survive atmospheric entry to hit Earth's surface originate almost entirely from tougher rocky or metallic **asteroids** (and much more rarely impacts on the Moon or Mars).
