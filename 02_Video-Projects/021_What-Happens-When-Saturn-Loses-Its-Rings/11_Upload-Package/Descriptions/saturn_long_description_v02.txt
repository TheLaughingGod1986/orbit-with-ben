Saturn's rings are made of ice, and they are raining into the planet right now. How long do they have left?

Saturn's rings are not a solid disc. They are water ice, chunks from grains to boulders, in a sheet only about ten metres thick. Astronomers using the Keck telescope measured ring rain falling into this gas giant along its magnetic field. Cassini's Grand Finale dives found a heavier leak from the D ring into Saturn's equator. Put together, the rings have somewhere between about 100 and 300 million years left. They won't last forever.
