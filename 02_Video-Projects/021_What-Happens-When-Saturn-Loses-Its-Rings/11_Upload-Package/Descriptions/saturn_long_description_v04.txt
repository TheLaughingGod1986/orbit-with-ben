Saturn's rings are made of ice, and they are raining into the planet right now. How long do they have left?

The rings are a crowd of water-ice chunks, from grains to boulders, spread tens of thousands of kilometres wide and only about ten metres thick in places. They are leaking in two ways. Charged grains follow Saturn's magnetic field into the upper atmosphere as "ring rain", measured from Earth with the Keck telescope. A heavier flow pours off the innermost D ring into Saturn's equator, which Cassini flew through on its 22 last dives in 2017. Together they give the rings roughly 100 to 300 million years. Their age is still debated.
