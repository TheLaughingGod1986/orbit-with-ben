### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **Solar Energy Output vs. Interplanetary Vacuum Density (NASA *Sun Fact Sheet*, D. R. Williams `2024`; IAU `2015` Resolution B3 `Prša et al. 2016`, *AJ* `152`, `41`; `Kopp & Lean 2011`, *Geophys. Res. Lett.* `38`, `L01706`):**
  - The Sun's core fuses hydrogen into helium at **`~15 million °C` (`1.57 × 10⁷ K` / `27 million °F`)**, and its visible photosphere has an effective temperature of **`5,772 K` (`~5,500 °C` / `9,930 °F`)**, radiating a total bolometric luminosity of **`3.828 × 10²⁶ watts`** into space (`IAU 2015 Resolution B3`).
  - At Earth's orbital distance (`1 AU` = `149.6 million km`), incoming total solar irradiance (`TSI`, the solar constant) is **`1,361 watts per square metre`** (`Kopp & Lean 2011`).
  - However, **temperature** is the measure of the average translational kinetic energy of atoms and molecules (`(3/2) k_B T`), whereas **heat transfer** in a vacuum occurs only via **electromagnetic radiation** (no conduction or convection). At sea level on Earth, air contains **`~2.5 × 10¹⁹` molecules per cubic centimetre** (`Loschmidt constant` `2.6868 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³` at `0 °C`, `1 atm`), which absorb solar and infrared radiation and collide billions of times per second to share thermal energy. By contrast, the solar wind in interplanetary space at `1 AU` contains only **`~5–10` protons and electrons per cubic centimetre** (`and <1 atom/cm³ in interstellar space`), meaning solar photons pass straight through empty space without warming the vacuum itself.
- **Boiling and Freezing in Low Earth Orbit (`+121 °C` to `-157 °C`) & NASA's `EMU` Spacesuit Thermal Control (NASA JSC-28432 *ISS Familiarization Manual*; NASA NP-2015-05-022-JSC *ISS Reference Guide*; `Thomas & McMann 2012` *U.S. Spacesuits*):**
