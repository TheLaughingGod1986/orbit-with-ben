### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **Space Transportation System (`STS`) Architecture (NASA SP-2011-613 *Wings in Orbit*, `Hale et al. 2011`; NASA SP-4225 *The Space Shuttle Decision*, `Heppenheimer 1999`; Jenkins 2016 *Space Shuttle: Developing an Icon*):**
  - Authorized by President Richard Nixon and NASA Administrator **James C. Fletcher** (`1919–1991`) on **5 January 1972** and engineered under NASA Johnson Space Center director **Christopher C. Kraft Jr.** (`1924–2019`) and Rockwell International chief engineer **Maxime A. Faget** (`1921–2004`), the **Space Shuttle** had four main elements:
    1. **The Orbiter Vehicle (`OV`):** `37.24 metres` (`122.17 ft`) long with a `23.79-m` (`78.06-ft`) delta wingspan, an **`18.29 × 4.57 m` (`60 × 15 ft`) payload bay**, a dry weight of `~68.5–78 tonnes` (`~109–110 tonnes` max landing weight), and three throttled **Rocketdyne RS-25 (`SSME`)** liquid-hydrogen/liquid-oxygen engines (`1,859 kN` / `418,000 lbf` thrust each at sea level, `2,279 kN` / `512,300 lbf` in vacuum; specific impulse `452.3 s` in vacuum), plus two `26.7-kN` Aerojet **Orbital Maneuvering System (`OMS`)** hypergolic engines.
    2. **The External Tank (`ET`):** `46.9 metres` (`153.8 ft`) tall and `8.4 m` (`27.6 ft`) wide (`~26.5 tonnes` empty on the `Super Lightweight Tank`), feeding **`~730 tonnes` (`~1.6 million lb`, `~2.03 million litres`)** of cryogenic `LH₂` (`-253 °C`) and `LOX` (`-183 °C`) to the three RS-25 engines for `~8.5 minutes` before being jettisoned to burn up over the Indian or Pacific Ocean (the only major non-reusable component).
    3. **Two Solid Rocket Boosters (`SRBs`):** `45.46 metres` (`149.16 ft`) tall, `590 tonnes` (`1.3 million lb`) each, burning ammonium perchlorate composite propellant (`APCP`) to generate **`~12.45–14.68 meganewtons` (`2.8–3.3 million lbf`) of thrust each** (`~83%` of total `~30.5 MN` liftoff thrust) for **`124 seconds`**, separating at `~45 km` (`146,000 ft`) altitude and parachuting into the Atlantic for recovery and refurbishment.
    4. **Thermal Protection System (`TPS`):** **`~24,300` unique silica-fibre tiles** (`LI-900` at `144 kg/m³` — **`94%` pure air by volume** — and denser `LI-2200` / `FRCI-12`) across the belly (`HRSI` black tiles up to **`1,260 °C`**) and upper fuselage (`LRSI` white tiles / `FIB` blankets), plus **`44` Reinforced Carbon-Carbon (`RCC`) panels** along the nose cap and wing leading edges enduring up to **`1,650 °C` (`3,000 °F`)** during `Mach 25` (`~28,000 km/h`) atmospheric entry, ending in an unpowered `1.5:1` to `4.5:1` lift-to-drag **"flying brick" glide** touching down at **`343–380 km/h` (`185–205 knots` / `213–236 mph`, typical `~361 km/h` / `195 knots`)**.
- **Orbiter #1 (Atmospheric Test Glider) — `Enterprise` (`OV-101`, `1977`):**
  - Rolled out at Palmdale on **17 September 1976** without engines or a functional heat shield, **`Enterprise` (`OV-101`)** flew **5 manned free-glide Approach and Landing Tests (`ALT`)** at Edwards Air Force Base between **12 August and 26 October 1977** (commanded by **Fred Haise** & **Gordon Fullerton** and **Joe Engle** & **Richard Truly**), releasing from the back of NASA's modified **Boeing 747 Shuttle Carrier Aircraft (`SCA`)** at `~6,000–7,300 m` (`20,000–24,000 ft`) to prove a `75-tonne` delta-wing spacecraft could glide and land dead-stick on a runway.
- **Orbiter #2 — `Columbia` (`OV-102`, `28` missions, `300.7 days` in space, `1981–2003`):**
  - First space-rated Shuttle orbiter (heavier by `~3.6 tonnes` due to early structural wingspar instrumentation). On **12 April 1981** (`20th` anniversary of Yuri Gagarin's flight), **John W. Young** (`1930–2018`) and **Robert L. Crippen** (`born 1937`) launched on **`STS-1`** — the only time in history a brand-new American spacecraft system flew astronauts on its very first orbital launch — landing `54.5 hours` (`36` orbits) later at Edwards AFB after `16` tiles were lost and `148` damaged.
  - Across **28 missions**, `Columbia` flew the first European **Spacelab** (`STS-9`, `1983`), the longest Shuttle flight in history (**`STS-80`**, `17 days, 15 hours, 53 minutes` in `November–December 1996`), the first female Shuttle commander **Eileen M. Collins** (`born 1956`) deploying the **`22.7-tonne` (`50,162-lb` with Inertial Upper Stage) `Chandra` X-Ray Observatory** (`STS-93`, `July 1999`), and Hubble Servicing Mission `3B` (`STS-109`, `March 2002`).
  - On **16 January 2003** (`STS-107`), `81.7 seconds` after liftoff at `~20 km` altitude (`Mach 2.46`), a **`0.76-kg` (`1.67-lb`) briefcase-sized piece of BX-250 bipod-ramp insulating foam** broke off the External Tank and struck `Columbia`'s left-wing leading edge at `~877 km/h` (`545 mph`) relative velocity, breaching **RCC Panel 8** (later proven by the **Columbia Accident Investigation Board [`CAIB`, August 2003]** firing a `0.76-kg` foam block at `840 km/h` into a spare RCC panel at Southwest Research Institute, punching a `41 × 42 cm` hole). During re-entry on **1 February 2003** at `~61.8 km` (`203,000 ft`) and `Mach 18–23`, superheated plasma penetrated the left wing spar, destroying the orbiter and claiming all `7` crew members (**Rick Husband**, **William McCool**, **Michael Anderson**, **Ilan Ramon**, **Kalpana Chawla**, **David Brown**, **Laurel Clark**).
- **Orbiter #3 — `Challenger` (`OV-099`, `10` missions, `62.3 days` in space, `1983–1986`):**
  - Converted from structural test article `STA-099`, **`Challenger`** made its maiden flight on **`STS-6`** (**4 April 1983**, first Shuttle EVA by Story Musgrave and Donald Peterson and deployment of `TDRS-1`), carried **Sally K. Ride** (`1951–2012`, first American woman in space on **`STS-7`**, `June 1983`) and **Guion S. Bluford Jr.** (`born 1942`, first African American in space on **`STS-8`**, `August 1983`), and on **`STS-41-B`** (**7 February 1984**) saw **Bruce McCandless II** (`1937–2017`) fly untethered **`98 metres` (`320 ft`)** from the orbiter using the nitrogen-thruster **Manned Maneuvering Unit (`MMU`)**.
  - On **28 January 1986** (`STS-51-L`), launching after an overnight freeze left the right Solid Rocket Booster aft field joint at **`-0.6 °C` (`31 °F`)** (far below the `11.7 °C` / `53 °F` minimum tested experience base), cold-stiffened **fluoroelastomer (`Viton`) primary and secondary O-rings** failed to seal (`Rogers Commission Report, June 1986`, demonstrated live by physicist **Richard P. Feynman** [`1918–1988`] dipping an O-ring clamp into ice water). Hot gas burned through the right SRB aft attachment strut and ruptured the External Tank at **`73.124 seconds` (`14.6 km` altitude, `Mach 1.92`)**, claiming all `7` crew members (**Francis "Dick" Scobee**, **Michael J. Smith**, **Judith Resnik**, **Ellison Onizuka**, **Ronald McNair**, **Gregory Jarvis**, and Teacher in Space **Christa McAuliffe**).
- **Orbiter #4 — `Discovery` (`OV-103`, fleet-leader `39` missions, `365.5 days` in space, `238.5 million km`, `1984–2011`):**
  - NASA's workhorse orbiter (`first flight STS-41-D`, **30 August 1984**; `final flight STS-133`, **24 February – 9 March 2011**, now displayed at the Smithsonian Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center).
  - Deployed the **`11.1-tonne` Hubble Space Telescope** (`STS-31`, **24 April 1990**, reaching a record `615-km` Shuttle altitude), launched the **`Ulysses`** solar polar probe (`STS-41`, `October 1990`), flew two Hubble servicing missions (`STS-82` in `1997` and `STS-103` in `1999`), carried `77-year-old` Mercury astronaut **John H. Glenn Jr.** (`1921–2016`) back to orbit on **`STS-95`** (`October 1998`), and flew **both "Return to Flight" missions** after `Challenger` (**`STS-26`**, `September 1988`) and `Columbia` (**`STS-114`**, `July 2005`, commanded by Eileen Collins, performing the first on-orbit `360°` Rendezvous Pitch Maneuver belly inspection).
- **Orbiter #5 & #6 — `Atlantis` (`OV-104`, `33` missions, `1985–2011`) & `Endeavour` (`OV-105`, `25` missions, `1992–2011`):**
  - **`Atlantis` (`OV-104`, `33` missions, `306.6 days` in space, `202.6 million km`):** Launched interplanetary probes **`Magellan`** to Venus (`STS-30`, `May 1989`) and **`Galileo`** to Jupiter (`STS-34`, `October 1989`), deployed the **`17-tonne` Compton Gamma Ray Observatory** (`STS-37`, `April 1991`), performed **`7` consecutive `Atlantis` dockings with Russia's `Mir` space station** (`STS-71` in `June 1995` through `STS-86` in `1997`, out of `9` total Shuttle–`Mir` dockings including `Discovery`'s `STS-89` and `STS-91`), delivered the US **`Destiny` laboratory module** to the `ISS` (`STS-98`, `2001`), flew the fifth and final **Hubble Servicing Mission (`STS-125`, `May 2009`)**, and flew the **final Space Shuttle mission in history (`STS-135`, `8–21 July 2011`**, commanded by **Christopher Ferguson**).
  - **`Endeavour` (`OV-105`, `25` missions, `296.1 days` in space, `197.8 million km`):** Built from structural spare parts (`1987–1991`) to replace `Challenger`. On its maiden mission (**`STS-49`**, `May 1992`), astronauts **Pierre Thuot**, **Richard Hieb**, and **Thomas Akers** performed the only **three-person spacewalk** in history to capture the stranded `Intelsat VI` satellite by hand. On **`STS-61` (`2–13 December 1993`)**, `Endeavour`'s crew (**Story Musgrave**, **Jeffrey Hoffman**, **Kathryn Thornton**, **Thomas Akers**, **Claude Nicollier**, **Richard Covey**, **Kenneth Bowersox**) completed **`5` back-to-back spacewalks (`35 hours, 28 minutes`)** to install **`COSTAR`** (`Corrective Optics Space Telescope Axial Replacement`) and **`WFPC2`**, correcting Hubble's **`2.2-micrometre` primary-mirror spherical aberration** (`~1/50th` the width of a human hair). `Endeavour` also launched the first American `ISS` module (**`Unity` `Node 1`** on **`STS-88`**, `December 1998`, joining Russia's `Zarya` across **`37` Space Shuttle `ISS` assembly and logistics flights**).
