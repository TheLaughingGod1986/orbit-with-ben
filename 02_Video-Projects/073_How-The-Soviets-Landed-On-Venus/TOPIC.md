### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`)
- **Crushed in the Clouds — `Venera 4`, `5` & `6` (`1967–1969`, NPO Lavochkin / `Georgy Babakin`, `Avduevsky et al. 1968`, *JAS* `25`, `537–545`; `Kliore et al. 1967`, *Science* `158`, `1683`; NASA NSSDCA):**
  - On **18 October 1967**, **Venera 4** (`383 kg` descent capsule designed by **Georgy Babakin**, `1914–1971`) became the first spacecraft to transmit direct measurements from inside another planet's atmosphere (`90–95% CO₂`), but its hull was rated for `25 atmospheres` and failed after `93 minutes` at **`~25 km` (`24.96 km` / `24–28 km` after Mariner 5 radar-altimeter reconciliation) altitude** (`~18–22 atm`, `262–271 °C`).
  - **Venera 5** (`16 May 1969`) and **Venera 6** (`17 May 1969`) were reinforced to `25–27 atm` and crushed at `~18–20 km` altitude after extrapolation showed surface conditions reached **`~470 °C` and `~90 atmospheres`**.
- **First Soft Landing on Another Planet — `Venera 7` & `Venera 8` (`1970–1972`, `Avduevsky et al. 1971`, *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences* `28`, `263–269`; `Marov et al. 1973`, *Icarus* `20`, `407–421`):**
  - **Venera 7** (`495 kg` lander, titanium alloy shell tested to **`180 atmospheres`** and `540 °C`): On **15 December 1970**, after its reefed fibreglass parachute partially tore during descent, Venera 7 struck the night side of Venus at **`~16.5 m/s` (`~37 mph`)**, rolled onto its side (`1%` signal strength), and transmitted surface temperature (**`475 ± 20 °C`**) and pressure (**`90 ± 15 atm`**) for **`23 minutes`** — the **first soft landing on another planet**.
  - **Venera 8** (`22 July 1972`, `50 minutes` on the day side): Measured surface illumination with a photometer (`~1–3%` of top-of-atmosphere sunlight — like an overcast afternoon on Earth, proving surface photography was possible without floodlights) and gamma-ray spectrometry (`K`, `U`, `Th`).
- **First Surface Panoramas & Aerobraking — `Venera 9` & `Venera 10` (`October 1975`, `Keldysh 1977`, *Icarus* `30`, `605–625`):**
  - Redesigned `1,560-kg` descent craft (`660-kg` surface lander) inside a `2.4-m` spherical heat shield: because Venus's lower `CO₂` atmosphere has a density of **`~65 kg/m³`** (`~6.5%` the density of liquid water), the lander jettisoned its three parachutes at **`50 km` altitude** and descended the rest of the way using only a **rigid annular metal aerobraking disc** (`reducing descent time so the pre-chilled `-10 °C` interior wouldn't overheat`) and a crushable metallic torus landing ring.
  - **Venera 9** landed on **22 October 1975** on a `20°` slope of angular basaltic boulders (`Beta Regio`) and operated for **`53 minutes`**, returning the **first photograph ever taken from the surface of another planet** (`180°` black-and-white scanning telephotometer panorama through a quartz periscope window, while its second camera's lens cap failed to eject).
  - **Venera 10** landed **25 October 1975** (`65 minutes` on the surface), photographing pancake-like lava slabs (`1` of `2` lens caps also stuck).
- **The Lens-Cap Curse & Thunder — `Venera 11`, `Venera 12` & NASA `Pioneer Venus` (`December 1978`):**
  - **Pioneer Venus Multiprobe** (`9 December 1978`, NASA): The un-parachuted **`90-kg` Day Probe** survived impact at `~35 km/h` and transmitted from the surface for **`67 minutes, 37 seconds`** (no camera).
  - **Venera 11** (`25 December 1978`, `95 minutes`) and **Venera 12** (`21 December 1978`, `110 minutes`) carried colour cameras, but **all four camera lens caps failed to release** because standard pressure-equalisation seals trapped high-pressure gas under the caps.
- **Colour, Sound & the `Venera 14` Lens Cap — `Venera 13` & `Venera 14` (`March 1982`, `Moroz 1983`, *Space Science Reviews* `35`, `3–9`; `Surkov et al. 1983`, *JGR* `88`, `A481`; `Ksanfomaliti 1983`):**
  - **Venera 13** landed on **1 March 1982** at `7.5° S, 303° E` (`Phoebe Regio`) where temperature was **`457 °C` (`855 °F`)** and pressure was **`89 atmospheres` (`~90 bar`)**. Pre-chilled to `-10 °C` with lithium-nitrate-trihydrate phase-change heat sinks inside its titanium sphere, it operated for **`127 minutes` (`2 hours, 7 minutes` — `4×` its `32-minute` design life)**, returning the **first 360° colour panoramas of Venus** (red-green-blue filtered scans designed by **Arnold Selivanov**), drilling a soil sample into a `0.05-atm` chamber for X-ray fluorescence (`leucitic basalt`), and recording the **first acoustic sound of wind on another planet**.
  - **Venera 14** landed `950 km` away on **5 March 1982** (`13.25° S, 310° E`, **`465 °C` and `94 atmospheres`**, surviving **`57 minutes`**): both redesigned pyrotechnic titanium camera lens caps popped off cleanly — but in a famous `1-in-a-million` coincidence, one **`12-cm` white titanium lens cap landed directly at the base of the spacecraft on the exact spot where the spring-loaded `PROP-V` soil penetrometer / acoustic-electrical arm deployed**, so Venera 14 spent its surface mission measuring the compressibility of its own camera lens cap instead of Venusian rock.
- **The Final Landers & Balloons — `Vega 1` & `Vega 2` (`June 1985`, `Sagdeev et al. 1986`, *Science* `231`, `1407–1411`):**
  - On **11 June** and **15 June 1985**, Soviet **Vega 1** and **Vega 2** dropped two surface landers (`Vega 2` operated `56 minutes` on the surface in northern Aphrodite Terra, drilling anorthosite-troctolite rock) and deployed **two `3.54-m` Teflon-coated helium superpressure balloons** at **`54 km` altitude** (`~10–35 °C`, `~0.5 atm`), which drifted `>11,000 km` across Venus for **`46.5 hours`** at `~250 km/h` (`69 m/s`) before the parent probes flew on to Halley's Comet. No spacecraft has landed on Venus since `15 June 1985`.
