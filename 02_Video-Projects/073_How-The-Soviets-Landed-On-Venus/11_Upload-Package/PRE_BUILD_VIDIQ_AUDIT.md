**Chapter list after audit** (5 chronological acts, each with a teach-point + `Teach on screen` markers):

1. **Crushed in the Clouds: `Venera 4` to `Venera 6` (`1967–1969`)** — When chief designer **Georgy Babakin** (`WHO: Georgy Babakin (1914–1971) — NPO Lavochkin chief designer`) sent **Venera 4** into Venus's clouds on **18 October 1967** (`WHEN: 18 October 1967`), its hull was built for `25 atmospheres`. After `93 minutes`, the air crushed it **`25 kilometres` above the ground** (`DATA` #1), revealing a `96.5% CO₂` atmosphere reaching **`~470 °C` and `90 atmospheres`** at the surface (`SCALE: 900 metres under Earth's ocean`).
2. **The First Soft Landing on Another Planet: `Venera 7` & `Venera 8` (`1970–1972`)** — Rebuilt as a **`495-kg` titanium sphere** tested to **`180 atmospheres`**, **Venera 7** plunged into Venus on **15 December 1970** (`WHEN: 15 December 1970`). Its parachute tore in the heat, dropping the probe the last stretch in free fall to hit the ground at **`16.5 m/s` (`37 mph`)** (`FACT: Parachute tore and still survived`). Rolling onto its side, its faint `1%` carrier signal transmitted **`475 °C` and `90 atmospheres` for `23 minutes`** — before **Venera 8** (`22 July 1972`, `50 minutes`) proved enough orange daylight (`1–3%` of sunlight) reached the ground for cameras.
3. **Falling Without a Parachute: `Venera 9` & `Venera 10` (`October 1975`)** — To get through the oven before the lander cooked, Soviet engineers (`WHO: Mikhail Marov (1933–2023) — Venera atmospheric scientist`) used Venus's thick lower air (**`65 kg/m³`**, `DATA` #2 + `SCALE: 6.5% the density of liquid water, ~50x thicker than Earth's air`): **Venera 9** (`22 October 1975`, `WHEN: 22 October 1975`) jettisoned its parachutes **`50 km` up** and fell on a flat metal aerobraking ring, using a quartz periscope window to transmit the **first black-and-white photograph ever taken on another planet's surface** (`53 minutes`), followed three days later by **Venera 10** (`65 minutes`).
4. **The Lens-Cap Curse & 127 Minutes in Colour: `Venera 11–13` (`1978–1982`)** — In **December 1978** (`WHEN: December 1978`), **Venera 11** (`95 min`) and **Venera 12** (`110 min`) landed cleanly, but **all four camera lens caps stayed sealed** under the `90-bar` pressure. Fixing the pressure-release bolts, **Venera 13** touched down on **1 March 1982** (`WHEN: 1 March 1982`) at **`457 °C` and `89 atmospheres`** (`WHO: Arnold Selivanov (1935–2019) — Venera camera designer`), using lithium-nitrate phase-change heat sinks to survive **`127 minutes`** (`DATA` #3 + `SCALE: 4x its 32-minute design life`), capturing the **first 360° colour panoramas** and the **first microphone recording of Venusian wind**.
5. **`Venera 14`'s 1-in-a-Million Shot & the `Vega` Balloons (`1982–1985`)** — Four days later on **5 March 1982** (`WHEN: 5 March 1982`), **Venera 14** (`57 minutes` at `465 °C` and `94 atm`) popped its titanium lens cap straight onto the spot where its spring-loaded soil penetrometer unfolded (`FACT: Tested its own lens cap`). In **June 1985** (`WHEN: June 1985`), **Vega 1 and 2** (`WHO: Roald Sagdeev (born 1932) — Vega scientific director`) landed on the surface and floated two helium balloons at `54 km` for `46.5 hours` across `11,000 km` — the last machines to touch Venus before future landers return.
