Venus is Earth's twin: almost the same size, made of the same rock, and it even soaks up less sunlight than Earth does. Yet its surface is hot enough to melt lead. So what happened to Venus?

Its air is about 96% carbon dioxide, ninety times thicker than ours, and the heat cannot get out: a runaway greenhouse. Earth holds about as much carbon dioxide, locked away in limestone and seabed by water. Venus has lost its water. Pioneer Venus found its heavy hydrogen (deuterium) about a hundred times richer than Earth's, the fingerprint of water broken apart and leaked to space. Whether Venus ever had oceans is still open: one climate model says shallow seas for billions of years, another says the steam never came down. DAVINCI, VERITAS and EnVision are designed to go back and read the rocks.
