So why Venus and not Earth?

Distance is part of it. Nearer the Sun, there is more light to start the climb. Warmer air holds more water vapour, and water vapour is itself a greenhouse gas. Past a certain line, the steam reaches high enough for sunlight to split it, and the water starts leaking away to space. Venus sits on the wrong side of that line.

Earth sits on the safe side, for now. Last week we saw the Sun getting brighter, about one percent every hundred and ten million years. That climb moves the line outward. In about a billion years, it reaches Earth.

This is not a warning about next century. The warming measured on Earth today is real, and it is a different ledger. Studies of Earth's climate find that burning our fossil fuels is very unlikely to start a full runaway like this. Venus is a story told in hundreds of millions of years.

What it shows is how thin the difference is. Same rock, same start, and one turn with the water.
