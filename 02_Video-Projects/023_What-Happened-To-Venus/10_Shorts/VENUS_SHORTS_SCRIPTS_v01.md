## 1. Mon 26 Oct: Venus Absorbs Less Sunlight Than Earth

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. NASA Mariner 10 Venus globe already turning, the bright cloud deck catching the light. Whoosh. Caption: LESS LIGHT. No Orbit.]

Venus soaks up less sunlight than Earth does.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Magellan radar plains. Orbit (Omni only) may enter small, visor tilted against the heat. If Omni is down, stay on the world.]

And it's the hottest planet in the solar system.

[VISUAL MUST: The cloud deck. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

It gets almost twice our sunlight, but its bright clouds bounce three-quarters of it away.
The air is thick carbon dioxide, ninety times heavier than ours.
Four hundred and sixty-five degrees.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening globe.]

The full film: What Happened to Venus?

[TEACH: About 1.9× Earth's sunlight, Bond albedo about 0.77, so Venus absorbs less sunlight than Earth. About 96.5% CO₂ at about 92 bar; about 464 °C (NASA NSSDCA).]

## 2. Wed 28 Oct: Could a Robot Survive on Venus?

Uses the proven "Could a Robot…" slot. **The robot beat needs Omni.** If Omni is down, the Short runs on the world pictures, and the VO stands either way.

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. Magellan's Maat Mons perspective view, already pushing in. Whoosh. Caption: TWO HOURS. No Orbit.]

The longest any machine has lasted on Venus is just over two hours.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Orbit (Omni) hovers small over the plain in the haze, one hand up against the orange light.]
[ORBIT ACTS: Orbit dips towards the ground, feels the heat, and pulls back up.]

Could a robot survive there?

[VISUAL MUST: Lava plains under orange haze. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

The ground is hot enough to melt lead.
The air presses like the sea nearly a kilometre down.
In 1982, a Soviet lander sent back colour photos, then the heat won.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to Maat Mons.]

The full film: What Happened to Venus?

[TEACH: Venera 13 (1 March 1982) lasted 127 minutes and returned colour images (NASA NSSDCA). Lead melts at 327 °C; the surface is about 464 °C at about 92 bar. Venera imagery is used only if its reuse is clear; otherwise NASA plates only.]

## 3. Fri 30 Oct: Venus Lost Its Water and Left a Clue

[VISUAL MUST: 0:00. The Venus cloud tops in motion (NASA). Whoosh. Caption: THE CLUE. No Orbit.]

Venus lost its water. And it left a clue.

[VISUAL MUST: About 1.5 s. Pioneer Venus probe art (NASA) falling through the cloud.]

In 1978, a probe fell through its clouds and tasted the air.

[VISUAL MUST: Simple text-free graphic: two hydrogen atoms, one heavier. The exact title is on screen from 9 to 14 s.]

Some hydrogen is heavier.
On Venus, the heavy share is a hundred times Earth's.
Sunlight split the water, and the light hydrogen escaped to space.

[VISUAL MUST: The last 3 s return to the opening cloud tops.]

The full film: What Happened to Venus?

[TEACH: Pioneer Venus measured D/H about 100× Earth's (Donahue et al. 1982). Photolysis and hydrogen escape preferentially lose H over D. The Short says Venus lost water. It does not say how much, or whether it was ever an ocean.]
