## Primary Sources

1. **NASA Science — Miranda Overview (`https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/miranda/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Miranda's `about 500 km in diameter` (`only one-seventh as large as Earth's moon`), `roughly equal amounts of water ice and silicate rock`, `slightly inclined` orbit, `smallest and closest to the planet` of the five Uranian moons known before Voyager 2, reflectivity (`none of them reflect more than about a third of the sunlight that strikes them`, darkened by carbonaceous material, porous surface brightening at opposition), discovery by `Gerard P. Kuiper on Feb. 16, 1948 at the McDonald Observatory in western Texas`, naming for `the daughter of Prospero in William Shakespeare's play, "The Tempest"`, `three large features known as "coronae," which are unique among known objects in our solar system`, separated from heavily cratered terrain by sharp boundaries `like mismatched patches on a moth-eaten coat`, giant fault canyons `as much as 12 times as deep as the Grand Canyon`, `a full 10 minutes` for a rock dropped off the edge of the highest cliff to reach the foot of the cliff, and early collisional breakup/reassembly vs. meteorite-melted slushy water hypotheses.

2. **NASA Science — Uranus Moons: Facts (`https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/facts/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Miranda's exact mean diameter (`293.0 (miles)` / `471.6 (kilometers)`), the five major moons (`Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, and Oberon`), and distance from the Sun (`about 1.8 billion miles (2.9 billion km) away from the Sun`).

3. **NASA Science / Astronomy Picture of the Day (APOD) — *Verona Rupes: Tallest Known Cliff in the Solar System* (Nov. 29, 2020, `https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2020-november-29-verona-rupes-tallest-known-cliff-in-the-solar-system/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Robert Nemiroff & Jerry Bonnell / NASA Science.
   - **Used for:** Verona Rupes as the tallest known cliff in the Solar System, estimated depth of `20 kilometers` (`ten times the depth of the Earth's Grand Canyon`), `about 12 minutes` fall time for a thrill-seeking adventurer from the top, reaching the bottom at `the speed of a racecar -- about 200 kilometers per hour`, survivable given `proper airbag protection`, and capture by the passing `Voyager 2 robotic spacecraft in 1986`.

4. **NASA / JPL Photojournal — `PIA00044`: *Miranda High Resolution of Large Fault* (`https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/miranda-high-resolution-of-large-fault/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).
   - **Used for:** Voyager 2 closest-approach clear-filter narrow-angle image acquired `Jan. 24, 1986` from `36,250 kilometers (22,500 miles)` at `660 meters (2,160 feet)` resolution, ridges and valleys produced by compressional tectonics, largest fault scarp below and right of centre (`5 km (3 mi) high, or higher than the walls of the Grand Canyon on Earth`), `slickensides` grooves made by contact of fault blocks as they rubbed against each other, and movement of the down-dropped block shown by the offset of the ridges.

5. **NASA / JPL Photojournal — `PIA01490`, `PIA00042`, `PIA00141`, `PIA01354`, `PIA00039`, `PIA18185`, `PIA01361` (`https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/south-polar-view-of-miranda/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/miranda-highest-resolution-color-picture/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mirandas-geologic-history-variety-of-terrain/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/uranus-innermost-satellite-miranda/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/titania-highest-resolution-voyager-picture/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mirandas-icy-face/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/uranus-montage-of-uranus-five-largest-satellites/`)**

6. **Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) News Release — *Uranus' Moon Miranda May Have an Ocean Beneath Its Surface, New Study Finds* (Oct. 28, 2024, reporting *Planetary Science Journal* study published Oct. 16, 2024, `https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/241028-uranus-moon-miranda-with-ocean-beneath-surface-new-study`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Jeremy Rehm / Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (reporting study by Caleb Strom, University of North Dakota; Tom Nordheim, JHU APL; and Alex Patthoff, Planetary Science Institute, *Planetary Science Journal*, Oct. 16, 2024).
