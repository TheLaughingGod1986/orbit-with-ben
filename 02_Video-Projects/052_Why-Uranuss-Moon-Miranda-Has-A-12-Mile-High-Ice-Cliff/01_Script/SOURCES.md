# Sources — 052 Why Uranus's Moon Miranda Has a 12-Mile-High Ice Cliff

Every number, date, named person, physical scale, and factual claim in `01_Script/miranda_ice_cliff_script_master_v00.md` is backed below by primary NASA Science, NASA JPL Photojournal, NASA APOD, Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL), and Library of Congress Name Authority sources with verbatim quotes.

## Primary Sources

1. **NASA Science — Miranda Overview (`https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/miranda/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Miranda's ~500 km diameter (`one-seventh as large as Earth's moon`), equal amounts of water ice and silicate rock, slightly inclined orbit, smallest and closest of the five Uranian moons known before Voyager 2, reflectivity (`none of them reflect more than about a third of the sunlight that strikes them`), discovery by Gerard P. Kuiper on Feb. 16, 1948 at McDonald Observatory in western Texas, naming for Prospero's daughter in William Shakespeare's *The Tempest*, three unique `coronae` separated from heavily cratered terrain like `mismatched patches on a moth-eaten coat`, fault canyons `as much as 12 times as deep as the Grand Canyon`, `a full 10 minutes` for a dropped rock to reach the foot of the highest cliff, and early collisional breakup/reassembly vs. meteorite-melted slushy water hypotheses.

2. **NASA Science — Uranus Moons: Facts (`https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/facts/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science Editorial Team, National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA).
   - **Used for:** Miranda's exact mean diameter (`293.0 miles` / `471.6 kilometers`), the five major moons (`Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, and Oberon`), and distance from the Sun (`about 1.8 billion miles (2.9 billion km) away from the Sun`).

3. **NASA Science / Astronomy Picture of the Day (APOD) — *Verona Rupes: Tallest Known Cliff in the Solar System* (Nov. 29, 2020, `https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2020-november-29-verona-rupes-tallest-known-cliff-in-the-solar-system/`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Robert Nemiroff & Jerry Bonnell / NASA Science.
   - **Used for:** Verona Rupes as the tallest known cliff in the solar system, estimated depth of `20 kilometers` (`12 miles`, `ten times the depth of the Earth's Grand Canyon`), `about 12 minutes` fall time for a thrill-seeking adventurer from the top, reaching the bottom at `the speed of a racecar -- about 200 kilometers per hour`, survivable with `proper airbag protection`, and capture by the passing `Voyager 2 robotic spacecraft in 1986`.

4. **NASA / JPL Photojournal — `PIA00044`: *Miranda — High Resolution of Large Fault* (`https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/miranda-high-resolution-of-large-fault/` / `https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA00044`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).
   - **Used for:** Voyager 2 closest-approach clear-filter narrow-angle image acquired `Jan. 24, 1986` from `36,250 kilometers (22,500 miles)` at `660 meters (2,160 feet)` resolution, ridges and valleys produced by compressional tectonics, largest fault scarp below and right of centre (`5 km (3 mi) high, or higher than the walls of the Grand Canyon on Earth`), and `slickensides` grooves where fault blocks rubbed against each other.

5. **NASA / JPL Photojournal — `PIA00141`, `PIA18185`, `PIA01490`, `PIA00042`, `PIA00039` (`https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mirandas-geologic-history-variety-of-terrain/`, `https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/mirandas-icy-face/`, `https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA01490`, `https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA00042`, `https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA00039`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory / U.S. Geological Survey (Flagstaff, Arizona).
   - **Used for:** JPL Navigation Ancillary Information Facility camera-pointing stabilization and USGS Flagstaff image registration (`PIA01490`), Miranda southern hemisphere colour view at `147,000 kilometers (91,000 miles)` and impact craters `30 km (20 mi)` and `5 to 10 km (3 to 6 mi)` in diameter (`PIA00042`), Inverness Corona `chevron` (`PIA01490`), and Titania comparison at `369,000 kilometers (229,000 miles)` and `13 km (8 mi)` resolution (`>1,600 km / 1,000 mi` diameter, `PIA00039`).

6. **Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU APL) News Release — *Uranus' Moon Miranda May Have an Ocean Beneath Its Surface, New Study Finds* (Oct. 28, 2024, reporting *Planetary Science Journal* study published Oct. 16, 2024, `https://www.jhuapl.edu/news/news-releases/241028-uranus-moon-miranda-with-ocean-beneath-surface-new-study`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (reporting study by Caleb Strom, Tom Nordheim, and Alex Patthoff, *Planetary Science Journal*, Oct. 16, 2024).
   - **Used for:** Three named coronae (`Arden`, `Elsinore`, `Inverness`), Voyager 2 imaging only Miranda's `southern hemisphere (the only part we've seen)` in `1986`, subsurface ocean `100-500 million years ago` at least `62 miles (100 kilometers) deep` beneath an icy crust `no more than 19 miles (30 kilometers) thick` inside Miranda's `radius of just 146 miles (235 kilometers)` (`filled almost half of the moon's body`), orbital resonances with nearby moons (`deform the moons like rubber balls`, compared with Jupiter's 2:1 Io-Europa resonance), absence of freeze-expansion cracks indicating a possible thin remnant ocean today, 2023 Ian Cohen Voyager 2 particle/magnetic reanalysis, and 2024 Richard Cartwright JWST Ariel carbon-dioxide ice study.

7. **NASA Science — *Gerard Kuiper* (`https://science.nasa.gov/people/gerard-kuiper/`) & Library of Congress Name Authority (`http://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n50046182` & `http://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n78095332`)**
   - **Authors / Publisher:** NASA Science & Library of Congress.
   - **Used for:** `WHO:` card dates and roles for Gerard Kuiper (`1905–1973`, Dutch-American planetary astronomer) and William Shakespeare (`1564–1616`, English playwright and poet).
