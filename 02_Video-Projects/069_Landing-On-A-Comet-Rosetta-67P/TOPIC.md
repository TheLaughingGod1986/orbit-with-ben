### 2.5 Primary Scientific & Agency Fact Sheet (Pre-Verified for `SOURCES.md`, `J2911` FIX 1, 3, 6, 7 Applied)
- **Biele et al. (`2015`, *Science* `349`, `aaa9816`, *"The landing(s) of Philae and inferences about comet surface mechanical properties"*):**
  - On **12 November 2014**, ESA's `100-kg` **Philae** lander touched down on Comet `67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko` at `Agilkia` (`TD1`) at **`15:34:04 UTC`** (`~1.0 m/s` descent speed) at `510 million km` from Earth (`3.0 AU` from the Sun, where `67P`'s orbital speed was `~18.5 km/s` / `~66,600 km/h`).
  - Local effective surface gravity at `Agilkia`: **`g ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ m/s²`** (`~10⁻⁴ to 5 × 10⁻⁴ m/s²` across the nucleus, roughly **`50,000×` to `100,000×` weaker than Earth's `9.81 m/s²`**), so the `100-kg` lander weighed **`~1 to 2 grams`** (about a paperclip).
  - **Four-event landing sequence (`J2911` FIX 7):** Because the cold-gas top thruster and anchor harpoons did not fire at `TD1` (`15:34:04 UTC`, outgoing vertical speed `0.325 m/s`), Philae rebounded up to `~1 km` altitude and travelled `~1 km` across the lobe, **grazed/collided with the Hatmehit crater rim at `16:20 UTC`**, made its **second touchdown (`TD2`) at `17:25:26 UTC`** (`1 hour 51 minutes` after `TD1`), and came to **final rest (`TD3`) at `Abydos` at `17:31:17 UTC`** (`64 hours` of primary battery science before hibernation).
- **Sierks et al. (`2015`, *Science* `347`, `aaa1044`) & Pätzold et al. (`2016`, *Nature* `530`, `63–65`):**
  - Comet `67P` is a contact binary ("rubber duck") with overall dimensions **`4.3 × 2.6 × 2.1 km`** (large lobe `4.1 × 3.3 × 1.8 km`, small lobe `2.6 × 2.3 × 1.8 km`), mass **`9.982 × 10¹² kg` (`~10 billion tonnes`)**, bulk density **`533 kg/m³`** (half the density of water ice, with **`72–75%` internal porosity**), rotation period **`12.404 hours`**, and surface albedo **`~6%`** (darker than coal).
  - At **perihelion on 13 August 2015** (`1.24 AU` / `186 million km` from the Sun), `67P` reached its maximum orbital speed of **`37.5 km/s` (`135,000 km/h`)** (ESA Rosetta fact sheet).
- **ESA/Rosetta/OSIRIS `1 June 2016` Sequence (`J2911` FIX 1 & 2):**
  - Captured on **1 June 2016** by Rosetta's `OSIRIS` narrow-angle camera over `25 minutes` from **`13 km` from the comet's centre** (`~11 km` above the surface, outbound past `3.1 AU`): shows sunlit **dust and ice particles drifting in the foreground close to the camera**, **background stars in the constellation Canis Major** trailing downward as the comet/spacecraft move, and high-energy **cosmic-ray pixel hits** above `67P`'s cliff terrain.
- **Altwegg et al. (`2015`, *Science* `347`, `1261952`; `2016`, *Science Advances* `2`, `e1600285`):**
  - Rosetta's `ROSINA` mass spectrometer measured a deuterium-to-hydrogen (`D/H`) ratio in `67P`'s water vapour of **`(5.3 ± 0.7) × 10⁻⁴` — more than three times (`3.4×`) Earth's ocean water (`1.5576 × 10⁻⁴`)**, showing that Jupiter-family comets like `67P` did not deliver the bulk of Earth's oceans; `ROSINA` also detected the amino acid **glycine**, phosphorus, and molecular oxygen (`O₂`).
