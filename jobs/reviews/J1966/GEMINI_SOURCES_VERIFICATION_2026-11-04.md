### Gap 3 — Sidereal vs. Solar Day Definitions, Historical vs. Modern Precision, Viewing Reference Direction, & NASA Science's `"Sunrise to Sunset"` Error (`FLAG` on Rows 1, 5, 6, 7)

1. **`science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/` `"Sunrise to Sunset Would Take 117 Earth Days"` vs. Script `"Sunrise to Sunrise Takes 116.75 Earth Days"` (`FLAG` on `SOURCES.md` Primary #1 / Row 1 Quote):**
   - By definition, a **solar day** ($P_{\text{solar}}$) is the period of one complete diurnal cycle from **noon to noon** (or **sunrise to sunrise**), related to the retrograde sidereal rotation period ($P_{\text{sid}} = -243.0226\text{ d}$) and sidereal orbital period ($P_{\text{orb}} = 224.701\text{ d}$) by:
     $$\frac{1}{P_{\text{solar}}} = \frac{1}{|P_{\text{sid}}|} + \frac{1}{P_{\text{orb}}} = \frac{1}{243.0226\text{ d}} + \frac{1}{224.701\text{ d}} = \frac{1}{116.750\text{ d}} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P_{\text{solar}} = \mathbf{116.750\text{ Earth days}} = \mathbf{2,802.0\text{ hours}}.$$
   - NASA NSSDC `venusfact.html` correctly reports `"Length of day (hrs): 2802.0 ... Ratio: 116.750"`, and the script narration (`v01` Lines 7, 20, 30, 36) correctly states that **sunrise to sunrise** (one full solar day) takes **`116.75 Earth days` (`2,802.0 hours`)**.
   - However, the outreach text on `science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/` quoted in `SOURCES.md` Primary #1 (`"because of the planet's extremely slow rotation, sunrise to sunset would take 117 Earth days"`) contains a **factual error on NASA's public outreach page**: **sunrise to sunset** (the daylight half of the solar day) lasts **half** of `116.75 Earth days`, or **`~58.375 Earth days` (`~58.4 Earth days`)**, whereas **sunrise to sunrise** lasts `116.75 Earth days` (`~117 Earth days`)! `SOURCES.md` must explicitly flag that `science.nasa.gov`'s `"sunrise to sunset"` phrase is an outreach error for `"sunrise to sunrise"`.

2. **Historical vs. Modern Sidereal Rotation Periods & Hour Conversion Precision (`FLAG` on Unexplained Precision Mixing):**
   - Across `v01` and `SOURCES.md`, six different determinations of Venus's retrograde sidereal rotation period appear:
     - **`240 days (±20%)`:** `Goldstein & Carpenter (March 8, 1963, Science 139:910–911, PMID 17743054)` from Oct–Dec 1962 Goldstone radar.
     - **`243.09 ± 0.18 days`:** `I. I. Shapiro (July 28, 1967, Science 157:423–425, PMID 17798698)` from 1961–1966 radar data.
     - **`243.0185 ± 0.0001 days` (`−5,832.44 hours`):** `M. E. Davies et al. (1992, JGR 97:13141)` from Magellan's `~500-day` radar landmark baseline (`1990–1992`).

3. **Viewing Reference Direction for `"Clockwise"` vs. `"Counterclockwise"` (`FLAG` per `J1807` Item 3 & `J1808` Gap 1):**
   - `v01` Lines 3, 32, 34, 36, 38, and `SOURCES.md` Row 6 describe Venus's retrograde rotation as `"clockwise"` and its orbital motion as `"counterclockwise"` **without stating the viewing reference frame**.
   - In 3D space, a rotation is only `"clockwise"` or `"counterclockwise"` when the observer's vantage point is specified: **viewed from above the North Ecliptic Pole (or above the Sun's / Earth's north pole)**, Venus orbits the Sun counterclockwise (prograde) while spinning on its axis **clockwise** (retrograde, since its IAU north pole of rotation lies in the southern ecliptic hemisphere at obliquity $177.36^\circ$).

4. **Synodic Near-Resonance (`5.001444` Venusian Solar Days, `243.0226 d` Actual vs. `243.16 d` Exact Resonance) vs. Exact `"Presenting the Same Face Every Conjunction"` (`FLAG` on Script Line 36):**
   - `Shapiro (1967, PMID 17798698)` noted that exact spin-orbit resonance with Earth's synodic period (`583.92 Earth days`) would require a sidereal rotation period of **`243.16 Earth days`** (making exactly 4 retrograde sidereal rotations relative to the Earth–Venus line, or exactly `5.000000` Venusian solar days per `583.92-day` synodic period).
