## Journey 2: outward through the Solar System

**Working idea:** *From the Moon to Pluto*: a calm trip outward, one world at a time, each further from the Sun than the last.

**Films, in order (seven, none used in Journey 1):** 013 Moon, 030 Apophis (a near-Earth visitor), 020 Jupiter, 021 Saturn's rings, 032 Titan, 033 Uranus, 034 Pluto. About 60 minutes at 8 to 10 minutes each.

**Outline.** The film starts on the nearest world, the Moon, slowly pulling away from us, and follows that outward motion. An asteroid that will pass inside our satellites marks the edge of Earth's neighbourhood. Then the giants: a fall into Jupiter where there is no ground to reach, Saturn's rings on a clock, and a landing on Titan under an orange sky. Past them the Sun shrinks: Uranus and its 21-year winters, and last Pluto, where a frozen heart with no craters shows the far edge is still alive. Each part is further out than the one before, so the distance from the Sun is the film's one running number, shown as a counter on the chapter cards, the way Journey 1 counted distance from Earth.

**No new voice.** Journey 1 had a short linking script between parts. Journey 2 can link with chapter cards that carry the distance and the next world's name, and no spoken link. If the Chief wants spoken links, that is a small voice job and waits for the result like everything else.

**Its difference from Journey 1:** Journey 1 left the Solar System after part 3. This one stays inside it, which matches what our best longs share (named Solar System worlds).

## Sleep 2: the quiet outer worlds and the deep sky

**Working idea:** *From Titan to the Last Star*: slow, low-stakes stories that move from the cold outer worlds to the far future.

**Films, in order (none used in Sleep 1):** 032 Titan, 033 Uranus, 034 Pluto, 006 Europa, 003 Exoplanets (21 min), 004 JWST galaxies (17 min), 005 The Last Star. About 80 minutes; add 064 Every planet at the front as a gentle opener for about 90.

**Outline.** It opens under Titan's thick air, where everything moves slowly, then drifts out past Uranus and its long seasons to Pluto's ice plains. It turns inward once, to the dark ocean under Europa's ice, then leaves the Solar System for the strangest planets found round other stars and the first galaxies seen by JWST. It ends on the last red dwarf burning down over trillions of years, the slowest story we have, so the film gets quieter and slower as it goes. The order runs from near to far and from now to the far future, the same rule as Sleep 1.

**Left out on purpose:** 002 Black hole, 007 Neutron star and 030 Apophis. Their stories are about danger to a body or to Earth, which does not suit a film to fall asleep to.

**No new voice.** Every part is a finished film's own narration. Follow whatever recipe Sleep 1 was built with (its build files were not read in this run).
