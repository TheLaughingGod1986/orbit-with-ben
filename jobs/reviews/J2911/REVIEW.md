## OWB:068 — FIX list (identified by `macbook-2`, resolved in `TOPIC.md` by `gemini`)

1. **Short S02 title was false (`RESOLVED`).** *"Why Is Mercury's Night Cold Enough to Freeze Nitrogen?"* Nitrogen freezes at about `−210 °C` (`63.15 K`) and boils at about `−195.8 °C` (`77.36 K`). A `−180 °C` (`93 K` / `100 K`) equatorial night does not liquefy or freeze nitrogen. Retitled `S02` to **`Why Is the Planet Closest to the Sun So Cold at Night?`** (`54 chars`, `0` exact YouTube collisions, aligned with the `2.84M`-view Astrum neighbour `YQ7DWNhjOpk`) and fixed its section heading to match the cold-night script.
2. **Short S01 gave the mean orbital speed instead of perihelion speed (`RESOLVED`).** `47.36 km/s` is the NASA NSSDCA fact sheet's *mean* orbital speed (`38.86 km/s` at aphelion `69.82 million km`). At perihelion (`46.00 million km`), Mercury accelerates to **`58.98 km/s` (`~59 km/s`)**, which is why its orbital angular velocity exceeds its `58.65-day` spin for `~8 Earth days`. Updated §2.3, §2.5, and `S01` (including the `5.5–8.5 s` overlay **`59 KM/S MAX SPEED`**, `4 words`) to `59 km/s`.
3. **Search-gap realism recorded (`RESOLVED`).** Updated `068/TOPIC.md` §2.2 to record honestly that while big channels have not made a dedicated `8–10 min` documentary titled *How Long Is a Day on Mercury?*, `evidence_2026-11-15_public_search.json` shows six recent small-channel `6:43–10:04` uploads on this exact question sitting at `3–134` views (`394` on a `3:45` clip) — reinforcing `studio/plan.json`'s decision to pause single-question longs after `034` Pluto until the multi-body/journey/sleep format tests read out.
