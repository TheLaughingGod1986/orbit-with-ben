Description
How far is the nearest star? Proxima Centauri, a small red dwarf 4.24 light-years away, is so faint you can never see it without a telescope. Even light, the fastest thing there is, takes more than four years to cross the gap.

We meet Proxima and its bright neighbours Alpha Centauri A and B, find out how parallax and the Gaia spacecraft measure a distance that large, from Friedrich Bessel's first star distance in 1838 to more than a billion stars today, and shrink the light-year down to a grapefruit Sun and a cherry-sized star thousands of kilometres away. Then the journey: how long it would take on foot, by car, by jet, at the speed of Voyager 1 and at the speed of light, and what waits at the end, the planet Proxima b and the Breakthrough Starshot plan to reach it.
