## 1. Specific Verification of J3520 Chief Ruling Tests

1. **Jupiter Planets-Only Mass Comparison (Line 52, Annotations Lines 58–59, Row 9):** **PASS (Script) / MINOR TABLE FORMAT FLAG (`SOURCES.md` Row 9)**
   - **Script Text:** Line 52 reads `"more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined"`; `[VISUAL MUST]` (line 58) and `[TEACH]` (line 59) both state `(>2× the mass of all the other planets combined)`.
   - **Live Primary Source:** `https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/` states Jupiter is `"more than twice as massive as the other planets combined."` (10 words). This removes the solar-mass inclusion error from `v02`.
   - **`SOURCES.md` Note:** Row 9 appends `https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/` in an unheaded 8th trailing table column while leaving the formation-context quote from `https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/` in column 7.

2. **Galileo Probe Data-Return vs. Transmission Failure vs. Crushing (Line 56, Annotations Lines 58–59, Row 10):** **PASS**
   - **Script Text:** Line 56 reads: `"On 7 December 1995, NASA's Galileo probe descended into Jupiter's atmosphere and returned data for fifty-eight minutes. Far deeper, pressure squeezes hydrogen into an electrically conducting liquid."`
   - **Annotations:** Line 58 (`[VISUAL MUST]`) specifies `"a 58-minute data-return timeline; show no crushing event, no depth/pressure endpoint, and no descent to the far-deeper metallic hydrogen region."` Line 59 (`[TEACH]`) notes `"Data duration is not a crushing time or depth; no linked 153 km/22.7 atm/71 km endpoint."`
   - **Live Primary Source:** `https://science.nasa.gov/mission/galileo-jupiter-atmospheric-probe/` states `"The probe hit the atmosphere Dec. 7, 1995, and returned valuable data for 58 minutes."` (15 words) and separately reports that `"The probe's transmitter failed 61.4 minutes after entry"` (8 words) at about 180 km below its entry ceiling. No reference levels (entry ceiling vs. 1-bar level vs. 71 km cloud deck) are conflated, and loss of transmission is no longer equated with physical crushing at 58 minutes.

3. **Earth Equatorial Scope and Named 408 km/h Cyclone Olivia Gust (Line 28, Annotations Lines 32–34, Row 5):** **PASS**
   - **Script Text:** Line 28 reads: `"Tilted twenty-three point four degrees, Earth spins every twenty-four hours. At the equator, the surface moves at about one thousand six hundred and seventy kilometres per hour — more than four times the four-hundred-and-eight-kilometre-per-hour gust measured during Cyclone Olivia. Rotation makes Earth forty-three kilometres wider across the equator than pole to pole."`
   - **Annotations:** Lines 32 and 34 preserve the equatorial scope (`1,670 km/h equatorial spin`) and the named Cyclone Olivia 408 km/h gust (`1,670 ÷ 408 = 4.09×`).
   - **Math & Live Primary Sources:** Using NSSDCA Earth parameters (`https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html`), equatorial circumference is $2\pi \times 6,378.137\text{ km} = 40,075.02\text{ km}$, giving $1,674.36\text{ km/h}$ per sidereal day ($23.9345\text{ h}$) or $1,669.79\text{ km/h}$ per 24-hour solar day ($\approx 1,670\text{ km/h}$), and equatorial minus polar diameter is $2 \times (6,378.137 - 6,356.752) = 42.77\text{ km} \approx 43\text{ km}$. Against the WMO/Geoscience Australia record non-tornadic gust of $408\text{ km/h}$ (`https://www.ga.gov.au/education/natural-hazards/tropical-cyclone-and-severe-wind`), $1,670 / 408 = 4.093\times > 4\times$.

4. **Neptune Roughly-Five Comparison (Line 89, Annotations Lines 91–92, Row 16):** **PASS**
   - **Script Text:** Line 89 reads: `"methane clouds whipped past two thousand kilometres per hour — roughly five times the speed of Cyclone Olivia's record gust"`.
   - **Annotations:** Lines 91–92 explicitly state `(roughly five times the speed; 2,000 ÷ 408 ≈ 4.90)` and note that `"a lower bound does not establish >5×"`.
   - **Math & Live Primary Sources:** `https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/neptune-facts/` gives a lower bound of `"speeds of more than 1,200 miles per hour (2,000 kilometers per hour)."` (12 words). Dividing $2,000\text{ km/h}$ by Cyclone Olivia's $408\text{ km/h}$ yields $4.902\times$, so `"roughly five times"` is accurate without treating a rounded $400\text{ km/h}$ as an exact denominator or asserting a strict $>5\times$ inequality from a $>2,000\text{ km/h}$ lower bound. The Voyager 2 (25 August 1989 Neptune flyby) vs. New Horizons (July 2015 Pluto flyby) distinction is also preserved.
