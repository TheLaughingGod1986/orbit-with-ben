What Happens When a Star Passes Through Our Solar System?
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F-jImX5h7ws

Gliese 710 is predicted to send about 10 extra comets a year into the inner Solar System for three to four million years — yet the comet shower peaks only after the star has already passed and is heading away again, because falling in from the distant Oort Cloud takes hundreds of thousands of years.

Sources: Berski & Dybczyński (2016), Astronomy & Astrophysics 595 L10 — predicted Oort Cloud comet flux (~10 observable comets/yr lasting 3–4 Myr) (https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201629835) · Bailer-Jones (2022), The Astrophysical Journal Letters 935 L9 — Gaia DR3 encounter distance and timing (https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac816a) · NASA Science, Oort Cloud Facts — the distant comet reservoir (https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/oort-cloud/facts/).

Pictures: NASA STS-83 Space Shuttle photograph of Comet Hale-Bopp over Earth's airglow limb, NASA ISS photograph of Comet C/2023 A3 Tsuchinshan-ATLAS by Matthew Dominick, and ESO La Silla Observatory night-sky star-trails time-lapse by R. Wesson (CC BY 4.0), alongside original animated explainers and AI-assisted voice and editing. Facts: every number is checked against the sources listed above.
Image credits: NASA/JSC; NASA/Matthew Dominick; R. Wesson/ESO (CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
