[VISUAL MUST: Cold open on moving picture from the five films' own shots, one per line: Saturn's ring plane (021), the Moon over Earth (013), the Andromeda approach (019), a neutron star flash (007), a dim red star (005). Nothing you can see in the night sky is permanent.

Not Saturn's rings. Not the Moon, where it hangs tonight. Not even the stars.

Saturn's rings are raining into the planet right now. The Moon drifts away from us by about three point eight centimetres every year. Our galaxy is heading for a meeting with Andromeda. And every star will one day go out, even the very last one.

So how long does each of them have?

That's this film. Five stories, one countdown. We start with the shortest clock in the sky, a few hundred million years, and end with the longest: the last starlight, about ten trillion years from now.

Every one of those clocks is still ticking. First, the rings.
