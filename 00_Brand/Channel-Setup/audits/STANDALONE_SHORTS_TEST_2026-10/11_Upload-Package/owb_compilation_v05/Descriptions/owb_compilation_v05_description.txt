Nothing you can see in the night sky is permanent — from Saturn's ice rings raining into the planet right now to the last red dwarf stars shining ten trillion years from now. How long does each of them have left?

This full-length countdown walks five clocks across the universe, from the shortest to the longest: Saturn's vanishing rings (100 to 300 million years), the Moon slowly drifting away from Earth at 3.8 cm a year and ending the window of perfect total solar eclipses, the Milky Way's future collision with Andromeda billions of years from now, the ultra-dense neutron stars left behind when giant stars explode, and the quiet red dwarfs that will outlive every other star in the cosmos.
