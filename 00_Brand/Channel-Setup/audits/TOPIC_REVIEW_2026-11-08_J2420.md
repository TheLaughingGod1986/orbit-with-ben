## Fixes that carry into the script and Shorts

### 062 Phobos
1. **Pick one decay rate.** The hook says "two centimetres a year"; §2.3 and the Short overlay say 1.8 cm/yr. Use the sourced figure, NASA's ~1.8 m per century (1.8 cm/yr), everywhere.
2. **Altitude vs radius for the Roche limit.** The brief gives Phobos's height as ~6,000 km *above the surface* (right: orbit radius ~9,376 km from Mars's centre), then gives a Roche limit of "~5,400 km" with no frame. Roche limits are measured from the planet's centre, and for a rubble pile the value depends on the model. State one sourced figure, in one frame, or drop the number and say "too close to hold together".
3. **Size.** "A seventeen-mile moon": Phobos is about 27 × 22 × 18 km (mean ~22 km, ~14 miles). 17 miles is only its longest axis. Say "about 22 kilometres across" (the brief uses that figure elsewhere).
4. **Don't call the grooves solved.** The 2015 NASA Goddard work (tidal stretch marks) is one explanation. Others say the grooves come from rolling Stickney debris, and that's still debated. Short 2 says the 2015–2016 work "proved" it. Say "one leading idea" and source it. The Short 2 title's "Giant Cracks" is fine if the line says "grooves".
5. **Short overlay is 5 words** (`1.8 CM CLOSER EVERY YEAR`). The cap is 4 words. Try `1.8 CM A YEAR`.
- Fine as written: 7 h 39 m orbit vs a 24 h 37 m sol, rising in the west about twice a sol, 30–50 million years (matches NASA, and 044's source row), Stickney ~9 km, HiRISE `PIA10368`, Perseverance eclipse footage (2022).
- Rule 18: the variant *What NASA's Closest Photos of Phobos Revealed* makes no "only/first/final" claim, so it's fine as is.

### 063 Saturn hexagon
1. **The eye is ~20×, not 50×.** NASA describes the central vortex as about 2,000 km across, roughly **20 times** an average Earth hurricane eye. §2.3 and Short 1 both say "fifty times". Fix.
2. **Voyager saw the hexagon, not the eye.** Short 1's payoff says the central hurricane "has spun without breaking since Voyager 1 and 2 first spotted it in 1980–1981". Voyager found the **hexagon**. Cassini imaged the polar vortex at its centre much later (infrared 2008, visible 2012). Rewrite so the 40+ years belongs to the hexagon.
3. **One width figure.** The brief has 30,000 km, "20,000-mile" (≈32,000 km) and "wider than two Earths" (Earth 12,742 km). They roughly agree, but the script and the thumb text must use one sourced number and its exact conversion. The variant title *Why Does Saturn Have a 20,000-Mile Hexagon?* is metric/imperial mixed against the rest of the house (km). Prefer km or "Two Earths wide".
4. **Present the mechanism as the leading explanation**, not settled: a jet stream meandering into a standing wave, reproduced in rotating-tank lab experiments, with models still debated on depth. The Short 1 overlay `6 SMALL VORTICES PINCH THE JET` is **6 words** (cap 4) and states one model as fact. Try `A BENT JET STREAM`.
5. **Short 2 title length is wrong in the brief.** *Why Did Saturn's Giant Hexagon Turn From Blue to Gold?* is 54 characters, not 49. Trim it, e.g. *Why Did Saturn's Hexagon Turn Gold?*
6. **Season length.** "7-year winter in total darkness": a Saturn season is ~7.4 years, but the pole isn't dark for the whole season. Say "years of polar night". Check `PIA21049` is the 2012/2016 colour pair before it goes in the shot list.
- Fine as written: ~320 km/h (200 mph) jet, six sides each ~14,500 km, the colour change from blue (2012) to gold (2016) as spring sunlight made haze, a ~10 h 39 m rotation link, and Cassini moving imagery for frame 0.
