### Notes on each

1. **What Would Happen If You Landed on Titan?** (Runner-up in J0251, literal hand-off at the end of `031`, and our #1 pick for `032`.) Directly backed by scheduled standalone Short `RLSJm59OSzI` (*Standing on Titan Feels Like Diving 15 Metres Down*, airing 10 Nov 2026) and riding the `021` Saturn-system Browse wave (+125 views in 24 h). Titan is the only moon in the solar system with a thick atmosphere (1.45 times Earth's surface pressure) and the only world besides Earth with standing liquid rivers, lakes and seas on its surface. Every familiar earthly rule is flipped: you do not need a pressure suit (only an oxygen mask and heavy insulation against −179 °C), water is frozen harder than granite bedrock, liquid methane falls as slow-motion rain the size of marbles, and in 14% of Earth's gravity (0.138 g) inside air four times denser than Earth's at the surface, a human wearing wings could fly by flapping their arms. Rich historical people and dates (`WHO`, `WHEN`): Christiaan Huygens (1629–1695) discovering Titan in 1655, Gerard Kuiper (1905–1973) detecting methane in 1944, ESA's *Huygens* probe parachuting onto Titan on 14 January 2005 and broadcasting for 72 minutes from a damp methane riverbed before *Cassini* set below the horizon, *Cassini* radar plumbed Kraken Mare at over 300 metres deep, and NASA's nuclear octocopter *Dragonfly* (launching 2028, arriving 2034). Subject words: *Titan Saturn moon, landing on Titan, Huygens probe footage, liquid methane lakes, Kraken Mare, NASA Dragonfly*.
2. **Why This Planet Has 21-Year Winters (Uranus).** Tied on points (24/30) and backed by scheduled standalone Short `ZpEA8stoSPA` (airing 3 Nov 2026). An Earth-sized impactor tilted Uranus by 97.77 degrees (~98°), so it rolls around the Sun on its side during its 84-year orbit, giving each pole 42 years of continuous sunlight and 42 years of freezing darkness. Explains why Uranus holds the coldest planetary atmosphere in the solar system (−224 °C, colder than further-out Neptune) and a lopsided magnetic field tilted 59° from its spin axis. Strong history (William Herschel 1781, Voyager 2 in January 1986) and zero overlap with `001`–`031`. Natural hand-off after `032` Titan.
