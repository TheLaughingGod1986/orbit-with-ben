### Idea 1 (Rank #1 — Scaffolded as `051`): *Why NASA's DART Spacecraft Changed an Asteroid's Orbit by 33 Minutes*
- **Title shape:** Why/How search question (`68` characters; script H1 `# Why Did NASA's DART Spacecraft Change an Asteroid's Orbit by 33 Minutes?`, front-loading `NASA's DART Spacecraft` and `Asteroid's Orbit by 33 Minutes`).
- **Thumbnail concept:** Real NASA/ASI LICIACube + Hubble impact plume erupting from rubble-pile asteroid moonlet Dimorphos beside spinning-top parent Didymos (`3 words: 33 MINUTES SHORTER`).
- **0:00–0:08 Hook:** Six point eight million miles from Earth where a five-hundred-and-sixty-foot moonlet of loosely packed boulders loops around a half-mile spinning-top asteroid, as NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test spacecraft slams head-on into the rock at three point eight miles per second on September 26, 2022, why did NASA's DART spacecraft change an asteroid's orbit by thirty-three minutes?
- **Midpoint twist (`"Wrong."`):** Before impact, engineers might have expected DART to punch a simple crater into solid stone and nudge the moonlet purely by the momentum of the half-ton spacecraft. **Wrong.** Images taken 53 miles away by the Italian Space Agency's shoebox-sized LICIACube and analysed in an August 21, 2025 NASA study revealed that Dimorphos is a weak "rubble pile" whose surface is less than 300,000 years old: the crash blasted 35.3 million pounds (16 million kilograms) of rock and dust into space — 30,000 times the mass of the spacecraft — acting like a rocket engine that doubled the spacecraft's punch, reshaped the moonlet from a squashed ball into an oblong watermelon, and shortened its orbit by 33 minutes and 15 seconds.
- **7 Number Objects:**
  1. **33 minutes (33 minutes and 15 seconds) vs 73 seconds** minimum success benchmark (surpassed by **more than 25 times**), shortening Dimorphos's **11-hour-and-55-minute** orbit around Didymos after DART's **3.8-mile-per-second (`6.1 km/s` / `14,000 mph`)** impact **6.8 million miles (`11 million km`)** from Earth on **September 26, 2022**.
  2. **805-metre (`half-mile`) Didymos vs 170-metre (`560-foot`) Dimorphos** orbiting **3,900 feet (`1,189 metres`)** apart, where Didymos spins once every **2.26 hours** and has a surface age of **12.5 million years** vs Dimorphos at **less than 300,000 years** (`40 to 130 times` older).
  3. **7 miles (`12 kilometres`) / 2 seconds before impact** final full DRACO frame (`100-foot-wide` patch) after launching **November 24, 2021** on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg and deploying **LICIACube** **15 days** before the crash.
  4. **35.3 million pounds (`16 million kilograms`)** of ejected dust and rock — **30,000 times greater than the mass of the spacecraft** (`<0.5%` of Dimorphos's mass, with almost **45%** hidden in the opaque inner plume) — imaged by LICIACube from **53 miles (`85.3 km`)** away at **15,000 mph (`21,140 km/h`)** and doubling the spacecraft's momentum punch (`momentum enhancement factor ~2`).
  5. **33 minutes and 15 seconds** final orbital reduction (from `11h 55m` to `11h 22m 37s` immediately, settling at `11h 22m 03s` ± `1.5 s`), shrinking the orbital radius by **120 feet (`37 metres`)** to **3,780 feet (`1,152 metres`)** and reshaping Dimorphos into an oblong watermelon.
  6. **37 free-flung boulders** ranging from **3 feet to 22 feet** across (excavated from a circle ~**160 feet** across, the width of a football field, and representing **2%** of surface boulders and **0.1%** of Dimorphos's mass) drifting away at **a little more than half a mile per hour** — the walking speed of a giant tortoise.
  7. **0.15 seconds** change in the binary pair's **770-day (`2.11-year`)** orbit around the Sun — a speed shift of **11.7 microns per second (`1.7 inches per hour`)** measured across **22 stellar occultations** between **October 2022 and March 2025** (`March 6, 2026` *Science Advances* study).

### Idea 2 (Rank #2 — Recommended for `052`): *Why Uranus's Moon Miranda Has a 12-Mile-High Ice Cliff*
- **Hook:** Just 290 miles (470 kilometres) across — one-seventh the diameter of Earth's Moon — Uranus's innermost major moon Miranda boasts Verona Rupes, a sheer fault scarp up to 12 miles (20 kilometres) high, ten times deeper than the Grand Canyon, where a rock dropped from the rim takes 12 minutes to hit the floor.
- **Midpoint twist:** After Voyager 2 flew past Miranda on January 24, 1986, planetary scientists thought the tiny moon had been shattered apart by a giant impact and reassembled inside-out. **Wrong.** Modern tectonic and tidal-heating models (including 2024 Johns Hopkins APL findings) show orbital resonance with neighbouring Uranus moons flexed a thin ice crust over a subsurface ocean up to 62 miles (100 kilometres) deep within the last 100 to 500 million years.

### Idea 3 (Rank #3 — Recommended for `053`): *Why NASA's Parker Solar Probe Flew Inside the Sun's Corona without Melting*

- **Midpoint twist:** How a million-degree plasma fails to melt a spacecraft because the corona's particle density is so low that heat transfer is governed by radiant sunlight rather than conductive gas, while magnetic "switchbacks" whip solar wind out of coronal holes.
