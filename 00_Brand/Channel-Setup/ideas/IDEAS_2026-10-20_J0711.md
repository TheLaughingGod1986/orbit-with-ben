At Mars (`54.6 million` to `401.4 million km` from Earth), a one-way signal takes `3 to 22 minutes`. On **November 18, 2026**, **Voyager 1** (launched **September 5, 1977**, cruising at **61,000 km/h**) reaches **one light-day** from Earth (**25.9 billion kilometers** / **16.1 billion miles** after **49 years, 2 months, and 11 days**), where a one-way radio command takes **24 hours** (**48 hours** round-trip). And at **Proxima Centauri** (**4.24 light-years** / **40.15 trillion kilometers**), a one-way signal takes **4.24 years** (**8.5 years** round-trip).
     - **Deep Space 1, AutoNav, and Remote Agent (NASA/JPL 1998–2001):** Launched **October 24, 1998** to test **12 new technologies**, NASA's **Deep Space 1** carried **Autonomous Optical Navigation (AutoNav)** and the **Remote Agent** artificial-intelligence system, flying within **26 kilometers** (**16 miles**) of asteroid **9969 Braille** at **15.5 km/s** on **July 29, 1999**, and within **2,171 kilometers** (**1,350 miles**) of **Comet 19P/Borrelly** at **16.58 km/s** on **September 22, 2001** while operating its xenon ion engine for **16,265 hours** (**4.3 km/s** delta-v).
     - **Perseverance's Off-World Driving Record & AEGIS Autonomous Laser Science on Mars (NASA/JPL Oct. 12, 2026 Release; Francis, Estlin et al. 2017 *Science Robotics*, `PMID 33157897`):** On **October 11, 2026** (**Sol 2,006**), NASA's **Perseverance** rover reached **45.22 kilometers** (**28.10 miles**), breaking **Opportunity**'s **45.16-kilometer** (**28.06-mile**) off-world driving record in **5.5 years** vs **nearly 15 years** — driving **more than 90%** of its distance using **AutoNav** (`"thinking while driving"` at up to **412 meters** in a single sol and processing **600,000 measurement points** per look vs **20** on **Sojourner** in **1997** and **~5%** AutoNav share on **Spirit** and **Opportunity**). Meanwhile, JPL's **AEGIS** (*Autonomous Exploration for Gathering Increased Science*, deployed since **May 2016** by **Raymond Francis** and **Tara Estlin**) analyses Navcam images after drives to fire **ChemCam** autonomously without Earth in the loop, boosting desired geological target hits over **2.5 kilometers** of unexplored terrain from **24%** (blind targeting) to **over 93%**.
     - **Autonomous Hypervelocity Intercept at 6.1 km/s — DART's SMART Nav (Daly, Ernst, Barnouin, Chabot, Rivkin et al., *Nature* 2023, `PMID 36858073` / `PMC10115643`):** On **September 26, 2022**, **11 million kilometers** from Earth, NASA's **DART** spacecraft handed complete control to its **Small-body Maneuvering Autonomous Real Time Navigation (SMART Nav)** system **4 hours and 5 minutes** before impact (`19:09:24 UTC`). Using **DRACO** camera images onboard, SMART Nav first detected the **170-meter** moonlet **Dimorphos** beside **805-meter** **Didymos** just **73 minutes** before impact, began manoeuvring toward Dimorphos **50 minutes** before impact, locked its final course **2.5 minutes** before impact, and struck at **6.1 kilometers per second** (`23:14:24 UTC`) to shorten Dimorphos's **11-hour 55-minute** orbit by **33 minutes**.
     - **Laser-Driven Nanocraft & Self-Replicating Probes Across the Galaxy (Kevin Parkin 2018, *Acta Astronautica* / `arXiv:1805.01306`; John von Neumann `1903–1957`, LOC `n50021202`; Arwen Nicholson & Duncan Forgan 2013, *International Journal of Astrobiology* / `arXiv:1307.1648`):** In the **Breakthrough Starshot** system model (**Kevin Parkin**, **2018**), a **4.1-meter** light sail accelerated for **9 minutes** by a **1.06-micrometer** ground laser array reaches **20% of the speed of light** (`0.2 c`), crossing the **4.24 light-years** to **Alpha Centauri** and **Proxima Centauri** in **about 21 years** — requiring complete onboard AI autonomy during its flyby because Earth is **4.24 years** of radio travel away.
