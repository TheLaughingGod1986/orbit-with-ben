  3. **Concrete quantitative planetary-geology landmarks (100% verbatim-sourced):**
     - **A Shield Volcano Nearly Three Times Everest's Height Over an Area the Size of Arizona or Italy (NASA Science Mars Facts `science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/`, NASA Image Library `PIA00300`, `PIA25476`, `PIA26219`, `PIA24993`, `PIA22181`, & MOLA / Wikipedia `Olympus_Mons`):** While Earth's highest mountain, **Mount Everest**, rises **8,848 meters (29,029 feet)** above sea level and Hawaii's **Mauna Loa** rises **9 kilometers (5.5 miles)** from its base on the ocean floor, Mars's **Olympus Mons** stands **21.287 kilometers (69,840 feet)** above the Martian datum as measured by the **Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MOLA)** (`nearly 22 kilometers` / `14 miles` above surrounding plains, and **26 to 27 kilometers** — `over 25 kilometers` / `16 miles` — above the plains of **Amazonis Planitia** `1,000 kilometers` to the northwest). Its shield edifice spans **600 kilometers (370 miles)** wide across **300,000 square kilometers (120,000 square miles)** — an area as large as **Italy**, the **Philippines**, or the state of **Arizona** — rimmed by a steep perimeter cliff scarp (**Olympus Rupes**, `PIA15443`) up to **6 to 8 kilometers (5 miles)** tall and a **2-kilometer-deep** annular crustal moat.
     - **From Giovanni Schiaparelli's 19th-Century "Olympic Snow" to Mariner 9 and Viking Orbiter 2's 8.7 × 10²¹-Gram Gravity Disk (`Giovanni Virginio Schiaparelli`, `1835–1910`, LOC `n85804243`; `Patrick Moore`, `1923–2012`, LOC `n79082203`; `William L. Sjogren`, LOC `no2007033781`, *Science* March 9, 1979, `PMID 17811123`):** As English astronomer **Patrick Moore** (`1923–2012`) noted, Italian astronomer **Giovanni Virginio Schiaparelli** (`1835–1910`) observed that the bright patch he named **Nix Olympica** (`"Olympic Snow"`) remained visible above global Martian dust storms and guessed it must be high. When NASA's **Mariner 9** entered Mars orbit in **1971** during a global dust storm, the clearing summit of Nix Olympica confirmed it was a giant shield volcano (`Olympus Mons`). On **March 9, 1979**, NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory geophysicist **William L. Sjogren** reported in *Science* (`PMID 17811123`) high-resolution Doppler radio-tracking gravity measurements from **Viking Orbiter 2** (`30°S to 65°N`, `500-kilometer` resolution), modeling Olympus Mons as a **600-kilometer surface disk** with a colossal mass of **$8.7 \times 10^{21}$ grams** (**8.7 billion metric tons**).
     - **Why Earth Cannot Build an Olympus Mons — Stationary Hotspot, 0.38 g Gravity, and a 70-Kilometer Lithosphere (`David E. Smith`, `born 1937`, LOC `nb2012003006`; `Maria T. Zuber`, `born 1958`, LOC `n91103960`; `Sean C. Solomon`, `born 1945`, LOC `nr93014292`; `Roger J. Phillips`, `1940–2020`, LOC `n85352655`; *Science* May 28, 1999 `PMID 10348732`, October 1, 1999 `PMID 10506567`, March 10, 2000 `PMID 10710301`, & March 30, 2001 `PMID 11283367`; NASA Mars Fact Sheet `marsfact.html`):** On Earth (`9.82 m/s²` surface gravity), mobile tectonic plates carry the crust across mantle plumes to form island chains like Hawaii. Mars (`half the diameter of Earth`, surface gravity **`3.73 m/s²`** or **`0.380`** — **38 percent of Earth's gravity**) lacks mobile tectonic plates: its **70-kilometer (43-mile) thick elastic lithosphere** stays fixed over a stationary mantle hotspot while the **Tharsis rise** emplaced **$3 \times 10^8$ (300 million) cubic kilometers** of magma (`PMID 11283367`). At the summit of Olympus Mons, **six nested calderas** form a depression **60 by 80 kilometers (37 by 50 miles)** across and up to **3.2 kilometers (2.0 miles)** deep above a magma chamber **32 kilometers (105,000 feet)** below the floor (`350 to 150 million years` old), while the **5-percent** average flank slope is so gentle that an observer near the summit sees the horizon just **3 kilometers** away, breathing an atmospheric pressure of only **72 pascals** — **12 percent** of Mars's **600-pascal** (`6.36 millibar`) average, compared with **32,000 pascals** (`32 percent` of Earth sea level) on Mount Everest.
     - **The Midpoint Twist ("Wrong.") — 2-Million-Year-Old Lava Flows and Hidden Tropical Glaciers on a "Dead" Volcano (`Gerhard Neukum`, `1944–2014`, LOC `n85125781`, *Nature* December 23, 2004, `PMID 15616551`; `James W. Head`, `born 1941`, LOC `n84047812`, *Nature* March 17, 2005, `PMID 15772652`):** For decades Olympus Mons was assumed to be a long-dead relic wrapped only in dusty landslide aureoles (**Lycus Sulci**, extending **750 kilometers** northwest) and impact craters like **15.6-kilometer (9.7-mile) Karzok crater** (`PIA24993`) and **10.4-kilometer (6.5-mile) Pangboche crater**. On **December 23, 2004**, **Gerhard Neukum** (`1944–2014`) and colleagues reported in *Nature* (`PMID 15616551`) using ESA's **Mars Express High Resolution Stereo Camera (HRSC)** at **10 to 20 meters per pixel** that Olympus Mons and four other major Martian volcanoes resurfaced during the **last 20 percent of Martian history**, with lava flows on Olympus Mons ranging from **115 million years** down to **as young as 2 million years**, suggesting the volcano is **potentially still active today** — alongside glacial deposits at the base of the Olympus Mons escarpment active **4 million years ago** and snow and ice deposition above **7,000 meters**. Then on **March 17, 2005**, **James W. Head** (`born 1941`), **Gerhard Neukum**, and colleagues showed in *Nature* (`PMID 15772652`) that **ice-rich rock glaciers** at the base of the Olympus Mons scarp formed during periods of increased **spin-axis obliquity**, leaving abundant **residual water ice** insulated beneath dust for future exploration.
