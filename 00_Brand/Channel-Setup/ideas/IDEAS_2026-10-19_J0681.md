  3. **Concrete physical experiments, not philosophy fluff:** Grounded in five quantitative, peer-reviewed physics landmarks:
     - **Nick Bostrom (2003, *Philosophical Quarterly*):** The 3-proposition trilemma, brain emulation cost ($10^{14}$ to $10^{17}$ ops/s per brain; $10^{33}$ to $10^{36}$ operations for all $100\text{ billion}$ humans across $50\text{ years}$ at $30\text{ million}$ seconds/year), planetary-mass computer ($10^{42}$ ops/s), and Seth Lloyd's ultimate $1\text{ kg}$ laptop limit ($5 \times 10^{50}$ ops/s on $10^{31}$ bits).
     - **Max Planck & NIST 2022 CODATA:** The Planck length ($1.616255 \times 10^{-35}\text{ m}$) and Planck time ($5.391247 \times 10^{-44}\text{ s}$) — why physicists ask whether spacetime has a minimum resolution, and why Planck units alone do not make space a cubic pixel grid.
     - **Silas Beane, Zohreh Davoudi, and Martin Savage (2012/2014, *European Physical Journal A* 50, 148, `arXiv:1210.1847`):** The first concrete physical test of a cubic space-time lattice using ultra-high-energy cosmic rays at the Greisen–Zatsepin–Kuzmin (GZK) cutoff ($\sim 6 \times 10^{20}\text{ eV}$), proving that if the universe runs on a grid that cuts off cosmic rays, the grid spacing must be $b \lesssim 10^{-12}\text{ fm}$ ($10^{-27}\text{ m}$, energy scale $b^{-1} \sim 10^{11}\text{ GeV}$) and the highest-energy cosmic rays would arrive aligned with the cubic axes of the grid rather than isotropically from all directions.
     - **Aaron Chou & Craig Hogan — Fermilab Holometer (2015/2016, *Physical Review Letters* / `arXiv:1512.01216`, DOE Fermilab release):** Twin co-located $40\text{ m}$ ($39\text{ m}$ optical baseline) $1\text{ kW}$ Michelson laser interferometers sampling at megahertz (a millionth of a second) to test holographic spacetime jitter down to $10^{-18}\text{ m}$ (a billionth of a billionth of a metre — $1,000$ times smaller than a single proton), ruling out Hogan's pixelated-spacetime correlation model to high statistical significance.
     - **Zohar Ringel and Dmitry Kovrizhin (2017, *Science Advances* `e1701758`, `arXiv:1704.03880`, Oxford/AAAS release):** Mathematical proof that quantum systems with gravitational anomalies (such as the thermal Hall conductance in fractional quantum Hall systems) suffer an intrinsic quantum Monte Carlo sign problem: classical simulation cost doubles with every added particle, so storing the quantum state of just a few hundred electrons requires a computer memory built from more atoms than exist in the observable universe.
