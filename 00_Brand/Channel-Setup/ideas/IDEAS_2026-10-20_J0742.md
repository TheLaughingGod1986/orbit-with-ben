  3. **Concrete quantitative astrophysics landmarks (100% verbatim-sourced):**
     - **A City-Sized Magnet Stronger Than Any Pulsar (NASA Image Library `PIA26274`, `PIA21085`, `27744663791_o`; Wachter, Kouveliotou et al., *Nature* May 29, 2008, `PMID 18509437`; Palmer, Gehrels, Kouveliotou et al., *Nature* April 28, 2005, `PMID 15858567`):** When a massive star explodes as a supernova, its crushed core forms a neutron star that compresses the equivalent mass of **half a million Earths** into a ball just **20 kilometers (12 miles)** across — about the length of **New York's Manhattan Island**. While normal pulsars spin rapidly, **magnetars** (investigated by astrophysicist **Chryssa Kouveliotou**, `born 1953`, LOC `no97025267`, and **Robert C. Duncan**, `born 1942`, LOC `n2002089927`) are slowly rotating neutron stars (periods of **5 to 12 seconds**) with surface magnetic fields exceeding **$10^{14}$ to $10^{15}$ gauss** (**100 trillion to 1 quadrillion gauss**, at least an order of magnitude stronger than normal radio pulsars). In October 2022, NASA's **NICER** and **NuSTAR** telescopes observed magnetar **SGR 1935+2154** unleash a laser-like fast radio burst that released in a fraction of a second about as much energy as the **Sun radiates in a year**.
     - **The December 27, 2004 Giant Flare of SGR 1806-20 That Struck Earth's Ionosphere (Hurley, Boggs, Smith, Duncan et al., *Nature* April 28, 2005, `PMID 15858565`; Palmer, Barthelmy, Gehrels, Kouveliotou et al., *Nature* April 28, 2005, `PMID 15858567`; Gaensler, Kouveliotou et al., *Nature* April 28, 2005, `PMID 15858566`):** On **December 27, 2004**, the magnetar **SGR 1806-20** erupted in a **380-second** giant gamma-ray flare triggered by catastrophic **global crust failure and magnetic reconnection**. As **Kevin Hurley**, **David M. Palmer** (`born 1962`, LOC `no2008167446`), **Neil Gehrels** (`1952–2017`, LOC `n88118918`), and **Bryan M. Gaensler** (`born 1973`, LOC `no2003008763`) reported in *Nature* on **April 28, 2005**, the flare released a total isotropic energy of **$2 \times 10^{46}$ ergs** — **100 times higher** than any previously observed giant flare — and in its **first 0.2 seconds** alone released **as much energy as the Sun radiates in a quarter of a million years (250,000 years)**. It caused an **ionospheric disturbance in Earth's upper atmosphere** recorded around the globe, and from day **6** to day **19** drove a polarized radio nebula (`4 × 10⁴³ ergs`) expanding at **a quarter of the speed of light**, bright enough to mimic a short gamma-ray burst out to **40 megaparsecs**.
     - **When Two Neutron Stars Collide — GW170817 and Short Gamma-Ray Burst Jets (Abbott et al., *Physical Review Letters* October 20, 2017, `PMID 29099225`, & October 19, 2018, `PMID 30387654`; Hallinan, Kasliwal et al., *Science* December 22, 2017, `PMID 29038372` & `PMID 29038373`):** What happens when two neutron stars — each only **11.9 kilometers** in radius (`1.17` to `1.60` solar masses, with interior pressures of **$3.5 \times 10^{34}$ dynes per square centimeter** at twice nuclear saturation density) — spiral together? On **August 17, 2017**, Advanced **LIGO** and **Virgo** detected **GW170817** (`2.74` solar masses total) in galaxy **NGC 4993** at a distance of **40 megaparsecs**, followed **1.7 seconds** later by short gamma-ray burst **GRB 170817A** on NASA's **Fermi** telescope and an ultrarelativistic jet and kilonova cocoon forging heavy elements (`r-process` nucleosynthesis).
     - **The Midpoint Twist ("Wrong.") — Why a Quasar Accretion Disc and Relativistic Jet Outlast Every Explosion (NASA Black Hole Anatomy & `PIA23122`; Heber Doust Curtis `1872–1942`, LOC `no2004050360`; Maarten Schmidt `1929–2022`, LOC `no2011097247`; Wu et al., *Nature* February 26, 2015, `PMID 25719667`; Bañados et al., *Nature* January 25, 2018, `PMID 29211709`):** A magnetar flare (`0.2 s` peak, `380 s` tail) or neutron-star merger (`1.7 s` delay) is a fleeting flash. The most persistently lethal environment in the cosmos is a **quasar** — the **billion-degree** magnetic corona, spinning accretion disc, and **relativistic particle jets** of a feeding supermassive black hole (`science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/anatomy/`). First spotted as `"a curious straight ray"` in galaxy **Messier 87** (`55 million light-years` away, `PIA23122`) by astronomer **Heber Curtis** in **1918** and unlocked when **Maarten Schmidt** measured the redshift of quasar **3C 273** (`2.4 billion light-years` / `749 megaparsecs` away, `PIA08617` / `7995525`), supermassive black hole jets fire particles at **close to the speed of light** across **hundreds of thousands of light-years**. In **2015**, **Xue-Bing Wu** and colleagues (*Nature*, `PMID 25719667`) discovered ultraluminous quasar **SDSS J0100+2802** (`z = 6.30`), whose **12-billion-solar-mass** (`1.2 × 10¹⁰ M☉`) black hole ionized a lethal proximity zone **26 million light-years** across, while **Eduardo Bañados** and colleagues (*Nature*, **January 25, 2018**, `PMID 29211709`) found quasar **ULAS J1342+0928** (`z = 7.54`, `800 million solar masses`) shining at **$4 \times 10^{13}$ (40 trillion) times the luminosity of the Sun** when the universe was only **690 million years old** (`5 percent` of its current age).
