     - **A 4-Billion-Year Impact Record Almost as Big as Mercury (NASA Callisto Facts & NSSDCA Galilean Satellite Fact Sheet):** Discovered on **January 7, 1610** by **Galileo Galilei** (`1564–1642`, LOC `n79003254`), Callisto is Jupiter's second-largest moon and the third-largest moon in the solar system, with a diameter of **4,821 kilometers** (**9,410 miles** / **15,144 kilometers** equatorial circumference) — **98.8% the diameter of the planet Mercury** (**4,879 kilometers**), yet with a density of just **1,830 kg/m³** versus Mercury's **5,427 kg/m³** and a surface gravity of **1.24 m/s²**. Its icy, rocky surface is **about 4 billion years old** and is the **oldest and most heavily cratered surface in our solar system**.
     - **Why Callisto Never Erased Its Craters — Sitting Outside the 1:2:4 Laplace Orbital Resonance (NASA Callisto & Ganymede Facts, NSSDCA, Pierre-Simon Laplace `1749–1827`, LOC `n81077094`):** While **Io** (**422,000 km**, **1.8 days**), **Europa** (**671,000 km**, **3.6 days**), and **Ganymede** (**1,070,000 km**, **7.155 days**) are locked in **Pierre-Simon Laplace**'s **4:2:1 orbital resonance** that flexes their interiors and drives **400 volcanoes** on Io, Callisto orbits alone at **1,883,000 kilometers** (**1,170,000 miles**) from Jupiter every **16.689 Earth days** (**400.5 hours**) at **8.2 km/s** — **1.8 times farther than Ganymede, 2.8 times farther than Europa, and 4.5 times farther than Io**. Outside the resonance, Callisto escaped strong tidal heating, leaving no active volcanoes or tectonic plates to erase **4 billion years** of impacts, including the **4,000-kilometer** **Valhalla** multi-ring impact structure (`PIA01400`, imaged at **60-meter** resolution over an **11-kilometer** / **7-mile** central zone from **1,219 kilometers** / **757 miles** by Galileo on **November 4, 1996**, `PIA00516`).
     - **Partial Differentiation Inside a "Hunk of Rock and Ice" (Anderson, Schubert, Jacobson, Lau, Moore & Sjogren, *Science* June 5, 1998, `PMID 9616114`):** Using radio Doppler gravity tracking from NASA's **Galileo** spacecraft across encounters **C3**, **C9**, and **C10**, **John D. Anderson** and **Gerald Schubert** (`PMID 9616114`) proved that Callisto's rock and ice have **partially, but not completely, separated** — allowing an outer clean-ice shell **less than 350 kilometers** thick above a mixed rock-and-ice interior and any rock-metal core **less than 25 percent** of Callisto's radius.
     - **The Midpoint Twist ("Wrong.") — Galileo's Magnetometer Discovers a Hidden Salty Ocean (Khurana, Kivelson, Stevenson, Schubert, Russell, Walker & Polanskey, *Nature* October 22, 1998, `PMID 9796812`; NASA Callisto Facts):** Scientists long dismissed Callisto as a boring **"ugly duckling moon"** and a dead **"hunk of rock and ice."** Then on **October 22, 1998**, **Krishan Khurana** and magnetometer principal investigator **Margaret Kivelson** (`born 1928`, LOC `n83213689`) reported in *Nature* (`PMID 9796812`) that Galileo's **11-meter** magnetometer boom detected **induced magnetic fields** around Callisto driven by eddy currents in an electrically conducting layer — revealing a **salty liquid-water ocean** that NASA models place **about 250 kilometers (155 miles)** beneath the icy crust, potentially interacting with a layer of rocks.
     - **A Carbon Dioxide, Oxygen & Hydrogen Exosphere and ESA's JUICE Explorer (Carlson, *Science* February 5, 1999, `PMID 9933159`; NASA Callisto Facts; ESA JUICE Factsheet):** On **February 5, 1999**, **Robert W. Carlson** (`PMID 9933159`) reported Galileo Near-Infrared Mapping Spectrometer airglow up to **100 kilometers** above Callisto, discovering a tenuous **carbon dioxide atmosphere** with a surface pressure of **7.5 × 10⁻¹² bar** at **150 kelvin** and a **4-year** loss lifetime (requiring active replenishment, alongside **oxygen** and **hydrogen** in the exosphere). And on **April 14, 2023**, ESA and NASA launched the **6,000-kilogram** **JUICE** (*Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer*) spacecraft with **85 square meters** of solar panels (`10` panels), arriving in **July 2031** to conduct multiple flybys of Callisto and probe its hidden ocean.
