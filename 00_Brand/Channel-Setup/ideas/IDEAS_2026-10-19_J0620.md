## 3. Detailed Breakdown of the 5 Candidates

### Idea 1 (Rank #1 — Scaffolded as `050`): *Why Saturn's Moon Iapetus Is Half Black and Half White*
- **Title shape:** Why/How search question (`54` characters; script H1 `# Why Is Saturn's Moon Iapetus Half Black and Half White?`, front-loading `Saturn's Moon Iapetus`).
- **Thumbnail concept:** Global *Cassini* dichotomy view (`PIA11690` / `PIA06166`) showing the walnut-shaped moon split down the middle between coal-black `Cassini Regio` (left) and snow-bright `Roncevaux Terra` (right) with the 12-mile-high equatorial ridge (`3 words: HALF COAL BLACK`).
- **0:00–0:08 Hook:** Orbiting two million two hundred and thirteen thousand miles from Saturn where a walnut-shaped moon of three-quarters water ice and one-quarter rock shows a leading hemisphere as dark as coal and a trailing hemisphere almost as bright as snow, as you fly within one thousand and twenty miles with NASA's Cassini spacecraft, why is Saturn's moon Iapetus half black and half white?
- **Midpoint twist (`"Wrong."`):** For decades after Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 flew past in 1980 and 1981, many scientists thought ice volcanism inside Iapetus had flooded its leading side with dark hydrocarbons, or that incoming dust alone painted a thick crust across half the moon. **Wrong.** When NASA's *Cassini* spacecraft swooped within 1,011 miles (1,627 kilometres) on September 10, 2007, 10-metre-per-pixel images proved the dark coating is only about a foot (a few tens of centimetres) thick and punched through by 20-to-50-metre bright ice craters: a whisper of dark dust from retrograde outer moon Phoebe (`8,049,668 miles` out) merely pulls the trigger, while Iapetus's 79-day (`1,904-hour`) rotation drives a global **thermal segregation** runaway that sublimates water ice out of the sun-warmed dark terrain and freezes it onto the cold trailing hemisphere and poles.
- **7 Number Objects:**
  1. **0.03–0.05 (4%) vs 0.5–0.6 (over 60%)** albedo contrast across Iapetus's **912-to-914-mile (`1,468–1,471 km`)** globe (`mean radius 457 miles / 736 km`), where dark `Cassini Regio` covers **40 percent** of the surface.
  2. **October 25, 1671** discovery by Giovanni Domenico Cassini (`1625–1712`) and **2,213,000-mile (`3,561,000 km`)** inclined orbit vs Titan at **759,200 miles (`1,221,850 km`)**, less than a third of Titan's **3,200-mile (`5,150 km`)** diameter.
  3. **8,049,668 miles (`12,952,000 km`)** retrograde `175°`-inclined orbit of captured Centaur moon **Phoebe** (`mean radius 66.2 miles / 106.5 km`, `1/16` Earth's Moon, `6%` reflectivity, `9-hour` spin, `18-month` orbit) shedding dark dust inward.
  4. **1,904 hours (longer than 79 days)** slow rotation period driving global **thermal segregation** discovered during the **September 10, 2007** *Cassini* flyby (`1,020 miles / 1,640 km` closest-approach design; down to `1,011 miles / 1,627 km`).
  5. **About a foot (`a few tens of centimeters`, `10 cm = 4 inches`)** thickness of the dark carbonaceous blanket, punctured by **20-to-50-metre (`66-to-164-foot`)** bright rayed craters at `10 meters (33 feet) per pixel`.
  6. **Up to 12 miles (`20 kilometers`) high**, **12 miles (`20 km`) wide**, and **over 808 miles (`1,300 km`) long** equatorial mountain ridge discovered **December 25, 2004** — rivalling **Olympus Mons on Mars** (`3× Mount Everest`) on a world **nearly 5× smaller than Mars and nearly 9× smaller than Earth**.
  7. **580-kilometre (`360-mile`)** Turgis basin, **504-kilometre (`313-mile`)** Engelier basin, and **445-kilometre (`276-mile`)** Gerin basin among **at least 9** giant impact basins on a moon with a density **1.2 times liquid water** (three-quarters ice, one-quarter rock).

### Idea 2 (Rank #2 — Recommended for `051`): *Why NASA's DART Spacecraft Changed an Asteroid's Orbit by 33 Minutes*
- **Hook:** On September 26, 2022, NASA intentionally slammed a 570-kilogram spacecraft at 6.1 kilometres per second into the 160-metre moonlet Dimorphos.
- **Midpoint twist:** Engineers calculated that a pure kinetic punch would shorten Dimorphos's 11-hour-55-minute orbit by about 7 minutes. **Wrong.** The impact blasted more than 1,000 metric tons of rubble into a 10,000-kilometre tail, acting like a rocket exhaust that multiplied the momentum transfer by a factor of ~3.6 and shortened the orbit by **33 minutes**.

### Idea 3 (Rank #3 — Recommended for `052`): *Why Uranus's Moon Miranda Has a 12-Mile-High Ice Cliff*
- **Hook:** Just 290 miles (470 kilometres) across — one-seventh the diameter of Earth's Moon — Uranus's innermost major moon Miranda boasts Verona Rupes, a sheer fault scarp up to 12 miles (20 kilometres) high, ten times deeper than the Grand Canyon, where a rock dropped from the rim takes 12 minutes to hit the floor.
- **Midpoint twist:** After Voyager 2 flew past Miranda on January 24, 1986, planetary scientists thought the tiny moon had been shattered apart by a giant impact and reassembled inside-out. **Wrong.** Modern tectonic and tidal-heating models (including 2024 Johns Hopkins APL findings) show orbital resonance with neighbouring Uranus moons flexed a thin ice crust over a subsurface ocean up to 62 miles (100 kilometres) deep within the last 100 to 500 million years.

### Idea 4 (Rank #4 — Recommended for `053`): *Why NASA's Parker Solar Probe Flew Inside the Sun's Corona without Melting*
- **Hook:** Flying at 692,000 kilometres per hour through a solar corona heated to more than 1 million degrees Celsius, protected by a 11.4-centimetre carbon-composite heat shield that keeps the spacecraft's instruments at room temperature (~30 °C).
- **Midpoint twist:** How a million-degree plasma can fail to melt a spacecraft because the corona's particle density is so low that heat transfer is governed by radiant sunlight rather than conductive gas, while magnetic "switchbacks" whip solar wind out of coronal holes.
