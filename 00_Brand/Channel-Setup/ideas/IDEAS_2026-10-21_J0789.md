  3. **Concrete quantitative planetary-science landmarks (100% verbatim-sourced):**
     - **A 243-Day Retrograde Sidereal Day Longer Than a 224.7-Day Year, and a 116.75-Day West-to-East Solar Day (NASA Science `science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/`, NASA NSSDC Venus Fact Sheet `venusfact.html`, NASA Photojournal `PIA23791`, `PIA00271`, `PIA00104`, `PIA00072`, `PIA01544`, & Wikipedia `Venus` / `Observations_and_explorations_of_Venus`):** Orbiting the Sun at an average distance of **108.21 million kilometers** (`108 million km` / `67 million miles`, `0.723 AU`, orbital velocity `35.02 km/s`, eccentricity `0.0068` — the most circular orbit of any planet) with an equatorial radius of **6,051.8 kilometers** (`12,104 km` / `7,521 mi` diameter, `95%` of Earth's `6,378.1 km` radius, mass `4.8673 × 10²⁴ kg` = `81.5%` of Earth's mass, mean density `5,243 kg/m³`, flattening `0.000`), Venus completes one orbit around the Sun in **224.701 Earth days** (`225 Earth days`). Yet Venus rotates clockwise (retrograde) on its axis with an axial tilt (obliquity to orbit) of **177.36 degrees** (`2.64 degrees` inclination of equator, essentially upside down without seasons) once every **243.0226 Earth days** (`−5,832.6 hours` / `243 Earth days` — `243.69` times slower than Earth's `23.9345-hour` sidereal day). Because its retrograde spin combines with its orbital motion around the Sun, a **solar day** (sunrise to sunrise) on Venus lasts **116.75 Earth days** (`2,802.0 hours` / `117 Earth days`, or `1.92 Venusian solar days` per Venusian year), with the Sun rising in the **west** and setting in the **east**.
     - **From 1610 Phases and 1761 Atmosphere to the 1961–1967 Radar Breakthrough (`Galileo Galilei`, `1564–1642`, LOC `n79003254`; `Mikhail Lomonosov`, `1711–1765`, LOC `n80060716`; `Giovanni Schiaparelli`, `1835–1910`, LOC `n85804243`; `Gordon H. Pettengill`, `1926–2021`, LOC `no2009129104`; `Richard M. Goldstein` & `Roland L. Carpenter`, *Science* March 8, 1963, `PMID 17743054`; `Irwin I. Shapiro`, `born 1929`, LOC `n86859885`, *Science* July 28, 1967, `PMID 17798698`):** After **Galileo Galilei** (`1564–1642`) observed the phases of Venus in **1610** (`583.92-day` / `584-day` synodic cycle) and **Mikhail Lomonosov** (`1711–1765`) discovered evidence of Venus's atmosphere during the **1761** transit, 19th-century observers guessed Venus spun in roughly **24 hours** until Italian astronomer **Giovanni Schiaparelli** (`1835–1910`) proposed a much slower, tidally locked rotation. Because Venus's opaque clouds blocked optical telescopes, its true spin remained hidden until MIT radio astronomer **Gordon H. Pettengill** (`1926–2021`) pioneered planetary radar ranging to Venus at Millstone Hill during the **1961** conjunction (and later led the `12.6-cm` radar mapper on *Magellan*, `PMID 17769272`) alongside NASA's **26-meter** Goldstone antenna in California, Jodrell Bank in the UK, and Yevpatoria in Crimea. On **March 8, 1963**, JPL radar astronomers **Richard M. Goldstein** and **Roland L. Carpenter** reported in *Science* (`PMID 17743054`) that *"Venus may rotate in a direction opposite to that of the earth at a rate of only one revolution in 240 days."* Retrograde rotation was confirmed in **1964**, and on **July 28, 1967**, MIT/Harvard astrophysicist **Irwin I. Shapiro** (`born 1929`) reported in *Science* (`PMID 17798698`) that radar data showed Venus's equator inclined by less than **2 degrees** to its orbital plane with a retrograde sidereal rotation period of **`243.09 ± 0.18 days`** — noting that the `583.92-day` synodic interval between close approaches to Earth (`38.2 million km` minimum distance) equals almost exactly **5.001444 Venusian solar days** (`4` apparent rotations as seen from Earth), an apparent coincidence that fooled early observers.
     - **The Midpoint Twist ("Wrong.") — Why Venus Didn't Need to Flip Upside Down: Atmospheric Thermal Tides, 60× Super-Rotation, and a 20-Minute Wobble (`Alexandre C. M. Correia` & `Jacques Laskar`, `born 1955`, LOC `nb2016008038`, *Nature* June 14, 2001, `PMID 11459048`; `David J. Stevenson`, `born 1948`, LOC `n85805645`, 2006; `Takeshi Horinouchi` et al., *Science* April 24, 2020, `PMID 32327594`; `Norihiko Sugimoto` et al., *Nature Communications* June 17, 2021, `PMID 34140504`; `Jean-Luc Margot`, `born 1969`, LOC `nr99034193`, 2021):** You might assume the only way a planet can spin backwards with a **177.36-degree** axial tilt is if a giant protoplanet smashed into it and knocked it completely upside down (as Caltech's **David J. Stevenson**, `born 1948`, and Alex Alemi modeled in **2006** with two giant impacts **10 million years** apart). **Wrong.** On **June 14, 2001**, French astronomer **Jacques Laskar** (`born 1955`) and **Alexandre C. M. Correia** proved in *Nature* (`PMID 11459048`) that any terrestrial planet with a dense atmosphere like Venus (`92 bars` surface pressure, `~65 kg/m³` surface density = `6.5%` of liquid water, `~4.8 × 10²⁰ kg` atmospheric mass = `92 times` Earth's, `96.5% CO₂`, `737 K / 464 °C`) evolves through **core-mantle friction** and **solar atmospheric thermal tides** into one of only **four final rotation states** (two prograde and two retrograde) — and that Venus could start with a normal prograde spin, slow to a standstill, and **reverse into retrograde rotation while its obliquity goes toward zero without its spin axis ever flipping upside down**! Even today, that atmosphere actively tugs on the solid planet: on **April 24, 2020**, Hokkaido University physicist **Takeshi Horinouchi** and colleagues reported in *Science* (`PMID 32327594`) using JAXA's **Akatsuki** orbiter (in orbit `December 7, 2015 – 2024`) that solar thermal tides near the **70-kilometer** cloud tops (`PMID 34140504`) pump angular momentum to keep the atmosphere **super-rotating 60 times faster** than the solid globe (`100 m/s` / `360 km/h` / `224 mph` winds circling Venus every **4 Earth days**), while UCLA astronomer **Jean-Luc Margot** (`born 1969`) and colleagues used **15 years** of Goldstone and Green Bank radar speckle tracking (`2006–2020`, published **2021**) to prove that atmospheric momentum transfer makes Venus's **243-day** sidereal day fluctuate by up to **20 minutes** (after *Magellan* and *Venus Express* found a **6.5-minute** shift over `16 years`) and revealed a **3,500-kilometer** radius metallic core (`I/MR² = 0.337`).
