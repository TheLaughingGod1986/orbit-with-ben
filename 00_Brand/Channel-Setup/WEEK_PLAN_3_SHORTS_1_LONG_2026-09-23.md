## Hook, word for word

Every Short uses the same two-line shape. Do not rewrite the Monday lines.

Line 1, 5 to 7 words, said in the first second while Orbit looks at the camera: the familiar thing in danger.
Line 2, while Orbit reacts: what they get if they stay.

No greeting. No buildup.

Monday:

> The Moon is leaving us.
> Stay here and I will show you what Earth looks like without it.

Picture: Orbit looking up as the Moon drifts back.

Wednesday:

> You could never stand on this star.
> One spoonful weighs more than all of us. Watch what it does.

Friday:

> Every star will die. Even our Sun.
> I will show you what is left after the last one goes out.

Sunday long, first 30 seconds, then the payoff:

> The Moon is leaving us. In this film I will show you why it is drifting, what Earth was like when it was close, and what happens when it is gone.

The spoken Short stays 22–27 seconds. The two lines are the opening. They are not stretched into a minute.

## How a Short is built

- 22–27 seconds. Captions the whole way. One idea.
- First second: Orbit and the danger are both on screen. No slow build.
- One hard fact next. Last 4 seconds return to the opening picture so the Short loops.
- The film that Short promotes is on screen around 9–14 seconds. The end card names that film.
- Studio Related points at that film once it has an id. Monday points at the Moon film. Wednesday points at Last Star. Friday points at Neutron Star. No new Short pin. No `/go/`.
- Thumb is a picture with the hook in yellow and white. Orbit is not on the thumb.
- Do not upload or schedule until QA then UAT have watched the cut. Do not pad a Short to chase the long-film script score of 90.
