## Six-Chapter Outline (`26:00`)

1. **`0:00–3:30` Cold Open — `$3.0 Billion` vs `$1.9 Billion`:** Cold open on rows of silver `B-29` noses at Wichita (`0:00`); why building an intercontinental pressurised bomber cost `58%` more than the Manhattan Project — and why Boeing's second prototype burned out of the sky over Seattle on `18 February 1943`.
2. **`3:30–8:00` Chapter 1 — Ordering `1,600` Bombers Before One Had Flown (`1940–1942`):** Hap Arnold's `3,200-mile` Pacific range problem; pressurised forward and aft crew compartments connected by a `35-foot` (`28-inch` diameter) crawl tunnel over unpressurised bomb bays (`WHO: Henry "Hap" Arnold`, `WHO: Edmund "Eddie" Allen`).
3. **`8:00–13:00` Chapter 2 — Magnesium Engine Fires & GE's Analog Fire-Control Computer:** Why `Wright R-3350` `2,200-hp` 18-cylinder radials overheated on climb (`magnesium` crankcases burning at `630°C`), paired with General Electric's central station analog computer correcting lead, gravity drop, air density, and parallax across remote turrets.
4. **`13:00–17:30` Chapter 3 — The "Battle of Kansas" (`March–April 1944`) & Flying Over the Himalayas (`Operation Matterhorn`):** Grounded B-29s lining snowy Wichita ramps; `600` engineers modifying engines outdoors in freezing gales; why staging from Chengdu, China burned `7 gallons` of fuel just to deliver `1 gallon` to forward bases.
5. **`17:30–22:00` Chapter 4 — Midpoint Crisis: Hitting the `200-mph` Jet Stream Over Tokyo (`Nov 1944 – Feb 1945`):** `40,000` Seabees carving `8,500-foot` coral runways on Saipan, Tinian, and Guam; `30,000-foot` daylight raids hit an uncharted `180–230 mph` jet stream (`500+ mph` groundspeeds), scattering `94%` of bombs off target.
6. **`22:00–26:00` Chapter 5 — LeMay's Low-Altitude Pivot & Industrial Verdict (`March–Aug 1945`):** Stripping guns and dropping to `5,000–8,000 feet` at night to cut climb strain and double payload (`WHO: Curtis LeMay`), presented soberly and factually with zero shock framing; how `3,970` B-29s shaped post-war pressurised flight.
