## Original Angle

Facing wartime aluminium-alloy and gun-turret bottlenecks in 1939–40, Geoffrey de Havilland and Air Marshal Sir Wilfrid Freeman backed an unarmed twin-Merlin bomber moulded from Ecuadorian balsa wood sandwiched between Canadian birch plywood in furniture workshops (`DH.98 Mosquito`) — flying at ~400 mph to carry a 4,000-lb "Cookie" bomb to Berlin (matching the bomb load of a ten-man B-17) with a two-man crew and the lowest loss rate (`0.7%`) in RAF Bomber Command.
