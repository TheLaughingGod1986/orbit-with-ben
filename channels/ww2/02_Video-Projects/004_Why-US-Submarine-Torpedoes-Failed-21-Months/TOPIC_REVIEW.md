## Correct the brief

- Christie was not Lockwood’s fellow rebel against the magnetic exploder. The Bureau’s own history, printed p.105, says his Southwest Pacific command retained it until spring 1944.
- Do not say every torpedo failed, that all faults ended together in September 1943, or that 21 months is a universal fleet repair interval. The museum dates satisfactory operation to autumn 1943; the Bureau history says performance was almost uniformly good by January 1944.
- The Bureau history gives 24 July for Nimitz’s disabling order; NHHC’s later H-008-3 gives 24 June. Use summer 1943 in narration, explicitly retain the source disagreement in SOURCES.md.
- Do not reduce depth error to a heavier warhead alone. The official history identifies placement/pressure errors in both control and measuring apparatus as well as mismatched exercise heads.
- Do not give an invented tanker tonnage, shot-by-shot reconstruction or quotation attributed to Daspit. Use the official history’s reported fifteen shots and twelve claimed hits, and call them reported figures.
