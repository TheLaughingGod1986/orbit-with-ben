## 2. Two Honest Real-Archive Thumbnail Concepts (Inspected US Federal Public Domain)

### Concept A (Primary) — Surfaced Type VIIC U-Boat Forced Under by Carrier Aircraft (`NARA 80-G-68713`)

- **Archive photograph:** **NARA `80-G-68713`** (Naval History and Heritage Command / National Museum of the U.S. Navy, Record Group 80: General Records of the Department of the Navy).
- **Inspected URL:** `https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nmusn/explore/photography/wwii/wwii-atlantic/battle-of-the-atlantic/engagements-german-uboats/1943-attacks-german/1943-june-4-u-603/80-g-68713.html`
- **Verbatim archival caption & provenance:**
  > *"Air Attacks on German U-boats: WWII. German Type VIIC U-boat, U-603, attacked by TBF-1 aircraft from USS Bogue (CVE-9) in the Atlantic, June 4, 1943. Pilot was Ensign E.R. Hodgson, who dropped four Mk. 17.2 depth bombs on the submarine. This photograph shows splashes from machine gun fire. U-603 was not sunk in this attack but was forced to submerge saving a nearby LCI convoy from attack. Note, U-603's exhaust plume. Official U.S. Navy Photograph, now in the collections of the National Archives."*
- **Why this real photograph works honestly (zero fabrication, zero casualties):**
  - Taken from an aircraft of the pioneer Atlantic escort carrier `USS Bogue (CVE-9)` immediately after the May 1943 turning point (`4 June 1943`), it shows the exact operational mechanism that defeated the wolfpacks: a surfaced `Type VIIC` U-boat caught on the surface in broad daylight and forced to dive, stripping it of the surface speed needed to intercept a nearby convoy.
  - Because `U-603` was forced to submerge rather than sunk in this attack, the image contains **no casualties or gore** while offering high-contrast visual drama (white bow wave, diesel exhaust plume, and conning tower against the Atlantic swell).
- **Grading & composition (`1280×720`):**
  - Full-frame real photograph (`100%` of frame), normal-to-bright high-contrast silver-grey grade lifting sea mid-tones so the white wake and `U-603` hull read clearly at `168×94 px` mobile preview (no dark grade).
  - **Cover text (`2 words` naming the object/state):** `CAUGHT SURFACED` (or `TYPE VIIC`, or `0 words` in clean-photo variant per `00_Brand/PLAYBOOK.md` §3).
  - **Callout:** One crisp `#FF3B30` arrow pointing to `U-603`'s conning tower and diesel exhaust plume.

### Concept B (Test & Compare Variant) — Consolidated PB4Y-1 / B-24 Liberator on Anti-U-Boat Patrol (`NARA 80-G-407707`)

- **Archive photograph:** **NARA `80-G-407707`** (Naval History and Heritage Command, Record Group 80: General Records of the Department of the Navy).
- **Inspected URL:** `https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/numerical-list-of-images/nhhc-series/nh-series/80-G-407000/80-G-407707.html`
- **Verbatim archival caption & provenance:**
  > *"80-G-407707 Consolidated PB4Y-1 'Liberator' patrol bomber. Seen from another PB4Y, while on Patrol over the Bay of Biscay, hunting for u-boats. Photographed December 1943."* (Official U.S. Navy Photograph, National Archives `80-G-407707`).
- **Why this real photograph works honestly (zero fabrication):**
  - Instead of fabricating a composite of a B-24 over a U-boat, this authentic air-to-air U.S. Navy patrol photograph makes the four-engine Consolidated Liberator (`B-24` / `PB4Y-1`) the hero subject banking over the open Atlantic swell.
- **Grading & composition (`1280×720`):**
  - Real archive photograph occupying the full frame, bright tonal separation between the pale maritime-camouflaged wings/twin tail fins and the textured ocean below (no dark grade).
  - **Cover text (`2 words` naming the object):** `VLR LIBERATOR` (upper-left, `#FFD600` signal yellow with `#0B0E14` keyline, well clear of the aircraft nose and bottom-right duration badge).
  - **Callout:** One `#FF3B30` arrow pointing to the long-chord Davis wing / fuselage bomb bay where extra fuel tanks gave the VLR Liberator its `800-mile` operational patrol radius.
