## 3. Reconciliations of Key Historical Numbers & Cause/Effect Chains

### A. March 1943 Convoy Totals vs Worldwide Monthly Totals (`[P01]`–`[P07]`)
- **Convoy `SC 121` (`7–11 March 1943`):** `13` ships of `~62,000 tons` sunk by a pursuit of `17` U-boats without loss to the attackers (`UK-RN-II-14.html`, `C03a`).
- **Convoys `HX 229` & `SC 122` (`16–20 March 1943`):** Attacked by a concentration of `40` U-boats starting on the night of `16–17 March` about `850 miles` from the nearest Allied air bases (`13` ships sunk on the first night; `54` air sorties, `32` sightings, `21` attacks starting 17 March, `ASW-51-5.html`, `C01a`, `C07a`). Total merchant losses in the two convoys: **`21` ships of `141,000 tons` for `1` U-boat lost** in British official records (`UK-RN-II-14.html`, `C01b`), or **`20` ships** in US Navy OEG Report 51 (`ASW-51-5.html`).
- **Worldwide March 1943 Totals:** Across all ocean theatres, U-boats sank **`108` Allied ships totalling `627,377 tons`** in British records (`UK-RN-II-14.html` prose & Table 31, `C03b`), of which **`83` ships (`487,034 tons`) were sunk in convoy** and **`25` ships (`140,343 tons`) were sailing independently** (`C04a`). US Navy OEG Report 51 (`ASW-51-5.html`) records **`107` ships of `627,000 gross tons`** sunk worldwide by U-boats. Including enemy aircraft and other causes, total Allied losses in March 1943 reached **`120` ships of `~693,000 tons`** (`UK-RN-II-14.html`).
- **Reconciliation verdict:** `SCRIPT_v01.md` (`[P01]`–`[P04]`) cleanly separates the `HX 229`/`SC 122` convoy loss (`21` ships / `141,000 tons`) from the global monthly U-boat total (`108` ships / `627,377 tons`, with `83` in convoy), correcting the conflation in `TOPIC.md`.

### B. Convoy `ONS 5` (`29 April – 6 May 1943`) Losses, Escort Command & U-Boat Kills (`[P41]`–`[P50]`)
- **Convoy size & merchant losses:** `~30` ships and `4` stragglers on 4 May (`ASW-51-5.html`); **`12` merchant ships sunk** (`UK-RN-II-14.html` & `ASW-51-5.html`).
- **Command handover:** Commander P. W. Gretton in `HMS Duncan` led Escort Group B7 at initial contact (`29 April`, `500 miles east of Cape Farewell`), reinforced by Capt. J. A. McCoy's 3rd Escort Group (`HMS Offa`) at `8 p.m. on 2 May`. Heavy seas forced `HMS Duncan` (`3 May`) and three of `Offa`'s destroyers (`4 May`) to detach to St. John's for fuel, so **Lt.-Cdr. R. E. Sherwood, RNR, in the frigate `HMS Tay` commanded the escort during the decisive 4–6 May battle**, reinforced early on `6 May` by Cdr. G. N. Brewer's 1st Escort Group (`HMS Pelican`) (`UK-RN-II-14.html`, `C42a`–`C44a`).
- **U-boats sunk (`7` across the passage) & cause/effect:**
  - `1` sunk early in passage south of Iceland by an RAF Coastal Command **Flying Fortress** (`U-710`);
  - `1` sunk on `4 May` by an RCAF **No. 5 Squadron Canso** flying boat from Newfoundland (`U-630`);
  - `5` sunk on `5–6 May` by **surface escorts** (`HMS Pink` in daylight on 5 May; `HMS Loosestrife`, `HMS Vidette` with Hedgehog, `HMS Oribi` by ramming in fog, and `HMS Pelican` with depth charges on radar echo during the night of 5–6 May, when `~24` U-boat attacks were defeated until `4:20 a.m.`).
  - **Zero U-boats were sunk by VLR Liberators in fog at `ONS 5`**, and `NHHC H-Gram 019-4` (`S8`)'s statement that *"10-centimeter radar aboard aircraft detected all 26 attempts by U-boats to attack convoy ONS 5"* is an internal slip in `H-Gram 019-4` conflating the surface escorts' Type 271 radar in fog with airborne radar. `SCRIPT_v01.md` rightly follows Roskill (`S1`) and OEG Report 51 (`S4`).
  - **Flagged nuance (`C45a`, `C48a`):** Because postwar German-British wreck audits swapped several hull numbers among those same five 5–6 May surface-escort attacks (`U-192`, `U-638`, `U-531`, `U-125`) and `U-630`, `[P48]` should add *"in Admiralty records"*.

### C. The `22–24 May 1943` Withdrawal Order & May Monthly Totals (`[P54]`–`[P56]`)
- **Dates:** After `17 May 1943`, `0` merchant ships were lost in the Atlantic north of `45°N` (`ASW-51-5.html`). - **May 1943 U-Boat & Shipping Totals:**

### D. Ferry Range (`2,000–2,500 Miles`) vs Operational Patrol Radius (`~800 Miles` vs `~450 Miles`) & `"Black Pit"` (`[P17]`–`[P28]`)
- **Range vs operational radius:** Standard shore-based maritime aircraft at the end of 1942 had an operational cover radius of only **`~450 miles`** from base (`UK-RN-II-8.html`, `C18a`). VLR Liberators had a still-air endurance of **`2,000 to 2,500 miles`** (`ASW-51-5.html`, `C21a`), which after outbound transit, convoy patrol loiter, and return fuel reserves provided temporary air cover at an operational radius of **`~800 miles`** from base (`UK-RN-II-8.html`, `C20a`).
- **Unverified `"600-mile"` figure:** Completely absent (`0` occurrences) across `SCRIPT_v01.md`, `PACKAGING_v01.md`, and `SHOT_LIST_v01.csv`.
- **Flagged terminology error (`C17a`):** `UK-RN-II-8.html` explicitly states: *"The enemy called the Azores air gap 'the black pit'."* `[P17]`'s statement that the zone south of Greenland *"became known to Allied crews as the Greenland air gap, or the Black Pit"* is flagged for correction.
- **VLR airframe numbers:** In February 1943 (`UK-RN-II-14.html` Table 29), out of `820` maritime patrol aircraft across all Atlantic convoy routes, only **`18` were VLR Liberators** (`9` in Iceland, `9` in No. 15 Group in Northern Ireland, all in `No. 120 Squadron RAF`, and **`0`** across all Western Atlantic sectors; `"about 20"` in `ASW-51-5.html`), rising to **`"over thirty"` (`<40`)** by May 1943 (`C24a`–`C28a`).

### E. Radar (`1.5 m` vs `10 cm`), `R-600 Metox` (`130 cm`), `Naxos` (`8–12 cm`), and Air-Cover Limits (`[P09]`–`[P15]`, `[P29]`–`[P38]`)
- **Kinematics & deterrence:** Type VIIC U-boats relied on `~17-knot` surface speed by day to outflank `7–10 knot` convoys and outrun `16-knot` `865-ton` Flower-class corvettes (`UK-RN-I-8.html`, `ASW-51-10.html`). Aircraft forced surfaced U-boats to dive (`ASW-51-10.html` Method B tracking), reducing their speed below convoy speed; across Jan–May 1943, only `2` ships were sunk in convoy in the Atlantic while air escort was present, and `0` in the major convoy battles of Table 32 (`UK-RN-II-14.html`, `C15a`).
- **Metric vs Centimetric Radar & German Countermeasures:** Airborne `ASV Mark II` operated on `1.5 metres` (`150 cm`, `UK-RAF-III-2.html`). After Leigh Light Wellingtons began night attacks in the Bay of Biscay in June 1942, Germany ordered the `R-600 Metox` receiver with its wooden `"Southern Cross Aerial"` (`ASW-51-4.html`, operational `~October 1942`), which detected radar down to a lower wavelength limit of **`130 cm`** (`ASW-51-14.html`). Allied **`10-cm` (`S-band`)** cavity-magnetron radar (`Type 271` at sea, `ASV Mark III`/`IV` in the air) operated far below `130 cm`. Despite capturing a British `10-cm` `H2S` (`"Rotterdam Gerat"`) in March 1943, an `"unexplained"` 6-month delay from March to September prevented Germany from fielding the `8–12 cm` `Naxos` receiver until autumn 1943 (`ASW-51-14.html`).
