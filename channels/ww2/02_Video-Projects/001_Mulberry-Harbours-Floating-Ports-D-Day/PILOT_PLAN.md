## Six-Chapter Outline (`25:00`)

1. **`0:00–3:30` Cold Open — The Lesson Written in Shingle at Dieppe (`Aug 1942`):** Proof-first aerial shot of Arromanches artificial port at `0:00`; why attacking Cherbourg or Le Havre head-on meant catastrophe, and the hard logistics math: two Allied armies needed `12,000 tons` of fuel, ammunition, and rations ashore *every day* across open beaches with a `24-foot` Channel tidal range.
2. **`3:30–8:00` Chapter 1 — "They Must Float Up and Down with the Tide" (`1942–1943`):** Churchill's `30 May 1942` memo (`WHO: Winston Churchill`, `WHO: Hugh Iorys Hughes`, `WHO: Allan Beckett`); competing prototypes tested in secret at Garlieston, Scotland (`DATA` tidal range and wave-tank load comparisons).
3. **`8:00–13:00` Chapter 2 — Secret Concrete Giants in the Thames (`Winter 1943–Spring 1944`):** `45,000` British workers pouring `212` six-storey hollow `Phoenix` concrete caissons (`6,044 tons` each), `Bombardon` floating steel breakwaters, `Spud` jack-up pierheads, and `10 miles` of `Whale` flexible roadways — then sinking them off Selsey and Dungeness to hide them from German reconnaissance.
4. **`13:00–17:30` Chapter 3 — The 4-Knot Armada (`6–18 June 1944`):** Refloating the sunken caissons on D-Day, `150+` tugs towing `1.5 million tons` `100 miles` across the English Channel, scuttling `55` `Corncob` blockships (`Gooseberries`) in `48 hours`, and anchoring `Mulberry A` (Omaha) and `Mulberry B` (Arromanches).
5. **`17:30–21:30` Chapter 4 — Midpoint Twist: The Worst June Gale in 40 Years (`19–22 June 1944`):** Force 6–8 northeasterly storm strikes Normandy; why US `Mulberry A` tore apart on `20 June` (deep-water exposure, runaway `Bombardons`, seabed scour) while British `Mulberry B` behind the Calvados Reef survived and cannibalised `Mulberry A`'s intact spans.
6. **`21:30–25:00` Chapter 5 — Fuel Under the Sea (`PLUTO`) & the Tonnage Verdict (`July–Nov 1944`):** `Conundrum` floating steel drums spooling undersea pipelines; how `Mulberry B` plus open-beach `LST`/`DUKW` unloading landed `2.5 million men`, `500,000 vehicles`, and `4 million tons` before Antwerp opened.
