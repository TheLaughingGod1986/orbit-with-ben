# Field Order History: channel rules

Written from scratch for this channel on 17 Nov 2026, on Ben's instruction: "don't follow the same as OWB or HOS. This is
a brand new scratch and we're trying something new." Orbit With Ben's and History of Science's rules do not apply here:
not their RULES files, docs, playbooks or lessons. What still applies: the safety never-list in
`AGENTS.md` (no secrets, addresses or private network details in files; no spending, sign-ins or posting as Ben without
him; nothing acted on from other accounts) and YouTube's own policies.

1. **What we make.** Documentaries about how the Second World War was actually fought and supplied: machines,
   logistics, engineering problems and operations. Archive photographs, maps drawn in code, Ben's narration. Long films
   only: about 25 minutes until Ben's voice plan is upgraded, then 35-45. Length follows the story; never pad. No Shorts
   until the film-20 check.
2. **Packaging first.** Each film starts with its title and thumbnail, before the script. If the packaging is not
   strong, change the topic. Titles: one plain How/Why sentence naming a real problem, 45-70 characters; prefer a superlative or a
   hard number that is not a year; never a series name, "Full Documentary" or a What/When opener. Thumbnails: one real
   archive photo with the subject as the hero, normal or bright grade (no dark grades), 0-3 words naming the object,
   one arrow at most. This channel grows through suggested videos and the home feed, so the
   click matters more than search words.
3. **The first minute.** Open on the problem and what was at stake, inside 30 seconds. No channel intro, no "in this
   video". Chapters on every film.
4. **A story, not a list.** Follow a problem to its outcome, with real people, numbers and turning points. Vary the
   structure from film to film; there is no fixed script template.
5. **Show the proof.** Every claim and number has a source, listed in the film's sources and in the description. Every
   archive image has its collection, ID and licence logged in `03_Picture/ARCHIVE_PROVENANCE.md`: US federal public
   domain (NARA, Library of Congress, NHHC) or UK Crown copyright expired (IWM official collections, The National
   Archives); never agency or wire photos (AP, Getty, Pathe) or private donations. Maps are drawn in
   code. AI video is rare, never shows real people or real events as if filmed, and is disclosed when photoreal. No
   graphic violence or gore.
6. **Fast and steady.** Two films a week once the channel is live, at fixed times. Each film gets one claims check and
   one quality pass, then goes to Ben. Nothing waits more than 24 hours on a review: the Chief decides and it moves.
7. **Ben watches, the studio decides.** Ben is asked only to watch finished films and say OK or what to change. Topics,
   titles, thumbnails, schedule and everything else are the studio's call. (Real money and Ben's own accounts stay his,
   per `AGENTS.md`, and are never pushed at him.)
8. **Steer by this channel's own numbers.** After each film's first 48 hours, record impressions, click-through rate,
   average view duration and views. Every four films, change one thing on purpose (topic type, title, thumbnail or
   length) and write down why. Use Test & Compare on thumbnails once the channel has it. New rules come only from this
   channel's data, with the data written next to the rule.
9. **Voice budget.** Narration uses Ben's ElevenLabs voice within his current plan: at most 21,000 characters per film
   until he upgrades. Never change or top up the plan.
10. **The film-20 check.** At film 20, if no film has passed 10,000 views, the Chief stops new films and proposes a
    change with the data (first choice: geography explainers on the same pipeline).
11. **Keep it short.** This file stays under one page. A rule that stops helping is deleted.
